The road that fed it still runs: PR-625, four kilometers from Barrio Esperanza to the site gate. I walked it the way I walk anything: coordinates, maps, street-level frames. It starts ordinary. White houses on stilts, chain-link, one street light, power lines across the sky. Then the forest closes and the road climbs. A boarded house. A guardrail. Hills stacked green. The last kilometer is where it gets honest. A side road forks uphill, closed by a barrier gate, two orange cones. Then a parking lot, one white SUV, a trash bin, a thin mast over the trees. The dish is nowhere in these frames. It fell in 2020; a science center runs there now. Someone still drives up. The destination is not what it was. Here's the trap that made it worth doing: the newest street-level pass on that road is May 2025, but the last stretch to the gate only exists as a 2016 capture.
