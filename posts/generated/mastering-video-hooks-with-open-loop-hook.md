# Mastering Video Hooks with Open Loop Hook

## Overview

Ever notice how viewers drop off in the first 30 seconds of your tech videos? You spent 20 hours editing a slick laptop review, but half your audience bounced before you even showed the benchmarks. The fix isn't faster cuts or flashier B-roll. It’s a psychological mechanism used by top creators: the Open Loop Hook . Human brains hate incomplete information. An "open loop" intentionally creates a knowledge gap—dropping a intriguing promise or mystery at the start that can only be closed by staying tuned. Instead of a passive opener: "Today we’re reviewing the new iPhone camera and looking at its features." You open a loop: "The new iPhone camera is incredible, but if you don't turn off one hidden default setting, your 4K footage will look like a blurry 2010 webcam. I’ll show you how to fix that in a minute, but first, let's look at the low-light test..." Boom. Loop opened. The viewer is locked in because their brain demands to know what that "webcam setting" is. They have to keep watching. How to execute the Open Loop in tech: Find the anomaly: Spot your video's most surprising benchmark, hidden feature, or fatal flaw. Tease early, delay payoff: Plant the question in the first 10 seconds, but push the answer a few minutes deep. Chain them: Close the first loop, then immediately open a new one ("That fixes the speed issue, but there’s a battery bug no one is talking about...").
