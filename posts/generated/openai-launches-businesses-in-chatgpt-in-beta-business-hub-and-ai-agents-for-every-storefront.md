# OpenAI Launches Businesses in ChatGPT in Beta — Business Hub and AI Agents for Every Storefront

## Overview

ChatGPT is starting to look a lot like a search engine — and OpenAI knows it. The company has launched Businesses in ChatGPT in beta, giving businesses a way to claim their profile, keep their product information current in a new Business Hub, and run AI agents that talk to customers on their behalf. For now, the Business Agents are open to eligible ecommerce and online-service businesses in the US. What businesses actually get The Business Hub is the control panel: claim your business, manage product info, and see aggregated insights about how people find you. From there, a Business Agent can hold conversations with customers — answering questions, recommending products, and pointing people toward a purchase. OpenAI's announcement , published on November 10, frames it as a way for businesses of every size to put a storefront inside the app where millions of people already ask questions. The company also says ads in ChatGPT will give businesses a new way to reach customers. Why this looks familiar TechCrunch put it bluntly, saying OpenAI is making ChatGPT "just a little bit more like Google" — a place where businesses manage a profile, show up in answers, and pay to be seen. Search has worked that way for years; the difference here is that the storefront is a conversation. Shoppers don't type a query and pick a link — they ask a question, and the agent answers as if it works for the shop. What it means for storefronts For a small business, the pitch is hard to ignore: a customer-facing agent that never sleeps, inside an app your customers already open every day. The open question is whether shoppers will actually buy through a chat — and whether businesses trust an AI to speak for them. OpenAI has been aiming its models at security work too, building AI models built for security , and the same logic applies here: an agent that talks to your customers has to be one you can trust with your brand. As AI moves into everyday products — even formal verification of crypto protocols is now AI's work — the storefront may be the next place it feels normal. Either way, ChatGPT's role is shifting. It's not just a place to ask questions any more — it's a place where businesses show up to answer them. Originally published on Rohan Tech Official .
