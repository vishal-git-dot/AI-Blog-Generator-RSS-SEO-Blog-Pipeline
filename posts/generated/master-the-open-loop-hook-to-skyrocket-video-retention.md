# Master the Open Loop Hook to Skyrocket Video Retention

## Overview

Stop losing viewers in the first 10 seconds. You spent days benchmarking that new M4 Mac Mini, but your retention graph still looks like a ski slope. The problem isn't your pacing—it’s your intro. You’re closing your hooks before the video even starts. Enter the Open Loop Hook . Grounded in the Zeigarnik Effect, Open Loops exploit a simple quirk in human psychology: our brains despise unfinished information. When you plant a compelling question, high-stakes claim, or wild scenario at the start of a video and intentionally delay the payoff, viewers are psychologically wired to stick around until that loop closes. Most tech creators give away the punchline instantly: "Today we're testing this $50 mechanical keyboard to see if it's better than a custom build." (Boring. Loop closed. Viewer leaves.) Instead, open the loop: "This $50 Amazon keyboard claims to beat my $600 custom build. But when I cracked open the case, I found a hidden flaw that could literally short out your motherboard—and I'll show you what happened when I plugged it in." Now the viewer’s brain is locked into two unresolved loops: Does the $50 board actually perform? What is the motherboard-destroying flaw? Here is your 3-step Open Loop formula for tech content: The Staggering Claim: Show the wild outcome or extreme setup immediately. The Dangling Mystery: Introduce a risk, bizarre detail, or unexpected twist coming up later in the video. The Delayed Resolution: Deliver your standard review or tutorial, but don't close the loop until right before your conclusion. Try it on your next GPU review, desk setup tour, or coding tutorial.
