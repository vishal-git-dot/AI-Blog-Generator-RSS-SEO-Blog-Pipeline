# Computing ANSI Z765-2021 Heated GLA, 16" OC Framing & 4x8 Drywall Takeoffs from a 2D Wall Graph in TypeScript

## Overview

If you sum the interior areas of every room in a 2D floor plan and call the result "square footage," your number will disagree with a US residential appraiser by 6% to 10% on almost every house. When we built the geometry kernel for PlanCaliber (a browser-based 2D/3D CAD and floor plan studio), we ran straight into three real-world measurement rules that naive room-area calculators get wrong: ANSI Z765-2021 Gross Living Area (GLA) is measured out-to-out along exterior finished surfaces (including interior wall thickness), while excluding garages, porches, decks, and enclosed unheated voids. 16" O.C. Wood Stud Framing is never just linearFeet / 1.333 : wall ends, corners, double top plates, single bottom plates, and king/jack/cripple framing around doors and windows typically add 30% to 45% more lumber. 4x8 Gypsum Drywall & Baseboard Takeoffs depend on wall topology: exterior walls receive drywall on one side and deduct exterior door widths once, while interior partitions receive drywall on two sides and deduct interior passage doors twice. Reconstructing Out-to-Out ANSI Z765-2021 Heated GLA from Centerline Polygons In a planar floor plan engine, walls are stored as centerline segments connecting vertices, and rooms are detected as minimal closed cycles of half-edges. Every exterior wall (or demising wall bordering an unheated garage) is touched by only one heated room cycle ( count === 1 ), meaning the outer thicknessIn / 2 strip lies outside the centerline loop.
