# Building a Semantic Model for Recycling Contamination Triage

## Overview

How I Put Commodity Measures Under a Local Multi-Agent Shift Desk TL;DR LD-106 looked acceptable if you only read the numbers. HDPE natural, 7,340 pounds, residue at 3.1 percent, moisture just over 1 percent. The spot-check note ruined that story: a battery pack sitting in the third gaylord. I built ResidueDesk-AI, a personal experiment, so a laptop at a fictional scale house would quarantine that load instead of waving it onto a tip floor because the residue percent was low. The full source is at https://github.com/aniket-work/ResidueDesk-AI . The PoC is a semantic model with a local coordinator on top. The model names three entities, Load, Commodity, and Supplier, and three measures on Load: NetWeightLb , ResiduePercent , and MoisturePercent . Commodity metric rows bind accept, resort, and moisture caps to each material. Five steps then read that projection: intake, residue scout, commodity grader, line router, and shift briefer. Nothing calls a model API. python main.py prints a table and a shift brief that lists reject and divert before the clean accepts. On the ten sample tickets the brief lands at 2 accept, 4 resort, 3 divert, 1 reject, average residue 8.36 percent. I kept the project small on purpose. Pydantic checks the ticket, the ticket layer, before any step runs. semantic.py lifts that row onto the semantic layer, and the agents only consume the projection. FastAPI serves the same function the CLI calls, plus a plain dashboard. From my experience, that split is what makes an agent demo debuggable. When LD-108 flips from accept to resort, the trace says moisture_cap and I can point at the mixed-paper metric row instead of guessing which prompt drifted. This write-up is my experiment, not a plant procedure. I have not bolted it to a live scale, and the percents are not a mill contract. I think the useful part is the layering: a ticket, a semantic model that names the measures, and a coordinator that is not allowed to invent a second definition of residue. Introduction I got annoyed at a pattern in agent demos I kept cloning. A coordinator, a handful of specialists, a daily brief, all of it running on a laptop with open-source pieces. The demos were usually about calendars, inboxes, or research summaries. Those are fine toys. They are a poor fit for the question I actually wanted to poke at, which is what happens when one bad sentence in a note should overrule a clean numeric field. Recycling contamination gave me that question without inventing a company story around it. A materials recovery facility lives on inbound loads. Each load shows up as a ticket: commodity, net weight, a residue percent from a spot check, a moisture reading, a supplier id, a timestamp, and a note written by whoever looked in the bale. That ticket is the bottom layer. I did not want the agents to be the place where "residue" got redefined in five slightly different ways. So I wrote a semantic model first, then pointed the agents at it. The battery example is the one I started with. Residue at 3.1 percent is inside the accept band I later set for HDPE. If the grader ran alone, LD-106 would go to tip_floor_primary . That felt wrong the moment I typed the note. Batteries, propane, aerosols, lithium packs, and syringes are the terms I put on a prohibited list. The scout finds them. The router stops there. I put it this way because I have watched too many pipelines average a text field into a score and then lose the one token that mattered. Moisture was the second itch. OCC at 3.2 percent residue and 8 percent moisture (LD-101) should accept. Mixed paper at 6.2 percent residue looks acceptable against the 8 percent accept cap I chose, until moisture is 19.4 percent (LD-108). Wet fiber blinds the story I was trying to model, so I worsen the call by one step. Accept becomes resort. I did not worsen divert into some fifth bucket. There is no bucket past the bunker in this PoC. I also wanted the output to look like a shift handoff, not a chat transcript. The briefer sorts reject, then divert, then resort, then accept. A person scanning a laptop at 6 a.m. should see LD-106 before they see the pretty PET load. In my opinion that ordering is the product. The agents are just how I kept the reasons separate enough to test. The tip floor still wants a call. Agents are not supposed to invent fields at this layer. Semantic layer. semantic.py . Entities are Load, Commodity, and Supplier. Decision layer. Intake, scout, grader, router, briefer. The grader bands ResiduePercent using the metric row for that commodity. The router may override the band. The ticket An inbound load has eight fields. load_id and supplier_id are strings. commodity is an enum: OCC, mixed paper, PET, HDPE, glass, LDPE film. net_weight_lb is an int capped at 80,000 because I wanted a ridiculous ticket to fail validation. residue_percent and moisture_percent are floats from 0 to 100. spot_check_notes is free text. received_at is a string timestamp. I did not parse it into a datetime. For this experiment the scout does not care about the hour, and I did not want timezone bugs in a PoC about residue. Intake Intake looks for three flags: weight under 400 pounds, blank supplier, blank timestamp. The 400 pound line is mine. I still send it to the resort bay if it would otherwise accept, because a partial bale in my sample set was usually a broken ticket, not a heroic small load. Residue scout The scout lowercases the note and looks for battery , propane , syringe , aerosol , and lithium . Any hit is a list of terms. I did not stem words. "batteries" would slip through, and I left that hole on purpose so I would remember how dumb keyword lists are. The sample note says "battery pack," so the test passes. A future note that says "batteries" would not, and I would rather see that miss in a test than pretend the list is complete. Commodity grader The grader only answers "which band." It does not pick the line. That was a deliberate split after I tangled them. Residue at or below accept_max is in_grade . Residue above that and at or below resort_max is out_of_grade . Residue above resort_max is heavy_residue . Moisture is a boolean, and only for fiber. OCC and mixed paper compare moisture_percent to moisture_cap . PET can be wet in the ticket and the grader will say so in the trace via the moisture field, but it will not set moisture_breach . I did that because my fiber story was about soggy bales, and I did not want a 2 percent moisture PET load to wobble. The caps I settled on, after the boring first run: OCC accepts through 5 percent residue, resorts through 12, moisture cap 18. Mixed paper accepts through 8, resorts through 18, moisture cap 16. PET and HDPE accept through 4, resort through 10, moisture cap 8, and that cap is unused for the breach flag. Glass accepts through 6, resorts through 15. LDPE film accepts through 10, resorts through 25. Film in my samples always carried loose paper, so a tight cap made every film ticket divert and I learned nothing. Each commodity points at a card id. The card text is a short paragraph in SPEC_CARDS . The coordinator copies the first 72 characters into the trace. That truncation is a little crude. It is enough to see SPEC-OCC-01 and the start of the sentence when I am scrolling a JSON dump. Line router The router is the only step allowed to return a disposition. Order of checks matters, and I wrote it in that order so I would stop second-guessing it at midnight. Prohibited terms return reject immediately. Moisture and residue do not get a vote. If the note contains ceramic or stoneware , return divert. LD-107 is glass at 4.4 percent residue, which is in grade, and it still diverts because the note mentions a ceramic mug. Otherwise map the band: in grade to accept, out of grade to resort, heavy residue to divert. If fiber moisture breached the cap, worsen one step. Accept becomes resort. Resort becomes divert. I wrote it that way after an early version returned "reject" as a string and the router had to understand two dialects. A list of terms is dumber and easier. The trace line includes residue and moisture even though the grader also prints them. When I tail a trace I want the scout line to stand alone. hits or ['none'] keeps the log from saying prohibited=[] , which I misread once as a real hit on an empty string. Small thing. It saved me a confused minute, so it stayed. Substring matching is the limitation I already mentioned. It will also false-positive if I ever write a note like "no battery found" because battery is still in the sentence. None of the sample notes do that. If I extend the PoC I would switch this to a word-boundary check and add a negation test. I am not going to claim the current list is careful. It is a tripwire for the sample set.
