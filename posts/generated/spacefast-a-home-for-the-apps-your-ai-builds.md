# Spacefast: A Home for the Apps Your AI Builds

## Overview

Have you ever asked an AI to build you something, watched it actually work, and then thought... now what? That is the strange gap in vibe coding right now. You can describe an app in plain English and get working code back in minutes. But a pile of code on your laptop is not a website. Somebody still has to put it online, give it a database, set up logins, and keep it running. That last mile is the boring part, and it is the part most of us get stuck on. On November 9, Automattic, the company behind WordPress.com, launched a product aimed squarely at that gap. It is called Spacefast, and it is not another AI builder. It is the place your AI-built app goes to live. What Spacefast actually is Think of it like this: if vibe coding is cooking a meal from a recipe you described out loud, Spacefast is the restaurant that serves it. You bring the dish, it provides the tables, the kitchen, and the address. Spacefast gives a vibe-coded project the things it needs to be a real website: hosting, a database, file uploads, email, analytics, and custom domains. You tell your AI assistant to publish, and you get back a working link. That is the whole idea. The product started as a hackathon side project called Stattic, led by Batuhan İçöz, and soft-launched in September before going public on November 9. Bring your own AI Here is the part I find most interesting. Spacefast writes no code and has no chat box of its own. Instead, it works with the AI assistant you already use: ChatGPT, Claude, Cursor, Codex, Grok Bot, OpenClaw, and more. You can also just connect a Git repository. That is a real difference from Lovable and Emergent, its best-known competitors. Those give you the builder and the hosting in one package, which is convenient, but you live inside their world. Spacefast is betting that people would rather keep their favorite AI and just rent the plumbing. It is a bit like the difference between a furnished apartment and an empty one. The furnished place is ready on day one, but everything in it belongs to the landlord. The empty one takes a little more work, but you choose the furniture. The boring half matters more than we admit Developers love to talk about building. We talk a lot less about deploying, and that is where most side projects quietly die. The demo works on your machine, the link never gets shared, and the project slowly fades into a folder you stop opening. Spacefast is trying to shrink that gap to almost nothing. Automattic says you can publish without even creating an account for your first project, and the free Personal plan includes a small monthly usage allowance, custom domains, and a built-in database. Paid plans start at $5 a month for more usage and unlimited projects. Photo by Nubelson Fernandes on Unsplash There is a catch worth knowing. Built-in app authentication is still listed as coming soon, so for now the gate on a private project is a password or a share link. And Spacefast charges for infrastructure, not AI credits, which is fair, but it means the free allowance is small by design. So what does it mean? I think the bigger story here is not Spacefast itself. It is what it says about where the web is heading. A few years ago, making a website meant picking a platform and learning its rules. Then AI showed up and made the building part easy for everyone. Now the companies that used to own the building part, like Automattic, are racing to own the part that comes after: the hosting, the database, the boring infrastructure that makes something real. The question is no longer "can AI build this?" It is "where does it live once it is built?" Spacefast is one answer. There will be more. So here is my question for you: if you could describe any small app into existence this weekend, what would you build first, and would you care where it lives, as long as the link works?
