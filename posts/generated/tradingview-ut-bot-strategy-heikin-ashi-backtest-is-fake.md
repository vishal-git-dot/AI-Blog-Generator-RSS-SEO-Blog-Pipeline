# TradingView UT Bot Strategy: Heikin Ashi Backtest Is Fake

## Overview

The short version. This TradingView UT Bot strategy backtest starts with the Heikin Ashi trap: on an HA chart the strategy looks like a money machine, and at real prices it isn't. It also covers the settings and repainting. The script is UT Bot Strategy by QuantNomad, one of the most-boosted open-source strategies on TradingView. I ported it line for line to Python and ran it on Exness tick data with real spread and commission: five forex and gold markets from 2020 to 2026, Bitcoin from 2024, and gold back to 2004. The Heikin Ashi chart backtest is fake. Put the strategy on a Heikin Ashi chart and it shows PF 8.6 to 11.5 with 65–69% winners on every market and timeframe I tried (gold, EURUSD, Bitcoin, M5 to H4). Fill the same signals at real prices and it's PF 0.90 to 1.19 with 35–38% winners. The table and the equity chart below show both side by side. Forex lost money on every timeframe. EURUSD, GBPUSD, NZDUSD and USDJPY: profit factor 0.52 to 1.00 from M5 to D1 after costs. Gold H4 is the only real positive. PF 1.17 on 1,219 trades, +2,245 USD per 0.01 lot. But 2020 to 2022 lost money, and the ten best trades made more than the whole result. Gold 2004–2019, data I didn't use to pick H4: PF 1.00. Sixteen years of break-even. M5 and M15 lose everywhere. About 35–40 trades a day, and the spread eats all of it. My verdict: UT Bot is a plain ATR trailing stop that flips long and short. It doesn't have an edge on its own. If you've seen a UT Bot backtest with a 70–90% win rate, check the chart type first. What is the UT Bot strategy on TradingView? It's a trailing stop that sits one ATR away from price and flips the trade when a candle closes on the other side of it. The UT Bot Strategy page is QuantNomad's strategy version of the "UT Bot" indicator by Yo_adriiiiaan, which credits HPotter for the idea. It's dated 15 November 2019, Pine v4, and had 9,114 boosts when I checked in November 2026. The "UT Bot Strategy with Backtesting Range" by the same author has identical rules plus a date filter, so this review covers both. Three inputs: Key Value (default 1), ATR Period (default 10) and a checkbox Signals from Heikin Ashi Candles (off). Here's the core of the Pine code: xATR = atr(c) nLoss = a * xATR xATRTrailingStop := iff(src > nz(xATRTrailingStop[1], 0) and src[1] > nz(xATRTrailingStop[1], 0), max(nz(xATRTrailingStop[1]), src - nLoss), iff(src < nz(xATRTrailingStop[1], 0) and src[1] < nz(xATRTrailingStop[1], 0), min(nz(xATRTrailingStop[1]), src + nLoss), iff(src > nz(xATRTrailingStop[1], 0), src - nLoss, src + nLoss))) ... buy = src > xATRTrailingStop and crossover(ema(src, 1), xATRTrailingStop) sell = src < xATRTrailingStop and crossover(xATRTrailingStop, ema(src, 1)) strategy.entry("long", true, when = buy) strategy.entry("short", false, when = sell) In plain words: while price stays above the line, the line moves up to close − Key × ATR and never moves down. When a close drops below it, the line jumps above price and starts following it down. Each crossing is a buy or a sell. ema(src, 1) is just the close itself. There's no stop loss or take profit. The line is the stop, but it's only checked at the candle close, and the strategy is always in the market, long or short. Here's what that looks like on gold H4 with the defaults: Gold went from about 3,350 to 4,350 in those eight weeks. UT Bot still flipped short 15 times, because a stop one ATR away gets hit by any normal pullback. The longs made +557 USD per 0.01 lot, the shorts lost 387, so it kept +171 of a 1,000-dollar move. In a range it's worse: Twelve trades in three weeks, net −234. That's the whole pattern of this strategy in two pictures. Should you use UT Bot on Heikin Ashi candles? Use the checkbox if you like, but don't put the strategy on a Heikin Ashi chart. The author says the same thing on the script page: "Do not add this strategy to HA candles, use usual candles and this checkbox." This is where most of the amazing UT Bot backtests on YouTube come from, so it's worth showing why. Heikin Ashi candles aren't real prices. The HA open is the average of the previous HA open and close, so it lags the market. When the strategy runs on an HA chart, TradingView fills the orders at those HA prices unless you change the fill setting in the strategy properties. So the backtest buys at a price that's usually below where the market actually was, and sells above it. The strategy decides at the bar close and fills at the next bar's open. The Heikin Ashi option uses security() on the same timeframe with lookahead = false , so it doesn't look ahead on historical bars either. The line on the live, unfinished candle can move while the candle is open, which is why the signal sometimes appears and disappears in real time. The strategy only acts once the bar closes. The thing that misleads people isn't repainting. It's the HA chart fills above. What does the author claim? Nothing about performance.
