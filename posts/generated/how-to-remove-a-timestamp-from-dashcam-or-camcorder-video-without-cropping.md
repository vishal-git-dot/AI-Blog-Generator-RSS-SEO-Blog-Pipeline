# How to Remove a Timestamp From Dashcam or Camcorder Video (Without Cropping)

## Overview

Dashcams, camcorders, baby monitors and action cameras love to burn the date and time into the corner of every frame. That overlay is handy when you need a record. It is less handy when you just want to share a beautiful mountain-road clip, a family birthday on an old camcorder tape, or a funny moment your car camera caught. This guide explains how to remove a timestamp from a video you recorded yourself: first by stopping it at the source, then by cleaning short clips you already have with Clearframe's video watermark remover . Use these steps only on footage you own or have permission to edit. Why the timestamp is burned into your video Most cameras record the time in two places: Metadata. The file itself stores a creation date. You can't see it in the picture, and renaming or re-exporting the file can change or strip it. The overlay (the "OSD"). The camera draws the date and time directly onto the image while it records. Once it is in the pixels, there is no layer to switch off later. Deleting metadata does nothing to the visible numbers. To get rid of the overlay you either crop it out, cover it, or rebuild the pixels behind it. The last option is what an inpainting tool does. This is the video version of a problem we already covered for photos. If your burned-in date is on a still image, see how to remove a date stamp from a photo . Step 1: Turn the timestamp off for future recordings The cleanest fix is not needing one. Before you edit anything, check your camera's settings so new clips come out clean. Dashcams. Look in the recording or system menu for Date Stamp , Time Stamp , Watermark , Date/Time Display or Stamp . Many brands also burn in speed, GPS coordinates or a logo; these usually have separate toggles. Phone companion apps often show the same option under camera settings. Camcorders. Consumer camcorders usually have a Data Code or Date/Time Display setting. On many tape-era models the date was only shown on playback and could be hidden; on others it was recorded into the picture and can't be removed from the tape. Security and baby cameras. The overlay is usually in the app's video or OSD settings. Keep in mind that for home security footage the timestamp is often the most useful thing in the frame. A reasonable compromise for dashcams: leave the timestamp on (it's valuable if anything happens), and only clean copies of the scenic or funny clips you want to post. Step 2: Trim the moment you want to share Clearframe's video tool is built for short clips up to 30 seconds in MP4, MOV or WEBM . That fits how most people actually share dashcam and camcorder footage: a 10-second near-miss, a sunset over the highway, a toddler's first steps. Trim before you upload: On iPhone, open the clip in Photos, tap Edit , and drag the yellow handles. On Android, most gallery apps have a Trim or Edit button. On desktop, QuickTime ( Edit > Trim ), the Windows Photos/Clipchamp editor or any free editor will do. Export at the highest quality your editor offers. Re-compressing a low-bitrate dashcam file twice makes every later step look worse. Step 3: Remove the timestamp with Clearframe Because a dashcam or camcorder timestamp sits in the same place in every frame , it is a textbook case for fixed-region cleanup. Include the full width of the longest time string, since "12:59:59" is wider than "1:01:01", and include any outline or shadow around the digits. Tips for cleaner results Box generously, but not wildly. A small margin around the digits removes the faint halo that compression leaves around text. A huge box forces the AI to invent more of the scene than it needs to. Corners over sky and road clean best. Sky, asphalt, dashboards and grass are easy to rebuild. A timestamp that sits over passing traffic, signs or faces is harder, and you may see slight shimmer. Night footage is harder. Dashcam night clips are noisy, and noise is hard to fake convincingly. Expect good but not perfect results. Check the first and last second. Scrub to the start and end of the clip; that's where artifacts tend to show first. If your overlay moves around the frame (some app-added stickers do), it is not a fixed region and this workflow is not the right fit. Timestamps from cameras almost never move, which is why they work well here. Alternatives, honestly compared Crop the corner Good for: timestamps that sit on a strip of dashboard or sky you don't care about. Downside: you lose part of the frame and usually have to scale up, which softens the video. Vertical crops for social can sometimes hide the timestamp for free, so check that first. Blur or cover it Good for: quick posts where nobody minds a blurred corner or a sticker. Downside: a blurred rectangle is still a distraction, and it looks like you are hiding something. Desktop editors Good for: long footage, moving overlays, or frame-by-frame control. Tools like DaVinci Resolve or After Effects can track and patch regions over minutes of video. Downside: time, learning curve and, for some tools, cost. For a 15-second clip, that's usually more work than the problem deserves.
