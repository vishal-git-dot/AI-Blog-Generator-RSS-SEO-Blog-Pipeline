# TrustIntern: I built a scam detective for internship offers in 5.5 hours, and my agents kept receipts

## Overview

This is a submission for the Hacktoberfest Open-Source AI Challenge Week 2: Side Hustle What I Built Every placement season, a lot of students in India get some version of this message. (This one is a composite I wrote; the pattern is the real part.) "Congratulations! You have been selected for our internship. Please pay a ₹1,999 registration fee to confirm your seat. Share your Aadhaar and the OTP you receive for verification. Offer valid for 24 hours only." It comes from a Gmail address, for a company whose website is a month old. And it works, because a student chasing their first résumé line is the person least likely to stop and check. TrustIntern is an investigation desk for internship offers. You paste in the company name, the recruiter's email, the website and the message, and you can attach the offer letter as a PDF or a screenshot. TrustIntern then: runs fixed checks (email domain vs. company, domain age via RDAP, the website itself, the wording of the message), sends a small team of research agents to look into the company, the recruiter and public complaints, with a Gemma 4 planner deciding which agents to launch, has Gemma 4 vision read the offer letter or screenshot, and returns a risk score you can recompute by hand . Every point is tied to a specific piece of evidence. Every report says: "Heuristic indicator score, not a probability of fraud." It shows evidence and calls nobody a scammer. The side hustle Who would pay for this? Not students, and that's on purpose. Students: free. The people being targeted shouldn't have to pay for protection. With local Gemma it costs almost nothing to run. Colleges and placement cells: paid. Bulk verification of the offers circulating in student WhatsApp groups, with a shareable report per offer. Job boards and internship platforms: paid API. Screen listings and recruiter accounts before they go live. Postmortem report: a one-off paid report for someone who has already been scammed. It's an evidence-backed write-up of what happened, which they can attach to a complaint to their college, the platform, or the cybercrime portal. That last idea didn't come from me. It came from my first tester (more on that below). Gemma 4 does the thinking, rules do the math Gemma 4 has two jobs: Planner. It reads what the fixed checkers found and decides which research agents to launch next. If a recruiter's name turns up, a recruiter agent goes after it. If a strange domain turns up, a domain agent does. The planner can only pick from an allowlist: in registry.py , "Unknown types from the planner are never executed." Multimodal reader. Scam offers rarely arrive as tidy PDFs. They arrive as WhatsApp screenshots, so Gemma 4 vision reads either and pulls out claims to compare against everything else. Locally, I run gemma4:e2b through Ollama on my own laptop, and it was fast. The hosted demo can't reach my laptop, so it falls back to Gemma 4 served through Google's Gemini API ( gemma-4-26b-a4b-it ). Same open-weight family. If no model is reachable at all, a heuristic planner takes over and the investigation still finishes. The one rule I never let bend, written into CONTRACT.md under "Scoring boundary (non-negotiable)" : Gemma / OSINT never invent indicator points. The docstring on gemma_client.py says it in four words: "Never scores." Gemma interprets evidence; scoring.py sums fixed points. Run the Suspicious sample with live lookups off and you get 90 every time, pinned by tests.
