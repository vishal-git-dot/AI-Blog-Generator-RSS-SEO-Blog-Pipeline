Google ships Gemini 4 Argon, gated on purpose Google formally announced Gemini 4 Argon, a frontier model built to hold deep reasoning across long, complex workflows: real-world software engineering, enterprise knowledge work in law and finance, and cyber defense. The rollout is deliberately staged. Argon first goes to more than 650 members of the Fairwind program, the trusted-access cohort of governments, national cyber agencies, critical infrastructure operators and core technology platforms that Google started on September 2. Google also opened the model to US government reviewers before launch under a voluntary evaluation arrangement, and the model began reaching Ultra subscribers on Tuesday. The spec sheet backs the long-horizon pitch. Output limits jumped from 64,000 to 1 million tokens, which Google says lets the model read an entire codebase in one task. Introductory pricing is $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output, with cached input 95% cheaper, moving to $4 and $20 after the promo window. On Google's own benchmarks, Argon scored 77.9% on DeepSWE v1.1, 51.3% for first place on Zapier's AutomationBench, 91.7% on LVBench, and 68% on CWE-bench v1, tied with GPT-6 Astra. Internally, Argon produced a quantum algorithm optimization about 40% better than published literature in minutes, its data center agents freed over 300 TiB of memory with a projected 500 TiB to 1 PiB total, and the model has been migrating Google's C/C++ codebases to Rust, from libraries like re2 up to more than 800,000 lines of the Fuchsia Zircon kernel. Two caveats deserve equal billing. Every number above is vendor-run, and since Argon is locked inside Fairwind, outside developers cannot independently reproduce any of it. Argon also replaces Gemini 3.5 Pro, the model Google announced early this year and then cancelled, so the naming reset is a quiet admission that the previous plan did not survive contact with reality. The team assembled the largest deception dataset to date to train probes on model internals and introduced a probe architecture that aggregates information across many layers and tokens. On the SHADE-Arena benchmark, the probes reach 98.8% AUC, beating a text-monitoring baseline built on Opus 5.5, and performance improves as the underlying model gets larger. The paper's sharper results come from what the authors call introspective deception. The probes can distinguish a model's real hidden goals from what it writes in its own transcripts, at up to 99.7% AUC, and they catch open-weight models lying about politically sensitive topics or concealing their actual beliefs under pressure, cases where reading the output alone would pass as honest. The dataset, called FIBS, is open-sourced so other teams can extend it. The context is regulatory: Anthropic signed the EU AI Act's Code of Practice on Transparency of AI-Generated Content in July along with about 190 other signatories, which requires marking AI-generated text from models released after August 2. The system is built on Google DeepMind's SynthID-Text and works by guiding the model's low-stakes word choices, overcast versus grey, into a hidden pattern that a detection key can read. The company walked through the limits as carefully as the mechanics. The watermark adds no tokens, does not change quality, speed or pricing, and carries no information about the user. It applies globally because Anthropic has no durable way to scope it by region yet, and older models will be retrofitted over the coming months. Generated files such as PNG, JPG and SVG get C2PA content credentials in their metadata instead. Short texts and code carry weaker signals, since factual writing offers few free word choices, though code comments still do. Light editing probably will not remove the mark; rewriting every word probably will, which raises the question of whether fully rewritten text is still AI-generated at all. Reception has been rough at the edges. Dozens of users posted subscription cancellations, some calling the move a conspiracy, while others noted it only inconveniences dishonest use. Anthropic plans a detection API, in private preview for regulators, law enforcement, media, fact-checkers, researchers and EU civil society groups, with enterprise access for compliance obligations. Since every major signatory of the same code will ship its own marking scheme, watermarking output is on its way to being a default property of AI text, not a feature. — Anthropic · NDTV Profit 🔗 Anthropic · NDTV Profit KD Agentic · AI Daily Digest
