# An MVP is not version 1 with fewer features

## Overview

An MVP is not "version 1 with fewer features". It is the smallest thing that answers one question: will anyone use this? Most teams build the opposite. They start with the billing system, an admin panel, three integrations and a design system — and only then ask whether the core idea works. By that point the answer costs six months. What is inside, and what is not Inside: One core scenario, end to end; The minimum data storage that scenario needs; Something a real user can touch and use this week. Outside (for now): Roles and permissions; Admin panels unless the scenario requires one; Integrations beyond the single most important one; Polish that nobody will notice until people use the product. The test is simple: if a feature can be postponed without changing whether you learn the answer, it is not in the MVP. What an MVP is often confused with Prototype — mockups and clickable screens; its job is to agree on logic and design. Days, not weeks. Pilot — a rollout to real users at limited scale; its job is to test the process and the load. Mock demo — a clickable fake used to sell an idea. Useful, but it answers nothing about behaviour. They usually follow each other: agree first, then test the hypothesis, then scale. How to know it worked One metric, decided before launch. Not "people liked it" but: Did users complete the core scenario without help? Did they come back a second time? Did the manual work it replaced actually decrease? If you cannot name the metric before the build starts, the MVP will be judged on vibes — and vibes always say "we need more features". The most common mistake Building the MVP for the wrong user: the one who pays, instead of the one who uses. The person who approves the budget is rarely the person who will press the buttons every day. Watch the second one. Two weeks, not two months A focused MVP fits in 2–6 weeks, and a landing or bot in days. The rule of thumb: if the estimate is longer, the scope is not an MVP — it is a full product with an MVP label on it. Cross-post. Full version: What an MVP is, in plain words .
