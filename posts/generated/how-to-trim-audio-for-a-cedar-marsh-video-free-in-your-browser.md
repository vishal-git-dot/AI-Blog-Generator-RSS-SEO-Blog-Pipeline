# How to Trim Audio for a Cedar Marsh Video — Free, in Your Browser

## Overview

A Cedar Marsh birdwatching video lives or dies by its audio. The footage is calm water and reeds, but the recording you bring home has wind gusts, footsteps on the boardwalk, and long silent gaps between calls. Trim it before you edit, and the video feels twice as good. You can trim it in the browser with AudioCutter — free, no install, and your file never leaves your device. Step 1: Open AudioCutter and load your recording Drag the audio file onto the page. MP3, WAV, M4A and most common formats work, and the waveform shows up in seconds so you can see where the calls actually are. Step 2: Keep only the good calls Drag the start and end handles around the section worth keeping — a clear bird call, your spoken note, the quiet water ambience. Play it back once to check you did not clip a call in half. Step 3: Fade the edges Add a short fade-in and fade-out so the clip starts and ends smoothly instead of cutting off mid-sound. Half a second is usually enough. Step 4: Export and use it in your video Export as MP3 or WAV, then drop the trimmed clip under your marsh footage in your video editor. The whole job takes about a minute. Disclosure: I built AudioCutter ( https://audiocutter.me/ ), a free browser-based audio trimmer. Files are processed on your own device and are never uploaded to a server.
