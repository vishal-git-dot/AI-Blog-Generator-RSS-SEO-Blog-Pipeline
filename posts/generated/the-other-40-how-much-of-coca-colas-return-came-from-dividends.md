# The Other 40%: How Much of Coca-Cola's Return Came from Dividends

Dividends turned out to be a small part of Apple's story. Over almost eight years they added 47 percentage points to a return of 817%. At the end I promised to run the same recipe on a company where the dividend is the main story. Coca-Cola is the obvious choice. It has raised its dividend every year for decades, and the payout is several times larger than Apple's, relative to the price. So the question for this part is simple: over ten years, how much of a Coca-Cola investor's return came from the price, and how much from the dividends? Want to try this yourself? Why is the share so different? For Apple, from January 2019 to October 2026, the price alone rose 770.0% and the total return was 817.4%. Dividends added 47.5 percentage points, about 6% of the total. For Coca-Cola, over ten years, the price rose 117.2% and the total return was 196.2%. Dividends added 79 points, 40% of the total. Two things decide the share. The first is the dividend yield: Apple paid out around one percent of its price a year or less, Coca-Cola about three percent. The second is how fast the price grew. Apple's price grew so fast that even a growing dividend stayed a small part of the story. Coca-Cola's price grew slowly and steadily, so the dividends had room to matter. The two periods aren't the same length, so this isn't a race between the two stocks. It's a lesson about charts. For Apple, a price chart tells almost the whole story. For Coca-Cola, a price chart leaves out 40% of what an investor actually got. Compare a growth stock with a dividend stock on price charts alone, and the dividend stock looks worse than it was. The fair comparison is total return against total return, which is exactly what the adjusted close gives you. A word of honesty Taxes come first. The 196% assumes every dollar of dividend was reinvested. A real investor outside the US loses part of each dividend to US withholding tax before it arrives: for a Czech investor that is usually 15% with the right form filed, 30% without it, and there may be more to pay at home. With 15% withheld, the total return over these ten years drops by about 13 percentage points; with 30%, by about 26. The price return doesn't change. Taxes hit exactly the part this article is about. I picked Coca-Cola because it is famous for its dividend. That makes it a good example, not a typical stock. Many companies pay no dividend at all, and some cut theirs when times are hard. The window matters too. Coca-Cola's price rose sharply in 2026. Measured to the first trading day of 2026 instead, the price was up 64%, the total return 118%, and dividends made up 46% of it, not 40%. Move the start or the end, and the share moves with it. And the rebuild assumes reinvestment at the close before each ex-dividend date, with no fees and fractional shares allowed. Other reasonable conventions give slightly different totals, by less than a percentage point here. Not investment advice.
