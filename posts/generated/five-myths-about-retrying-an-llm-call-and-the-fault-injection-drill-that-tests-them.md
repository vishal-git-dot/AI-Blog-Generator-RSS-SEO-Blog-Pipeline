# Five Myths About Retrying an LLM Call, and the Fault-Injection Drill That Tests Them

## Overview

Your retry loop is maybe eight lines. Why does it double your bill and duplicate side effects? I keep seeing the same pattern in reviews. The retry logic looks correct, and everyone stops reading there. That is the problem. Retries are where cost, correctness, and latency quietly disagree. Below are five claims I hear often, the evidence that breaks them, and a small drill you can run in an afternoon. Label: the code here is a template, not a benchmark. I have not run it against your stack. Run the drill yourself and trust your own numbers, not mine. Myth 1: "A retry is basically free" A retry is not a repeat. It is a second request with the same input. You pay to send the prompt again, on every attempt. Long system prompts make this expensive fast. Corrected model: retry cost scales with input_tokens × attempts × concurrent_callers . Measure attempts, not errors. Ask yourself: does your dashboard count attempts, or only final outcomes? Most count outcomes. Myth 2: "HTTP 200 means the model finished" A 200 means the transport succeeded. It says nothing about the answer. Truncated completions, empty content, and stream cuts all arrive as 200. Your retry loop never fires. Corrected model: treat finish_reason , content length, and schema validity as part of the success check. A response is not a result. How many of your retries are actually triggered by business-level incompleteness? Usually zero. Myth 3: "Exponential backoff with jitter is enough" Backoff controls timing. It does not control volume. If every caller retries four times, a partial outage becomes a full one. Jitter spreads the pain, not the load. Corrected model: add a retry budget per request and per minute. Cap total attempts across the fleet. Rules that survive contact with production: Retry 429 and 5xx only. Never retry 400 or 401 . Honor Retry-After when present. It beats your formula. Stop after a fixed attempt count. Then fail over or fail closed. Emit one metric per attempt, tagged by error class. Myth 4: "Retrying is idempotent because the call is stateless" The model call is stateless. Your handler is not. If the tool call wrote a ticket, charged a card, or sent mail, a retry repeats the side effect. Corrected model: retries are only safe behind an idempotency key or a commit barrier. The barrier is a local check: did the side effect already happen? Rhetorical but useful: what would your retry loop do to a payment webhook twice in a row? Myth 5: "You can resume a stream where it stopped" You cannot resume mid-token. The connection is gone, and so is the partial state. What you can do is checkpoint at message boundaries. Re-request with the completed prefix as context. Corrected model: streams are not resumable transports. They are progress signals. Persist what you received, then decide.
