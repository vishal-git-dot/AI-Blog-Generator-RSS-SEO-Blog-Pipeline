# How to get clients to confirm dog grooming appointments

## Overview

How to Get Clients to Confirm Dog Grooming Appointments (Without Chasing Them) No-shows and last-minute cancellations are one of the most frustrating parts of running a grooming business. You block out a 90-minute slot, prep the tub, turn down another booking — and then the client simply doesn't show up. The good news: most no-shows aren't malicious. They're the result of unclear expectations, no reminder system, or a booking process that makes confirming feel optional. This guide walks you through practical steps to get clients to confirm dog grooming appointments consistently — with scripts, timing, and a confirmation checklist you can implement this week. Why Clients Don't Confirm (It's Usually Not What You Think) Before fixing the problem, understand the root causes: No reminder system. If you only confirm at booking, the appointment is out of sight and out of mind for weeks. Confirmation feels like extra work. If clients have to call during business hours, many won't bother. Unclear consequences. If there's no stated policy, confirming feels optional. Wrong channel. A client who books by text may never check email — and vice versa. No-shows were never penalized. Clients learn your real rules from your behavior, not your policy page. The fix is a system, not a single message. Here's how to build it. Step 1: Set the Expectation at Booking Confirmation starts the moment the appointment is made. Train clients from the first interaction. What to say at booking (in person or by phone): > "I've got you down for Bella on Thursday, March 14 at 10 AM. You'll get a text reminder 48 hours before — just reply YES to confirm. If I don't hear back within 24 hours, I'll reach out once more, and after that the slot may be released to the waitlist." That single script does four things: Tells them when the reminder is coming. Tells them how to confirm (reply YES — low effort). Tells them what happens if they don't. Makes confirmation feel normal, not pushy. If you use paper or digital booking forms, add a line at the bottom: "I understand I'll receive a confirmation request 48 hours before my appointment and agree to respond within 24 hours." A simple checkbox dramatically improves response rates. Step 2: Use a 48-Hour / 24-Hour Reminder Sequence One reminder isn't enough. Two is the sweet spot for most grooming salons. 48-hour reminder (text or email): > "Hi [Name]! Reminder: Bella's grooming appointment is Thursday, March 14 at 10 AM. Reply YES to confirm, or C to cancel/reschedule. Thanks! — [Salon Name]" 24-hour follow-up (only if no response): > "Hi [Name], just checking in — I haven't heard back about Bella's appointment tomorrow at 10 AM. Please reply YES to keep the slot, or let me know if you need to reschedule. If I don't hear back by 5 PM today, I'll need to release the time to another client." This two-touch system works because it's: Short (one screen, no scrolling). Actionable (reply YES — no app, no login). Time-bound (a clear deadline). Step 3: Make Confirming Ridiculously Easy Every extra step reduces response rates. Remove friction wherever possible. Do: Accept confirmation by text, email reply, or one-tap link. Use a booking form that includes a "Confirm appointment" button. Let clients confirm by replying with a single word. Don't: Require a phone call during business hours. Ask clients to log into a portal for a 5-second task. Send a message that requires them to open an attachment. If you're still using paper appointment books, a simple text reminder from your phone is enough to start. You don't need expensive software to fix this. Step 4: State Your No-Show Policy Clearly — and Enforce It A confirmation system only works if there's a real consequence for ignoring it. You don't need to be harsh — just consistent. A fair, client-friendly policy: First no-show: friendly call, no charge, note in client file. Second no-show: 50% of service fee charged before rebooking. Third no-show: prepayment required for all future appointments. Put this policy: On your booking form. In your confirmation reminder (one short line). On your website's FAQ page. The key is enforcement . If you waive the policy every time, clients learn that confirming doesn't matter. If you apply it calmly and consistently, word spreads — and no-shows drop. Step 5: Follow Up on Unconfirmed Appointments the Same Day Don't wait until the morning of the appointment to notice a client never confirmed. Build a daily habit: End-of-day checklist: [ ] Review tomorrow's appointments. [ ] Flag any that haven't been confirmed. [ ] Send one final "last call" message before closing. [ ] Release unconfirmed slots to your waitlist. This turns a potential empty slot into a filled one — often within an hour, if you keep a waitlist. Step 6: Reward Reliable Clients (Quietly) You don't need a formal loyalty program. Small gestures go a long way: Priority booking for clients who always confirm. A free nail trim after 10 confirmed appointments. A simple "thank you for confirming!" in your reply. This reinforces the behavior you want without penalizing anyone. What to Say: Copy-and-Paste Confirmation Scripts For first-time clients: > "Hi [Name]! Looking forward to seeing [Pet] on [Date] at [Time]. You'll get a reminder 48 hours before — just reply YES to confirm. Any questions before then, just text me here." For repeat clients who often forget: > "Hi [Name]! Quick reminder about [Pet]'s appointment on [Date] at [Time]. Reply YES to confirm or C to reschedule. See you soon!" For unconfirmed appointments (24-hour mark): > "Hi [Name], I haven't heard back about [Pet]'s appointment tomorrow at [Time]. Please confirm by [deadline] so I can hold the slot. Thanks!" For a no-show follow-up: > "Hi [Name], we missed you and [Pet] today at [Time]. Let me know if you'd like to rebook — I'll hold a slot for you this week. Just a heads-up: our policy notes that a second missed appointment requires a 50% deposit to rebook." The Real Fix: A System, Not Willpower Trying to remember who confirmed and who didn't — across texts, emails, DMs, and phone calls — is where most groomers burn out.
