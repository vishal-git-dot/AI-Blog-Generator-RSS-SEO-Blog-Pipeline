# 27: Gaming Keyboards, N-Key Rollover, and Anti-Ghosting: Everything Gamers Need to Know

## Overview

Gamers have pushed keyboard technology to its limits, demanding performance features that most office typists will never need. N-key rollover, anti-ghosting, ultra-fast actuation, programmable macros — this guide breaks down every gaming keyboard feature and explains why each one matters. N-Key Rollover Explained N-Key Rollover (NKRO) is the keyboard's ability to correctly register every key that is simultaneously pressed, regardless of how many keys those are. In competitive gaming, it's common to press 4, 6, or even more keys at the same time (e.g., WASD + Shift + Space + a weapon key). A keyboard without NKRO will "ghost" — failing to register some of those simultaneous presses. Rollover Type Keys Registered Simultaneously Suitable For 2KRO 2 keys Basic office typing 6KRO 6 keys Most gaming scenarios NKRO (Full) All keys Competitive gaming, fast typing Anti-Ghosting Ghosting occurs when pressing multiple keys causes additional, unpressed keys to be registered as pressed — a "ghost" keypress. Anti-ghosting technology uses matrix scanning or individual key registration to prevent this. Most gaming keyboards advertise anti-ghosting, but the specific implementation varies significantly in quality. Actuation Force and Response Time Gaming keyboards often feature switches with lighter actuation forces (35–45g vs the 45–60g of typical office switches) to enable faster key presses. Some high-end gaming keyboards also feature optical switches that register keystrokes at the speed of light — literally — using infrared light beams instead of physical metal contacts.
