# How to Build a Small Business Website: Pages, Content and Launch

## Overview

How to Build a Small Business Website A good small business website explains the offer, earns trust, and makes it easy to contact the company. Visual polish matters, but a beautiful site with unclear services or broken forms will not serve customers. Start with the customer's task Choose the primary action: book, request a quote, call, or purchase. Identify the questions people ask before taking that action. A repair service needs opening hours and coverage areas; a contractor needs project examples and a quote process. Choose the right format A single-page website works for one focused service. A multi-page site suits several distinct offerings. An online store introduces catalog, checkout, delivery, and order management. Build only what the business can maintain. Plan essential pages Start with Home, Services, About, Contact, and appropriate privacy information. Add authentic case studies, testimonials, and FAQs when they answer real questions. The homepage should state the offer plainly, explain the process, show evidence, and provide a clear call to action. Make the experience practical Prepare accurate descriptions, original photographs, service conditions, and contact details before design begins. Test navigation and forms on actual phones. Use readable typography, clear buttons, and layouts that work without hover. Protect customer data Collect only information required to answer an inquiry. Review privacy notices against applicable law. Use HTTPS, updated software, backups, and limited administrative access. Test the entire form-delivery process, not just the on-screen success message. Make pages discoverable Create useful pages for genuinely distinct services. Give each a descriptive title, heading, and URL. Link related content and check that search engines can access important pages. Avoid thin, nearly identical location pages. Choose a maintainable system Ask whether the owner can edit services, images, and contact details without a developer. Confirm domain ownership, backup procedures, ongoing costs, and migration options. Pre-launch checklist The main visitor action is clear. Services and contact details are accurate. Forms deliver test inquiries. Mobile navigation works. Testimonials and photographs are genuine. Privacy information matches data practices. HTTPS, backups, and access controls are configured. Important pages have titles and internal links. Someone owns ongoing updates. After launch, improve the website based on customer questions and real inquiries rather than adding features for their own sake.
