# 8 Mac menu bar apps that actually earn their spot on a coding Mac (2026)

## Overview

You know the moment. You glance up at the menu bar to check something and it's a wall of 25 icons, half of which you can't name. The MacBook notch has quietly eaten three of them. The menu bar is the most valuable real estate on a Mac, and most of us let it fill up by accident. Here's the short list of menu bar apps I'd actually let stay there on a Mac used for coding, and what each one earns its spot for. The rule I use An icon stays only if I look at it or click it at least once a day. Everything else gets hidden or quit. With that rule, these eight make the cut. 1. Ice (free, open source) Ice is the first thing I'd install, because it fixes the problem every other item on this list creates. It hides and shows menu bar items so you keep only the ones you care about visible. It's free, open source, and needs macOS 14 or later. If you've ever lost an icon behind the notch, this is the fix. 2. Stats (free, open source) Stats puts a system monitor in your menu bar: CPU, memory, disk, network, battery and more. You pick which modules show. Handy when a build, a Docker container or a local model suddenly makes the fans spin up and you want to know why. Install with brew install stats or grab the DMG from the releases page. 3. Itsycal (free) Itsycal is a tiny menu bar calendar that reads from the Mac Calendar app. You can see and create events, view and complete Reminders, and join virtual meetings from it. It has a configurable shortcut to open it, and yes, it supports Vim motions for navigation. 4. Maccy (free, open source) Maccy is a clipboard manager that does one job: keep your copy history and let you search it fast. Hit the shortcut, type, press Enter. It's keyboard-first, stores everything on your Mac, and is MIT licensed. It needs macOS 14 or later. Once you have clipboard history you stop re-copying the same error message three times. 5. Amphetamine (free on the Mac App Store) Amphetamine keeps your Mac awake with a simple switch in the menu bar, or automatically through Triggers. You can keep it awake indefinitely, for a set time, while a file downloads, or while a specific app is running. Useful when a long test run or a coding agent is working and you step away. 6. CleanShot X ($35 once, or via Setapp) CleanShot X is the screenshot and screen recording tool I'd pick for bug reports and PR descriptions. It adds annotation, a video editor with smart zooms, and optional cloud links. The Basic license is $35 once with a year of updates (optional renewal at $19 a year). Cloud Pro is $10 per user per month billed annually. It also comes with a Setapp subscription. It needs macOS 13 or later. 7. Rectangle (free, open source, Pro available) Rectangle moves and resizes windows with keyboard shortcuts or by dragging them to screen edges and corners. Repeat a shortcut to cycle through sizes. It lives in the menu bar for quick access to layouts. The free version is open source and supports macOS 14+. Rectangle Pro adds custom shortcuts for any size and position. 8. TokenBar ($5/month Basic or $15 lifetime Pro) If you code with AI agents, the number you check most is probably how much of your limit is left and when it resets. TokenBar puts usage and reset times for Claude, Codex, Cursor, Gemini and Copilot in one menu bar view. It shows things like session and weekly usage and a pace check against the reset. It's built on Peter Steinberger's MIT-licensed CodexBar and credits Pixel Agents for its agent status view. It doesn't give you any extra quota, it just shows you where you stand. Basic is $5/month, Pro is $15 lifetime, and it needs macOS 14 or later. What didn't make the cut Weather, a second clock, VPN icons I only touch once a week, and every app that adds an icon by default and never asks. Those all go behind Ice. What's the one menu bar app you'd never remove? Drop it in the comments, I'm always looking for a ninth.
