# How to Find Free Full-Text Papers by DOI in 5 Steps

## Overview

(Disclosure: Written by the Equalang team, makers of the translation tools mentioned in this guide.) Pasting a raw DOI into doi.org often drops you straight onto a publisher paywall, even when a legal, free full-text PDF exists in an institutional repository or preprint server. This guide walks through a five-step resolution workflow—plus a zero-dependency Python 3 script querying the Unpaywall, OpenAlex, and Europe PMC REST APIs—to locate legal open-access PDFs and license metadata from any DOI. Why doi.org Hits a Paywall Even When a Paper Is Free A Digital Object Identifier ( DOI ) such as 10.1145/3592442 is a permanent handle managed by the International DOI Foundation. When you paste https://doi.org/10.1145/3592442 into your browser, the resolver does one job: it forwards you to the publisher's canonical landing page for the Version of Record ( VoR ). That default redirect creates a common misconception. Hitting a subscription paywall on a publisher's site does not mean the paper lacks a legal, free full-text PDF. Under public-funder mandates (such as NIH, Wellcome Trust, and Horizon Europe) and university open-access policies, authors routinely deposit the peer-reviewed text into institutional repositories, disciplinary preprint servers, or PubMed Central. Because the primary Crossref record stays pointed at the publisher's commercial URL, you need an open-access resolution layer to bridge the gap between a raw DOI string and those legal repository copies. Below is a five-step resolution workflow to turn a raw DOI into a verified, free full-text PDF in under 30 seconds, ordered from direct URL redirects to programmatic API and metadata lookups. Unpaywall harvests legal open-access copies from over 50,000 publishers, institutional repositories, and preprint servers worldwide. Step 2: Query OpenAlex for Every Alternate Repository Location and License Sometimes a paper is hosted in three different places—a paywalled journal site, a university archive after an embargo lift, and a preprint server—and you want to see every option plus its exact reuse license. OpenAlex indexes these relationships in a single record. You can search the DOI in the web interface at https://openalex.org/ , or inspect the raw JSON metadata directly in your browser by pasting: https://api.openalex.org/works/https://doi.org/<DOI> Look at two fields in the response: open_access.oa_url : The direct link to the primary open-access full text. locations : An array listing every repository where the work resides (such as Zenodo, HAL, Europe PMC, or a university eprints server), along with is_oa , version ( publishedVersion , acceptedVersion , or submittedVersion ), and license . Checking locations is especially useful when one institutional server is temporarily offline or when you need to confirm whether a paper carries a CC BY license before reusing or translating it. Step 3: Decode Preprint Prefix Patterns and Check Biomedical Mandates You can often tell where a free PDF lives simply by reading the digits before and after the slash in the DOI: DataCite arXiv DOIs ( 10.48550/arxiv.XXXX.XXXXX ) : Every arXiv paper automatically receives a DataCite DOI with the prefix 10.48550 . You do not even need a lookup tool—strip 10.48550/arxiv. and append the remaining identifier to https://arxiv.org/pdf/ . For example, 10.48550/arxiv.2305.18290 maps directly to https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.18290 . Biomedical & Life Sciences DOIs (PubMed Central / Europe PMC) : Even when an article is published in a subscription medical journal, research funded by the NIH, Howard Hughes Medical Institute, or European agencies must be deposited in PubMed Central ( https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/ ) or Europe PMC ( https://europepmc.org/ ), either upon publication or after a publisher embargo. Paste the raw DOI into the PMC or Europe PMC search bar; if a PMCID (such as PMC8376543 ) is linked to that DOI, the full text and downloadable PDF are legally free. For a broader overview of disciplinary archives that index legal manuscripts by DOI, see our guide to legal open-access alternatives for finding research papers . Frequently Asked Questions Can every DOI be resolved to a free PDF legally? No. While roughly half of recent scholarly articles have a legal Gold or Green open-access copy indexed by Unpaywall and OpenAlex, older articles or papers published in closed subscription journals whose authors never self-archived a postprint may not have a free web copy. When all five steps above show no open version, use your university library's Interlibrary Loan ( ILL ) service or email the corresponding author listed on the doi.org abstract page for a personal copy. What is the difference between a journal DOI and a preprint DOI for the same paper? A paper first posted on arXiv or bioRxiv receives a preprint DOI (such as 10.48550/arxiv.2305.18290 or 10.1101/... ). Once accepted and published by a journal, the publisher assigns a separate Crossref DOI for the Version of Record. OpenAlex and Google Scholar link these versions together under one work cluster, which is why searching the journal DOI in OpenAlex ( locations ) or Unpaywall often leads you straight back to the free preprint or accepted manuscript. Why can't I find an open-access paper with a CC BY-NC or CC BY-ND license on Equalang Plaza? Creative Commons licenses that include NC (Non-Commercial) or ND (No-Derivatives) prohibit commercial redistribution or derivative adaptations such as full-text translations. You can still locate and read those papers for free via oadoi.org , OpenAlex, or PubMed Central, whereas Equalang Plaza strictly hosts and accepts community shares for public-domain works and open-access papers licensed under CC BY , CC BY-SA , or CC0 .
