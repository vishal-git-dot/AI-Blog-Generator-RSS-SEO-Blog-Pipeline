# How to write a gallery page for an interior designer

## Overview

For an interior designer, the gallery page is usually the most visited page on the website. It is also the page most often done badly: forty unlabelled photos in a grid, no context, and no idea what the client actually asked for. Here is how to write a gallery page that works for you, not just looks nice. Think in projects, not photos Visitors don't hire you for a single beautiful corner. They hire you because you solved a problem similar to theirs. So group your work into projects, each with its own short story. A project card might read: "3BHK apartment, Pune – young family, wanted more storage without losing floor space." That one line tells a visitor more than ten pictures. A simple structure for each project Keep every project to the same small format so people can scan quickly: Title : type of space and city ("2BHK flat in Bhopal", "Café interior in Kochi") The brief : two or three sentences on what the client needed What you did : layout changes, materials, lighting, custom furniture Constraints : budget range in words (modest, mid-range), timeline, rented vs owned, Vastu preferences if relevant Photos : six to twelve, in a logical order – entrance, living, kitchen, bedrooms Client quote : only if the client actually said it and agreed to be quoted Write captions that teach A caption like "Living room" wastes space. Try "Living room – we moved the TV wall to free up the window and used a light oak laminate to keep the room bright." Captions are where you show your thinking. They also give search engines real text to understand the page, which helps people find you when they search for things like "modular kitchen designer in Jaipur". Show before and after, carefully Before-and-after photos are persuasive, but take them from the same angle and in similar light, or the comparison looks fake. Get written permission from the homeowner, and avoid showing anything personal: family photos, documents, house numbers. Let people filter Once you have more than eight or ten projects, add simple filters: Residential, Commercial, Kitchens, Bedrooms, Offices. Many visitors arrive with one specific need, and a filter saves them from scrolling through a restaurant when they only care about wardrobes. Don't ignore speed High-resolution photos are heavy. Export images at sensible sizes (around 1600px wide is plenty), use modern formats like WebP, and lazy-load images further down the page. A gallery that takes ten seconds to open on mobile data will lose most visitors before they see your best work. End every project with a next step At the bottom of each project, add a short line and a button: "Planning something similar? Share your floor plan on WhatsApp." Make it easy for an interested visitor to act while they are excited. Getting help If you are building the site yourself, most website builders have decent gallery templates; the writing is the part only you can do. If you would rather work with a team, it helps to understand how Indian agencies differ before choosing one. This overview of website building companies in India from MGTech Labs explains the options in plain terms. Quick checklist Every project has a title, brief and what you did Captions explain decisions, not just room names Permission taken for every photo shown Images compressed and fast on mobile A clear contact button after each project A gallery page written this way works like a portfolio and a sales conversation at the same time.
