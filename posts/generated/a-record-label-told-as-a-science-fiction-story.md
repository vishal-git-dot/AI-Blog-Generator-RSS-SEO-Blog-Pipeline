# A record label told as a science-fiction story

## Overview

Most label sites are a list of releases with cover art and buy links. That's fine. It's also forgettable. Universal Rift Records is an independent electronic music label where high-velocity drum & bass, hypnotic trance and mechanical techno converge, and the catalogue isn't just a list. It's a science-fiction world told in chapters, from first contact to the awakening of a machine supermind. The site is live, and this is how I think about building a label as a story. Why a story at all Electronic music already lives in sci-fi territory. The sounds, the titles, the artwork all lean toward space, machines and signals from somewhere else. Most labels gesture at that and move on. I wanted to commit to it: one world, one long arc, and every release a piece of it. That gives the catalogue a shape. A release isn't just the next one in the list. It's the next chapter, and it belongs somewhere. The sounds of one world Sound What it brings Drum & bass Velocity Trance Hypnosis Techno Machinery The territories The world is split into territories: Rift, Event Horizon, Starbirth and Signal. They give releases a place to belong, so a listener can follow a part of the world they like instead of scrolling a flat release list. I'm deliberately not spelling out every rule of each territory here. Some of that is for the music to do. Chapters, not just releases The story runs from first contact to the awakening of a machine supermind. Releases such as First Signal and Monolith sit inside that arc. The titles do some of the storytelling on their own, which is the point: you should be able to read the catalogue and feel the story moving even before you press play. The sounds meet in this world. Drum & bass brings the velocity. Trance brings the hypnosis. Techno brings the machinery. They aren't kept in separate rooms; the territories are where they meet. If you make drum & bass, trance or techno that sounds like it belongs in this world, there's a demo submission form on the site. Read a bit of the story first. It helps. What I ask of every new release Does it move the story forward or deepen a part of it? Which territory does it belong to? Do the title and artwork read as part of the same world? Will it sit comfortably next to First Signal and Monolith? Is it out where people actually listen? What's honestly early The label is young. With an inaugural release, the catalogue is at the beginning of its story, not the middle. That's how labels start, and I'd rather say so than dress it up with borrowed credibility. The story is also a commitment. Once you tell people a world has rules, you have to keep them. Every future release has to fit, which is harder than just putting out the next good track.
