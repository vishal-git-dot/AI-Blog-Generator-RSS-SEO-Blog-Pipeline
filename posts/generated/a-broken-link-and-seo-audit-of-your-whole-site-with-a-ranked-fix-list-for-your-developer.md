# A broken-link and SEO audit of your whole site, with a ranked fix list for your developer

## Overview

Most sites collect small SEO problems quietly: a page that now 404s, a link to a partner site that died, a title that grew past 60 characters, a noindex left on after a migration, a new section nobody added to the sitemap. None of them is urgent on its own, which is why they pile up. What a site owner or an agency needs is not a 40-page report but a short list, worst first, with the exact URLs to fix. This post sets that up as a scheduled audit in Python, then compares the SEO audit and broken-link tools on the Apify Store using the prices their Store pages listed at the time of writing. Disclosure: I'm Aurelix, and I built the Broken Link Crawler & SEO Audit . It's a paid tool (prices below). Check each Store page before you buy: prices change. What one run returns You give it one site. It reads the site's XML sitemaps (from robots.txt or /sitemap.xml ), follows internal links on the same host up to maxPages , and returns: One page row per URL : status_code , redirects , title and title_length , meta_description and its length, h1_count , canonical_url , noindex , word_count , link and image counts, broken_links (each with its URL and status code), images_missing_alt_count , in_sitemap , response_time_ms , and issues : the issue codes on that page, most severe first. One summary row : pages_audited , health_score (the share of pages with no high or medium issue), status-code counts, and issues ranked by severity and pages affected, each with up to five example URLs. Broken means a real 404/410, a 5xx or a dead host. Links that answer 401, 403 or 429 (common for sites that block automated checks) are counted as unverified, so they don't clutter the fix list.
