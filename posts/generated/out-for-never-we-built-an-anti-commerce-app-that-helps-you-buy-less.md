# Out for Never — we built an anti-commerce app that helps you buy less

## Overview

There is a whole class of purchases you make not because you need them, but because the moment of buying feels good. The checkout rush. The "it's on the way" dopamine. The tiny progress bar marching toward the shipping confirmation. Out for Never is the app I built to break that loop — and instead of a savings app that nags you, it gives you the whole buying experience without the buying . What it does You type in the thing you almost bought: the gadget, the impulse Amazon cart, the thing in your size at 2am. Pick a price, a category, how strong the temptation was. Then you place the order. Theatrically. Instead of a real charge, a completely fictional logistics network kicks in. Your "parcel" gets a tracking number. A courier picks it up. It travels across a route that doesn't exist, hits absurd incidents, gets manually rerouted, and eventually — beautifully — is never delivered. You keep the real money. The parcel goes nowhere. And somehow that scratch is itched . Every avoided purchase is recorded in a local vault: money kept, streaks, temptation intensity, a 28-day heatmap, categories, savings goals, even a CSV export and JSON backup. All in your browser's localStorage . No account, no tracking, nothing leaves your device. You can also quarantine a temptation for 1–30 days instead of deciding right now. Try it (it's free) The core intervention is completely free — no signup, no card. Go to outfornever.com , run through your first fake order, and watch your parcel get lost somewhere south of reason. The voucher for dev.to readers Because I'd rather you gift the experience than buy more stuff, there's a launch voucher for the premium fictional gifts (a cinematic reveal story you can send to someone): DEVTOFOREVER — 50% off any premium gift, valid for the first 25 redemptions . Drop it into the Voucher code field on the Send page. It applies to the Spark, Cinematic and Grand Gesture tiers — no physical item is ever shipped, because nothing is shipped, because nothing exists. A weird but real behaviour change The product is deliberately absurd. But the mechanism is real: a lot of impulse buying is about the anticipation , not the object. Giving that anticipation a fictional outlet — with courier lore and "ESTIMATED NON-ARRIVAL" countdowns — is, frankly, better entertainment than the real parcel will ever be. It's a Cloudflare Worker + a framework-free SPA, one of those "why not build the joke" weekend projects that refused to stay a joke.
