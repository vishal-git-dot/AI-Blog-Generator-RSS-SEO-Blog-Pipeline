# I Submitted My Free Tool to 100+ Directories — Here's What Actually Works (and What's a Trap)

## Overview

I build free PDF tools that run 100% in the browser — no signup, no upload, no server. The site now has 61 tools. Building it was the fun part. Getting anyone to find it was the real work. Over the last month I worked through 100+ free directories and launch boards, submitting the same site over and over. My submission log now has 182 entries: 32 submissions, 98 skips, 17 outright failures, 25 blocked on sign-in. Here's the honest picture — including the traps nobody warns you about. What actually went live with zero friction A small group of directories just worked. No account, no payment, no badge, no email verification dance: SiteLike — submitted, listing live the same day, with iLovePDF and SmallPDF shown as similar sites. Zearches and MeWebLink — both live within an hour of submitting. Toolfound — free, but it verifies you own the site (a meta tag in your HTML). Listing live with a "Verified owner" badge and a launch date months out. Favors.dev — magic-link sign-in, listing in review, go-live date given upfront (Nov 21). The Tool Directory, FinancesOnline, Startup Buffer, HyzenPro — plain forms, confirmation shown, pending human review. The pattern: the good ones ask for your URL, a name, a one-line description, and a category — then they get out of the way. If a "free" directory needs more than that from you, keep reading. Trap 1: "Free" means "add our badge to your homepage" This was the single most common trap. The pricing page says Free $0. The submit form works fine. Then, at the last step: "Add our badge/link to your site and we'll verify it before publishing." I hit this on ShowMeBest, Twelve Tools, LaunchAF, EarlyHunt, Nick Launches, Launch Llama, Launchtory, Shipybara (which wants a widget embed instead), and the free routes of Mydentify, DevPages and MarketingDB. The only no-badge route on most of them is a paid plan — $19, $29, $39, $49. A badge is not free. It's a piece of someone else's branding on your homepage, forever, in exchange for a listing that may or may not send you a single visitor. I skipped every single one. If your site is the product, your homepage is not ad inventory. Trap 2: paid-only behind a "Submit" button Some directories don't even pretend. aitools.fyi ($37), Startups.fm (£49), Whatsthebigdata ($599/$799), AI With Me ($26.90), Toolfolio ($99+). And SaaSCity, which used to be free — its FAQ now says "No. New listings are paid since November 6, 2026" ($19.99 one-time). Lesson: never assume a directory is free because a blog post from 2024 says so. Check the live pricing page before you fill in a single field. I wasted real time on forms that only revealed the price at the final step. Trap 3: "Please use your business email" GetLatka, SaaSworthy, SoftwareSuggest and Crozdesk all rejected pdfwordtools@gmail.com — "Please register with your corporate email." My site lives on a free pages.dev subdomain, so there is no company domain and no work email to give them. Same wall, different shape: Tiny Startups, StartupBase and AppLauncher.dev reject the URL itself — "Sites on free hosting subdomains can't be listed." If your project lives on *.pages.dev , *.vercel.app or *.netlify.app , a surprising number of directories won't take it at all, free or paid. A custom domain isn't just branding — it's a submission prerequisite on a lot of these sites. Trap 4: the account wall is the real submission Roughly a quarter of my log is BLOCKED, not by the directory's rules but by sign-in. The pattern repeats: magic link requested, code expired before I could use it (WhatTheAI: 5 links expired; Ubersuggest: 5 codes; LaunchAF: 4 codes). GitHub login rejected. Google password rejected. Some sites offer only Google sign-in, some only GitHub, some email-plus-password with no magic-link fallback. Practical takeaway: if a directory's only sign-in route is one you can't complete in under five minutes, walk away and come back when you can sit with it live. Re-requesting expiring codes in a loop just gets you rate-limited. Trap 5: dead sites and broken forms Not every failure is your fault. AI Valley's form returned the same "There was an error trying to send your message" on three separate attempts across two days. Launching Next silently swallowed six submissions — no confirmation, no error, no listing. Slant has been down with a Cloudflare 526 SSL error on three re-checks. store.app's DNS is dead; AI Lib is unreachable; Appscope throws a Heroku application error. Check the site actually loads and the form actually confirms before you invest twenty minutes in it. And if a form gives you no confirmation and no reference number, don't submit it again "just to be sure" — I did that once and likely created a duplicate in someone's review queue. The queue reality nobody mentions Even the honest free routes are slow. PeerPush put me at queue position #10,752 with an estimated wait of three months and eighteen days . SaaS Page's earliest free week was Dec 28. ScrollLaunch: January 2027. The Tool Directory and FinancesOnline: manual review, no timeframe given. Free directories are a slow-burn channel. Submit, forget about it, and let the listings trickle live over months. If you need traffic this week, directories are the wrong tool — write something useful instead (like this post). What I'd do differently Check pricing live before filling any form. Blog lists go stale; SaaSCity flipped to paid overnight. Skip anything that needs a badge, embed, or link-back. Your homepage is not the price of a listing. Buy the custom domain first if you plan to do directory submissions seriously. pages.dev gets rejected outright in too many places. Use a dedicated product email and keep its inbox open while you submit — magic links and 6-digit codes expire in 5–10 minutes. Log every attempt — site, date, outcome, URL. After 100+ submissions you will absolutely re-submit somewhere you've already done. I nearly did. Verify "live" yourself. A confirmation screen is not a listing. Search the directory for your product name and open the listing page. The scoreboard so far From 100+ directories worked: a handful live already (SiteLike, Zearches, MeWebLink, Toolfound, DevHunt, Startup Fame), a couple dozen submitted and sitting in review queues, and the rest skipped or blocked for the reasons above. Total spent: $0. Total badges added to my site: 0. Distribution is unglamorous work, but it's honest work — and the directories that respect builders (plain form, no badge, no paywall) are the ones worth your time. The site is PdfWord — https://pdfwordtools.pages.dev — 61 free PDF tools, all in your browser. If you've done the directory grind for your own project, I'd genuinely like to hear which ones actually sent you traffic. That's the data point I still don't have.
