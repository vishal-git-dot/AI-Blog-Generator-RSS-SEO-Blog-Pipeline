# Pre-Deployment SEO Checks: 14 Tasks with HTML Examples

## Overview

Your website builds successfully, the design looks good, and the deployment is ready. Before you ship, check the details that affect how visitors and search engines access your content. Here are 14 practical checks covering HTML metadata, content, images, performance, and indexing. 📖 Read AI Build Infra's website launch SEO checklist on Issuu for a compact reference sheet. 1. Give every page a clear purpose Each page should answer a specific question or help visitors complete a task. Before deployment, check: Does the page explain its topic immediately? Does the content match what the title promises? Is the next step clear? Avoid publishing multiple service pages that contain almost identical copy. 2. Check your title tags Write a unique, descriptive <title> for every important page. html <title>Website Launch SEO Checklist | AI Build Infra</title> 3. Add useful meta descriptions A meta description should explain what readers can expect from the page. html <meta name="description" content="Prepare your website for launch with 14 practical SEO checks covering titles, headings, images, internal links, mobile usability, indexing and loading speed."> Aim for approximately 150–160 characters, including spaces, as a writing guideline. Google may generate a different search snippet based on user queries. 4. Use one clear H1 per page Establish a clear semantic hierarchy for search engines and screen readers. html <h1>Website Launch SEO Checklist</h1> <h2>Review Your Page Content</h2> <h3>Check Titles and Descriptions</h3> Avoid jumping levels (for example, putting an <h3> directly under an <h1> without an <h2>). 5. Remove unfinished content Scan your templates for: "Lorem ipsum" placeholder text Sample testimonials or stock team bios Broken contact email addresses and test phone numbers Ensure all pricing and service descriptions reflect your live offerings. 6. Use keywords naturally Include relevant search terms where they help clarify the page topic. Avoid keyword stuffing or repeating phrases unnaturally in every heading. 7. Keep URLs descriptive and clean Choose readable, static URLs that indicate page hierarchy: text https://example.com/services/web-development/ If you are migrating or changing an existing URL structure, set up 301 redirects from the legacy URLs to the new pages. 8. Connect related pages (Internal Linking) Link related guides and service pages together using contextual anchor text. Optimize your images Use modern, lightweight image formats (WebP or AVIF) for photos and illustrations. Test the mobile experience With mobile-first indexing, test your site on a physical device: Verify touch target sizing (buttons and links should be easily tappable). Ensure there is zero horizontal scrolling. Check that navigation menus collapse and expand smoothly. 11. Check loading speed & Core Web Vitals Audit your production URLs with Google PageSpeed Insights : Aim for Largest Contentful Paint (LCP) under 2.5 seconds. Eliminate Cumulative Layout Shift (CLS = 0). Defer non-critical third-party scripts. 12. Review indexing & robots settings Ensure staging rules are removed before going live: Verify your robots.txt does not contain Disallow: /. Confirm production pages do not have <meta name="robots" content="noindex">. Ensure your staging environment remains private. 13. Validate canonical URLs Prevent duplicate content issues across HTTP/HTTPS and www/non-www variants: html <link rel="canonical" href="https://example.com/website-launch-checklist/"> Ensure the canonical points to the live, preferred production URL. 14. Complete a final launch check Test all contact forms, webhook integrations, SSL certificates, and your sitemap.xml.
