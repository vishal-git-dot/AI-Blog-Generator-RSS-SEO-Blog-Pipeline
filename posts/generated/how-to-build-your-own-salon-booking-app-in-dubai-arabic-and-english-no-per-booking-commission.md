# How to build your own salon booking app in Dubai (Arabic and English, no per-booking commission)

## Overview

Spend ten minutes in any UAE salon owners' group and you'll see the same question every few weeks: "Which booking software do you use?" The replies split three ways. Some owners love a big marketplace app. Some complain about setup fees, per-booking commissions, or a booking page that doesn't look like their brand. And a surprising number admit they still take most bookings on WhatsApp and write them in a diary at reception. This guide is for the third and second groups. It walks through what a salon booking app actually needs, then builds one step by step with an AI app builder, using the exact prompts you'd type. At the end there's an honest section on what you give up compared with a ready-made salon platform, so you can decide which route fits your salon. What a salon in the Gulf actually needs from a booking app Before choosing any tool, write down how a booking moves through your salon today. For most small and mid-size salons in Dubai, Abu Dhabi, Riyadh or Doha it looks like this: The client finds you on Instagram, Google Maps or by word of mouth. They message to book , usually on WhatsApp, often in Arabic, sometimes in English, Russian or Tagalog. Reception checks who is free : which stylist, which chair or room, how long the service takes. The booking goes into a diary , a spreadsheet, or a shared calendar. Someone reminds the client the day before, if they remember. The client arrives , pays at the counter, and maybe books the next visit. The pain points are always in steps 2, 3 and 5: messages answered late, double bookings when two receptionists use different diaries, and no-shows because no reminder went out. So the minimum app is smaller than most software makes it look: A public booking page showing services, prices and durations, in Arabic and English. A slot picker that only shows times when the chosen stylist (or any stylist) is free. An admin screen for reception : today's bookings by stylist, with buttons to confirm, move or cancel. A client list with visit history and notes ("allergic to this product", "prefers Sara"). Reminders , at least a list of tomorrow's bookings that reception can message, or automatic messages if you connect a provider. Everything else (gift cards, packages, loyalty points, inventory) can come later. Step 1: describe your salon in one prompt In an AI app builder like Mythex you start by describing the app in plain language. Be specific about your services and how your salon works. The more real your description, the less you'll need to fix later. Here's the first prompt: A booking app for a ladies' beauty salon in Dubai. Arabic and English with a language switch, Arabic right-to-left. Public booking page: services with category, price in AED and duration (haircut 45 min AED 150, blow-dry 30 min AED 90, colour 120 min AED 450, manicure 45 min AED 100, pedicure 60 min AED 130). Client picks a service, then a stylist or "any available", then a free time slot, and enters name and phone number. Opening hours Saturday to Thursday 10:00–22:00, Friday 14:00–22:00. Admin page for reception: today's bookings grouped by stylist, confirm / reschedule / cancel, and a client list with visit history and notes. Mythex writes the code, creates a Postgres database for services, stylists, clients and bookings, and shows a live preview within a few minutes. Open the booking page in the preview and try to book something, as a client would. Step 2: fix what doesn't match how your salon works The first version is never exactly right, and that's fine. You keep refining by chat. Typical follow-ups for a salon: Each stylist only does some services. Nour does colour and blow-dry, Sara does haircut and blow-dry, Maria does nails only. Only show a stylist for services they do. Add a 15-minute cleaning buffer after every colour appointment. When a client books with "any available", assign the stylist with the fewest bookings that day. Add a "home service" option for manicure and pedicure with an area field (Dubai Marina, JLT, Downtown, Jumeirah) and AED 50 extra. After each change, book a test appointment again. Try the awkward cases on purpose: two people booking the same slot, a booking that runs past closing time, a Friday morning. Step 3: make the admin side useful for reception Reception is the person who will live in this app all day, so ask them what they need. Usually it's something like: On the admin page add a day view with one column per stylist and bookings as blocks, like a calendar. Let reception drag a booking to another time or stylist. Add a "walk-in" button that creates a booking for now with the first free stylist. Add a "Tomorrow" tab listing every booking with the client's phone number and a WhatsApp button that opens a pre-filled reminder message in Arabic or English, depending on the client's language. That last one is a practical trick. Fully automatic WhatsApp or SMS messages need a messaging provider account (and each message has a cost), but a one-click pre-filled WhatsApp link costs nothing and still cuts no-shows, because reception actually sends the reminders. If you later want automatic messages, you can ask the builder to connect a provider such as Twilio or the WhatsApp Business API using your own account keys. Step 4: protect the admin pages The booking page is public; the admin pages must not be. Ask for it explicitly: Only signed-in staff can open the admin pages. Add a staff login with email and password, and two roles: Owner (sees everything including revenue) and Reception (bookings and clients only). Mythex builds the sign-in with your app's own database. It isn't a separate login product, so test it: open the admin URL in a private window and check that it asks you to sign in. Step 5: publish it on your own domain When it works, publish. You get a public URL straight away, and you can connect your own domain (for example book.yoursalon.ae) from the project's domain settings. Put the link in your Instagram bio, your Google Maps profile and your WhatsApp Business auto-reply. That's where most Gulf salon clients will find it. If you want the app on clients' phones, the Pro plan can also turn the same app into an Android app (a download, or Google Play internal testing) and an iPhone app through TestFlight. These are your web screens wrapped as an app, not a fully native app, so for most salons the web booking link is enough to start. What you get, and the honest limits What you get: a booking app that's yours. Your branding, your services, Arabic and English, your domain, and your client data in your own database. No per-booking commission: you pay for the builder plan, not per appointment. When something changes (a new stylist, Ramadan hours, a new service), you describe the change and it's done. What you give up compared with a ready-made salon platform: No marketplace. Platforms like Fresha or Booksy also list you in their consumer app, which can bring new clients. Your own app only gets the clients you send to it. If most of your new clients come from a marketplace, keep it and use your own app for repeat clients. Payments need your own provider. Taking deposits online means connecting a payment provider account (Stripe, or a Gulf provider such as Tap or Telr) and asking the builder to add it. Not hard, but it's your account and their fees. Point-of-sale hardware and inventory aren't included out of the box. If you need a card terminal integration, a dedicated salon POS is still the easier choice. You are the one who maintains it. Changes are by chat, not code, but someone at the salon has to own the app. A sensible path for many salons is to build the booking page and reception screen first, run it next to your current system for two weeks, and only switch fully once reception prefers it. For salon groups and teams If you run more than one branch, or you have a manager, a marketing person and reception all touching the app, the Business plan adds a shared team workspace. Each person signs in with their own account and role (Admin, Editor, Viewer), you can keep internal apps members-only so only your staff can open them, and Google Workspace sign-in works if your salon uses Google for email. That's useful when the same workspace also holds a staff rota app or a stock list for each branch.
