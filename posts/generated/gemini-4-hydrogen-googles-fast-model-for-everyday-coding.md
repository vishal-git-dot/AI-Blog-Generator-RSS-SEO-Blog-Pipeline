# Gemini 4 Hydrogen: Google's Fast Model for Everyday Coding

## Overview

Have you ever asked a search engine a question with three parts, a typo, and a very specific detail, all in one go, and watched it answer only the easy part? Google introduced Gemini 4 Hydrogen on November 12, 2026, a fast model built for everyday coding and multi-step work where an AI carries out a task on its own. It is the third member of the Gemini 4 family, next to Carbon and Argon, and it is already rolling out across Search, the Gemini app, Google AI Studio, the Gemini API, and Google Antigravity. The fast one in the family Here is the simplest way I think about it. Carbon and Argon are the deep thinkers, for long, hard reasoning problems, the kind where you want the model to sit with a tricky architecture decision or an unclear bug for a while. Hydrogen is the one that gets on with the job. It is built for rapid execution: writing code, running tests, and checking changes without making you wait. Google positions it as the model for the work you do all day, every day, rather than the rare problem that needs a whole afternoon of thinking. That split feels familiar to me. Building software has always had both jobs. Somebody has to draw the plan, and somebody has to lay the bricks quickly and neatly. A team with only planners never ships anything, and a team with only fast hands and no plan builds the wrong thing, quickly. Photo by Gabriel Heinzer on Unsplash Working as a pair That pairing is not just a marketing idea. In Antigravity, Google's coding platform, a new feature called Orchestrator switches between Carbon and Hydrogen inside the same session, so Carbon can coordinate while Hydrogen powers the worker agents that build and test changes at the same time. On its own, Hydrogen is also a step up from the model it replaces, Gemini 3.8 Flash: Google says it finishes tasks 2.5 times faster at its default setting, with its Terminal-Bench 4.1 score rising from 18.2 percent to 57 percent. Those are Google's own numbers, so take them as a claim to watch rather than a settled fact. What it costs, and where you will meet it For developers, the pricing is simple: $1.50 per million input tokens and $7.50 per million output tokens, flat across its one million token context window. It comes with four thinking levels, low, medium, high, and a new extra high level called xhigh, so you can pick how much reasoning a task is worth before you pay for it in time and tokens. But most people will meet Hydrogen without ever opening an API dashboard. In Google Search it is now the default model in AI Mode, and it handles the hardest questions in AI Overviews. Google says it breaks a complicated question into its parts and answers each one, even when the question has typos or very specific details. The Verge reported the same rollout on November 12, 2026, quoting Robby Stein, Google's VP of product for Search, who called it the biggest model upgrade to Search yet. Fast is a feature, not a shortcut Speed on its own is not very interesting. What matters is whether a fast model stays careful while it hurries. Google says Hydrogen is more disciplined when a tool call fails or a search comes back empty, and that it adapts instead of guessing its way forward. If that holds up, it matters more than any benchmark, because the whole point of an everyday model is that you trust it with the boring, repeated work. So here is my question for you. If the fast model gets good enough to handle the routine coding, the searching, and the first draft of a task, what do you want to spend your own thinking time on? The planning, the judgment calls, the parts no model can do for you? That is the work Hydrogen is supposed to free up. Cover photo by Mohammad Rahmani on Unsplash
