# How to Trim Audio for a Sycamore Glen Video — Free, in Your Browser

## Overview

A sycamore glen is the kind of place that makes you want to keep the camera rolling — and then you get home with forty minutes of audio for a three-minute video. The fix is simple: trim the recording down to the parts that actually belong in the video, before you touch the timeline. Step 1: Open AudioCutter Go to AudioCutter and open your audio file in the browser. It handles MP3, WAV, M4A and FLAC, and the file never leaves your device, so there is no upload wait and no account to create. Step 2: Mark the section you want Drag the handles on the waveform to select the good part. Play it back once. Cut the false starts, the long pauses while you were framing the shot, and anything after you stopped talking. A tight selection is what makes a video feel professional. Step 3: Smooth the edges Add a short fade-in and fade-out so the audio does not start or stop with a click. This matters most over quiet outdoor footage, where every abrupt edge is audible. One or two seconds of fade is plenty. Step 4: Export and edit Export as MP3 for easy sharing, or WAV if you plan to mix further in a desktop editor. Drop the trimmed clip under your Sycamore Glen footage and adjust the volume to sit under any narration. Trimming first means you are never dragging a forty-minute file around a three-minute timeline. A few practical tips Always keep the untrimmed original, and trim a copy. If your video has several glen scenes, trim each audio piece separately — it is faster than fixing one long file. And remember that trimming only removes audio; it cannot clean up wind or background hiss, so cut around the noisy parts instead of trying to rescue them.
