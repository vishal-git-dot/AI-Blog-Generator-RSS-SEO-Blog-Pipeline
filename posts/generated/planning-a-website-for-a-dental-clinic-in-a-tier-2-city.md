# Planning a website for a dental clinic in a tier-2 city

## Overview

If you run a dental clinic in a city like Nashik, Indore, Coimbatore or Dehradun, most of your new patients still come through word of mouth. But nowadays that word of mouth is usually followed by a quick search on the phone. A neighbour says "go to Dr. Sharma's clinic", and the person types the name into Google before calling. What they find in those few seconds decides whether they call you or the clinic down the road. Here is a practical way to plan a website that actually helps in that moment. Start with the questions patients really ask Before thinking about design, write down the questions your front desk answers every day. In most clinics the list looks like this: What are your timings, and are you open on Sunday? Do you do root canals, braces, implants, kids' treatment? Where exactly are you? Is there parking? Can I book an appointment for today? Roughly what will a cleaning or a filling cost? Each of these should be answered on the site within one or two taps. If your receptionist says the same sentence twenty times a day, that sentence belongs on the homepage. Keep the structure small A clinic site does not need thirty pages. A good starting structure is: Home – clinic name, doctor names, timings, a call button and a WhatsApp button near the top. Treatments – one short section per treatment in plain language. "Root canal: saving a badly decayed tooth instead of removing it" is more useful than technical terms. Doctors – qualifications, registration details and a real photo. Patients in smaller cities often choose the doctor, not the clinic. Visit us – embedded map, landmark-based directions ("opposite the SBI branch, first floor"), parking notes. Contact / Appointment – a simple form with name, phone and preferred time. Nothing more. Think about language In tier-2 cities, many patients are more comfortable in Hindi, Marathi, Tamil or another local language. You don't have to translate everything on day one. Even a short local-language section with timings, address and treatments makes older patients and their families feel welcome. Photos matter more than you think Show the actual waiting area, the chair, the sterilisation setup. Patients worry about hygiene, and a clean, well-lit photo of your autoclave builds more trust than any slogan. Avoid stock photos of smiling models; people can tell. Be careful with claims Medical advertising has rules, and patients are also sceptical. Skip phrases like "best dentist in the city" or "painless guaranteed". Describe what you do, how you do it and who does it. Before-and-after photos should only be used with written consent. Who builds it You can use a DIY builder if you have time, or work with a local freelancer or an agency. If you want a sense of how an Indian development team approaches projects like this, the overview by MGTech Labs on working with a web development firm in India is a useful read before you start talking to anyone. Whoever you choose, ask who will update timings and holiday notices after launch, because an outdated site is worse than none. A simple checklist before launch Clinic name, address and phone match your Google listing exactly Call and WhatsApp buttons work on mobile Timings include holidays Every treatment page answers "what, why, how long" The site loads in a few seconds on mobile data A small, honest, fast website will quietly do its job for years. That is all most clinics need.
