# Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash Generates and Edits 15-Second Videos in the Gemini API for $0.07 a Second

## Overview

On November 5, 2026, Google made Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash ( gemini-omni-1.2-flash ) generally available across the Gemini API and Google Cloud. Two weeks after shutting down Veo 3.1 Preview in the developer API on October 22, Google is positioning Omni Flash as its default model for generating, editing and extending video with synchronized audio. Three headline changes stand out: Native 15-second clips , up from 10 seconds in Omni 1.1 Flash, selectable in one-second steps from 3 to 15 seconds. A 30% price cut on video output tokens, dropping 720p generation from about $0.10 per second to roughly $0.07 per second ($12.00 per million video output tokens, down from $17.50). Multi-turn conversational editing and stronger reference adherence , accepting up to 6 reference images and refining clips across turns without starting over. One date in the release notes matters if you already ship on Omni 1.1: on December 3, 2026, every API call to gemini-omni-1.1-flash will automatically route to gemini-omni-1.2-flash . I spent Friday evening wiring gemini-omni-1.2-flash up on Linux with Python 3.12, google-genai 3.0.0, raw REST calls and ffprobe . Because my AI Studio key is on the Free Tier, where Omni 1.2 enforces a hard zero-quota wall, I couldn't render paid clips from my own prompts. Everything else on the wire was testable right now, and the first bug hit in 90 milliseconds.
