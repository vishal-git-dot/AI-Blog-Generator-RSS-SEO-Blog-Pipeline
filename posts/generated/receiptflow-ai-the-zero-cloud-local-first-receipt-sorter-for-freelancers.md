# ReceiptFlow AI: The Zero-Cloud, Local-First Receipt Sorter for Freelancers

## Overview

This is a submission for the Hacktoberfest Open-Source AI Challenge Week 2: Side Hustle What I Built Every tax season or quarterly review, I found myself facing the same chaotic chore: a desktop folder cluttered with scattered PDF invoices, train ticket receipts, cloud subscription receipts, and equipment purchases. When I looked for tools to automate this, I ran into two big dealbreakers: Predatory SaaS Pricing: Mainstream tools like Expensify, QuickBooks, or Dext charge $15 to $35 every single month just to scan basic receipts. As an independent builder, paying $200–$400 a year for simple OCR felt absurd. Privacy Dealbreakers: Uploading my receipts to cloud services meant transmitting sensitive banking details, client names, home addresses, and confidential project costs to remote servers and proprietary LLM APIs. I built ReceiptFlow AI to solve my own problem first—and package it as a clean, viable side-hustle product that other freelancers and indie builders would gladly pay for. ReceiptFlow AI is a 100% local, privacy-first receipt and invoice organizer . Documents are parsed on-device using open-weight models powered by Ollama ( gemma2:2b for embedded text and llava:7b for images). The frontend keeps all metadata inside the browser's IndexedDB , and clean CSV exports are generated client-side. No cloud databases. No monthly API bills. Zero documents ever leave the machine. Who Would Pay For It? Freelancers & Consultants bound by NDAs: Anyone working under non-disclosure agreements who cannot legally or ethically feed client-related expense documents into public cloud LLMs. Indie Builders with Subscription Fatigue: People tired of monthly recurring charges for basic utilities. Packaging this as a $29–$49 one-time purchase or desktop application offers huge value while costing me $0 in ongoing server/API overhead. Frequent Travelers & Digital Nomads: Anyone who needs to process travel receipts on trains, flights, or places with spotty internet connectivity. Open-Source AI Core (Ollama + Open-Weight Models) Text Extraction & Parsing: For PDFs containing embedded digital text, PyMuPDF extracts the raw characters in memory. I feed this text to Google's Gemma 2 2B ( gemma2:2b ) running locally in Ollama. It is fast, lightweight, and runs smoothly on consumer laptops without needing high-end GPUs. Vision Recognition: For photo receipts and scanned receipts, image bytes are encoded into base64 and processed locally by LLaVA 7B ( llava:7b ). Structured JSON Prompting: The prompt instructs the model to act as a strict invoice data extraction engine, returning only a single JSON object with standardized keys ( vendor , invoice_number , date , currency , category , subtotal , tax , total , line_items , notes ). 2. Backend Service (FastAPI + Pydantic v2) Built with Python 3.11 and FastAPI , bound strictly to loopback ( 127.0.0.1 ). Defensive Extraction: Strips markdown code fences (like triple-backtick JSON wrappers), isolates the JSON body with regex, and validates data through Pydantic models with defensive defaults. Stateless by Design: The backend never saves files to disk or writes to a database. Open-source AI was not just an implementation detail for ReceiptFlow AI— it was the only way this product could work . True Privacy Without Trusting "The Cloud": Closed AI APIs (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google Cloud) require transmitting private financial documents over the wire. Even with zero-retention policies, regulated freelancers and privacy-conscious builders cannot take that risk. Running an open-weight model locally guarantees that data never leaves the laptop. Zero Marginal Inference Cost: If I built this side hustle on top of closed APIs, every receipt processed would burn API credits ($0.01 to $0.05). A sudden influx of users or bulk uploads would destroy profitability. With open-weight models, my marginal cost per inference is literally $0.00. This makes a one-time purchase or open-core monetization model sustainable. Total Model Independence: I am never at the mercy of sudden API deprecations, rate limits, or vendor price hikes. If a newer, faster model comes out, switching requires changing just one environment variable in .env . Offline Resilience: I can process and organize my expenses on a train, on a flight, or in a coffee shop with spotty Wi-Fi.
