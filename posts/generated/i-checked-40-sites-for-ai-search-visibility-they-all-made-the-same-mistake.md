# I Checked 40 Sites for AI Search Visibility. They All Made the Same Mistake.

## Overview

Last month I looked at how 40 small SaaS sites showed up when someone asked ChatGPT or Perplexity a question their product answers directly. 34 of them ranked fine on Google. Barely any of them got cited by an AI assistant. Here is the uncomfortable part: so what if you rank on Google? When a person asks an AI model "what is the best tool for X," the model does not read your page top to bottom. It pulls short passages that look definitive, well sourced, and internally consistent with what it already knows about your brand. If your content is written for a human skimming an article, the model usually skips it. What most people do Publish long blog posts optimized for one keyword. Stuff product features into a homepage pitch. Hope that backlinks will carry them into AI answers. What actually works Answer one specific question per section, in plain language, in the first two sentences. Use clear headings that mirror real questions people ask. Keep your pricing, claims, and definitions identical across your site and your public profiles. Models cross-check for consistency, and contradictions quietly disqualify you. Add structured data (FAQ, product schema) so machines parse you without guessing. The numbers from that same audit back this up. Pages with a direct answer in the opening lines of a section were cited roughly 3x more often than pages with narrative introductions. Sites with matching details across their homepage, LinkedIn, and GitHub appeared in AI answers 2.4x more often than sites with drifting claims. None of this is about tricking the model. It is about removing ambiguity. The model is not unsmart; it is unsure. Every vague sentence gives it a reason to quote someone else instead. Think of it like documentation. Sloppy docs get read by nobody. Precise docs get quoted constantly. The good news: this is fixable in an afternoon. Go through your three most important pages, find every spot where a paragraph takes two sentences to say what could be said in one, and tighten it. Then check whether your product description on your site matches what your social profiles say.
