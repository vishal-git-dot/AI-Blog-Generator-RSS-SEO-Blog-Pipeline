# A naive SEC EDGAR expectations-gap rule failed its 2023+ holdout

## Overview

A simple fundamental rule can look excellent in a backtest and still fail a real holdout. I recently finished a point-in-time audit of an un-tuned SEC EDGAR expectations-gap screen on 28 live US stocks. In the pre-2023 training window, the rule looked strong. On filings from 2023 onward, it did not hold up. Every figure below was recomputed from a local as-filed quarterly panel built from official SEC EDGAR companyfacts and cached daily prices on 26 November 2026 (America/New_York). This is a research write-up, not investment advice, and not a signal feed. The leak that naive tests miss A 10-Q or 10-K is not available on the fiscal period end date. If a test enters on period end, or treats a later restatement as if it had been known then, it is using numbers the market had not seen. In this panel, every one of the 1,478 as-filed quarterly rows has a positive lag between period end and the SEC filed date. The shortest lag is 16 days. The GE Aerospace 10-Q for the period ended 2026-06-30 was filed on 2026-07-16 (accession 0000040545-26-000049 , checked against the raw companyfacts file). The test enters on 2026-07-17, the first trading day after the filing. The median lag in the panel is 32 days, and 935 rows (63.3%) lag by 30 days or more. The point-in-time rule is strict: Stamp every observation by the SEC filed date, never the period end date. Build trailing returns from the last close on or before filed . Enter on the first trading day strictly after filed , close-to-close versus SPY. Look up year-ago comparables only from facts that had already been filed. A pytest audit of the real panel passed 4 tests. 100% of the 1,478 rows satisfy entry_date > filed_date , and the trailing 6-month and 12-month lookback dates are on or before the filed date. This is a live-ticker prototype. Delisted, acquired, or bankrupt names are not included, so a backtest can look too good. Coverage is also shorter for some names, including CEG (17 quarters), XOM (28 quarterly as-filed rows from 2018-05-03), DIS (30), and VRT (31). 27 rows are missing a trailing 12-month return because there was not enough price history. A row is an expectations-gap candidate when both of these hold: Operational inflection: revenue year-over-year growth acceleration is greater than 0, or operating-margin year-over-year change is greater than 0 (or both). Price not already extended: trailing 12-month excess return versus SPY is less than or equal to 0. The numeric score adds one point for each of acceleration, margin expansion, and a non-extended trailing relative price. No parameter was tuned on the train or held-out sample. That screen produced 286 candidate rows: 204 filed on or before 2022-12-31 (train) and 82 filed on or after 2023-01-01 (holdout). Forward excess is the stock close-to-close return minus the SPY close-to-close return over 63, 126, 252, or 504 trading days. Held-out sample sizes shrink at longer horizons because some 2023+ filings have not finished the window, or a close was not available on the exact calendar date. In the full 1,478-row panel, forward excess is missing on 21, 45, 78, and 164 rows at those four horizons. What the holdout showed If I had published only the combined sample, the 12-month mean excess versus SPY would have been +11.04% with a 57.89% hit rate (N=266). That mixes the training window with the holdout. The holdout is the test that matters. The un-tuned rule looks strong in the pre-2023 sample. It does not hold up out of sample. Held-out hit rates are below 50% at every horizon. At 12 months the held-out mean is pulled up by a right tail while the median is -11.53% and the hit rate is only 33.87%. At 24 months the held-out mean is -21.66% (N=36, t=-2.467). Two caveats apply to every t-stat. First, quarterly filings for the same name produce overlapping holding periods, especially at 12 and 24 months, so a simple one-sample t-stat overstates significance. Second, the universe still excludes delisted names. This phase therefore demonstrates a working, auditable point-in-time pipeline. It does not demonstrate a validated expectations-gap edge. That is the correct outcome to log, not a result to dress up. Past tests, even careful ones, can fail later. None of the names or filings mentioned here is a recommendation to trade.
