# WooCommerce, Shopify or Custom eCommerce?

## Overview

Each eCommerce system in this comparison can be the best option, but there is no universal “best one,” only the one that is best for you, your situation, and your business. I build and support stores on all three of these approaches for clients, so this is not a feature checklist copied from marketing pages. It is my honest opinion on when Shopify, WooCommerce, or a fully custom eCommerce build is the right call, where each one shines, and where each one quietly costs you more than you expected. The Cheat Sheet Platform Ideal For Watch Out For Cost Reality Shopify Established brands moving online, small businesses selling straightforward products, and anyone who wants a store that just works with almost no maintenance Transaction fees, limited control over checkout and URL structure, weaker content and SEO flexibility Predictable monthly fee that looks more expensive than it is; almost everything operational is included WooCommerce Businesses that win on organic search and need heavily optimized product landing pages, or complex configurable products like decking and fencing Plugin-stack builds, premium add-on subscriptions, hosting and caching problems, and providers who assemble instead of engineer Cheaper on paper, more expensive in practice; hosting, maintenance, and plugin fees add up in the short run, not just the long run Custom eCommerce SaaS and subscription business models, custom products and product workflows, and businesses where the buying experience is the differentiator Choosing the wrong builder; AI-generated code without engineering experience behind it creates the same mess it was supposed to avoid Higher initial investment, dramatically lower long-term cost for a serious business, and AI code generation has cut the entry price significantly Shopify: For a Hosted Store That Needs Little Maintenance If you have an established, well-recognized brand and you want to start selling online, or you are a small business owner who wants very easy, very automated maintenance of your shop, and what you sell is relatively straightforward, Shopify is always the best option. That is my opinion, and I say it as someone who makes a living building the alternatives. Some people hem and haw over the fact that Shopify can look slightly more expensive than a hosted WordPress site running WooCommerce. In my experience, WooCommerce and WordPress will always cost you more, and not just in the long run, in the short run too. Behind the scenes, Shopify takes care of an enormous amount for you: hosting, security, PCI compliance, checkout optimization, software updates, and uptime during traffic spikes. That is well worth what it charges. A few things you get with Shopify that are easy to take for granted: Scaling is not your problem. You never worry about your store falling over during a rush of sales, a holiday promotion, or a viral moment. That capacity is baked into the platform. Fulfillment integrations are reliable. You can connect directly to fulfillment providers in far more dependable ways than the equivalent WordPress plugin stack. The checkout converts. Shopify has spent years optimizing one checkout flow across millions of stores, including Shop Pay. You benefit from all of it on day one. Maintenance is nearly zero. There are no plugin updates to test, no PHP versions to manage, and no security patches to schedule. The trade-offs are real but narrow: you pay transaction fees, you accept Shopify’s URL structure and checkout constraints, and deep content or SEO customization is harder than it is on WordPress. For most product businesses, none of that outweighs a system that simply does not break. WooCommerce: When Search Rankings or Product Complexity Drive the Business WooCommerce is not often my recommendation, but it has genuine advantages in two situations. The first is search. If your growth depends on organic visibility, WooCommerce gives you the ability to create heavily optimized product landing pages for SEO and AI search that can outrank what Shopify allows. You control the markup, the structured data, the internal linking, the page speed work, and the content around every product. Shopify keeps a lot of that surface area locked down; WordPress hands you all of it. The second is advanced product configuration. Businesses selling things like decking, fencing, or anything built from measurements and options often need configurators that go beyond a dropdown and a price. This kind of thing is possible in Shopify, but it is difficult, and you regularly hit the limits of what the platform will let you do. On WooCommerce you can build exactly the configuration flow the product requires. Here is the part most comparisons leave out: a good WooCommerce developer changes the effectiveness of this platform dramatically, and the wrong one is how WooCommerce earned its reputation for being slow and fragile. The thing to watch out for is any provider whose plan is to piece together a pile of plugins to build your shop, especially premium plugins with recurring fees. Every plugin is code you did not write, running on every page load, with its own update cycle and its own conflicts. With code generation through Claude and OpenAI’s models, many of those solutions can now be built cleanly and minimally in custom code. A small, purpose-built feature should replace a premium subscription add-on, not sit alongside six of them. If a proposal for your store reads like a shopping list of paid plugins, keep looking. And budget honestly. WooCommerce’s software is free, but quality hosting, caching that actually handles cart sessions , security, updates, and ongoing development are not. That is why the “cheaper than Shopify” argument usually collapses within the first year. Custom eCommerce: For SaaS, Subscriptions and Product Workflows Custom eCommerce is the ideal choice for anyone building a software-as-a-service or subscription-based business model around a custom product or product workflow. If the way people buy, configure, onboard, or use your product is itself the differentiator, no off-the-shelf platform is going to express it well. A lot of people balk at this option because they assume the initial cost is high and the lifetime cost is higher. For a serious business, it is the opposite: provided you find a reasonable provider, the initial investment is far outweighed by what you save later. I have seen subscription systems bolted together with WooCommerce, and it always pains me to see how much time, effort, and money went into forcing plugins to approximate what the business actually needed. The result is a patchwork that behaves like a house of cards: every plugin update is a risk, every new feature is a workaround stacked on a workaround, and nobody can safely touch it. With a custom system you build precisely what you want, which is far more maintainable and sustainable long term: No growth ceiling. When the business scales up, you are not fighting platform limits or plugin performance; the architecture was designed for your model from the start. The ideal user experience. You can create exactly the buying and onboarding flow that works best for your specific audience, not the closest approximation a theme allows. Real ownership. The system is an asset you control, not a stack of rented subscriptions that can change pricing or disappear. If you want to set yourself apart and you have a highly specific idea of what will work best for your audience, this is the road to take. The good news is that AI code generation has drastically reduced the barrier to entry; you no longer need to spend hundreds of thousands of dollars to create a real web application. I wrote more about that shift in replacing SaaS with custom software . That said, you still need a solid custom eCommerce provider , or at minimum someone who genuinely knows how to architect, scale, and maintain a web application. Throwing prompts at an AI without that background is dangerous, and it produces the same fragile mess as the worst WooCommerce builds, just faster. Which One Should You Choose? Strip away the feature charts and the decision is simpler than the industry makes it: Choose Shopify if your products are straightforward and you want a store that runs itself. Pay the fee, skip the headaches, and spend your energy on the brand. Choose WooCommerce if organic search is your growth engine or your products need real configuration logic, and only with a developer who writes lean custom code instead of stacking premium plugins. Choose custom if you are building a SaaS or subscription business, or the buying experience itself is your competitive edge. The upfront cost buys you a system that fits, scales, and belongs to you. There is no wrong platform here, only a wrong match. Pick the one that fits how your business actually makes money, and be just as careful about who builds it as what it is built on.
