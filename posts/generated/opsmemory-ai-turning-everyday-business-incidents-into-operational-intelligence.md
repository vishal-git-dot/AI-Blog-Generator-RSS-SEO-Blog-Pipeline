# OpsMemory AI: Turning Everyday Business Incidents into Operational Intelligence

## Overview

OpsMemory AI: Turning Everyday Business Incidents into Operational Intelligence Small businesses make dozens of operational decisions every day. When should a courier collect parcels? Which response actually resolves a recurring delivery problem? What should the team do differently the next time an incident occurs? These decisions often depend on experience. Yet that experience is rarely captured in a way that can be retrieved and reused. A problem gets solved, the team moves on, and the details gradually disappear into conversations, messages, and individual memory. When the same issue returns, people may repeat the same ineffective response or start investigating from scratch. OpsMemory AI explores a different approach: an operations intelligence agent that uses remembered business experience to inform future decisions. Rather than treating every operational question as a new problem, it connects incidents, actions, and recorded outcomes through persistent memory. The problem: businesses forget what they learn Imagine a small business experiencing repeated courier delays. The team knows that deliveries have been late, but knowing that a problem happened is not the same as knowing what response worked. Consider two possible actions: Remind the courier team about the existing pickup schedule. Request an earlier pickup slot. Both actions sound reasonable. But which one has supporting evidence from the business's own experience? A conventional chatbot might provide general suggestions. An operations memory agent should instead look for relevant past incidents and outcomes before recommending a next step. That distinction is the foundation of OpsMemory AI. The idea: operational memory as a decision-making resource OpsMemory AI is a lightweight application designed to help small businesses preserve operational knowledge and make it useful again. Its central principle is simple: An incident should not end when it is resolved. The experience should remain available to inform the next decision. The system brings together three important pieces of information: Incident: What happened? Action: What did the team do? Outcome: What happened after that action? These records provide context for future operational decisions. The project uses Hindsight as its persistent memory layer. Instead of relying only on the current conversation, the application can retain operational information and retrieve relevant memories later. How the system works The current prototype follows a straightforward workflow. 1. Capture an operational incident The application records an incident in a structured format. For example: Three customer deliveries were delayed because the courier failed to collect parcels on time. This creates a record of the operational problem rather than leaving it only in someone's memory. 2. Record the action and its outcome The team records what it tried and what happened afterward. In the demonstration scenario, the business requested an earlier courier pickup slot. The next day's deliveries arrived on time. This feedback matters because an action without an outcome is incomplete operational knowledge. 3. Retain and retrieve memories using Hindsight Hindsight provides the persistent memory layer. The application sends operational information to Hindsight and retrieves relevant memories when generating an evidence-based recommendation. This makes the memory system part of the application's actual workflow, rather than an optional feature or a decorative chatbot history. The prototype also exposes retrieved memories in its interface so that users can inspect the information behind a recommendation. 4. Compare recorded experience OpsMemory AI presents recorded actions and their outcomes so that users can distinguish between an action with a positive recorded result and one that did not resolve the problem. In the current demonstration, the successful earlier-pickup example is available in Hindsight-backed memory. A separate unsuccessful reminder example is stored in the application's local feedback data and displayed for comparison. This distinction is intentional: the interface should make the source of evidence clear rather than imply that every displayed record came from the same memory source. 5. Produce a cautious recommendation Based on the available evidence, the prototype identifies the earlier pickup request as the best-supported recorded action. It also communicates an important limitation: the recommendation is based on one distinct successful feedback record. It does not claim statistical certainty or prove that the action caused the improvement. For operational decision-making, showing the strength and limits of evidence is just as important as producing a recommendation. Technology behind the prototype The application combines a small set of technologies: Python: Core application logic and integration. Streamlit: Interactive user interface. Hindsight: Persistent memory retention and retrieval. Ollama with Llama 3.2 1B: Local language-model infrastructure. JSON: Structured storage for the prototype's operational records. The application is designed to run locally. This made it possible to experiment with persistent memory and language-model integration without depending on a paid hosted model API. The architecture is deliberately lightweight, making the prototype easier to inspect, run, and extend. A design decision: make memory visible One important design choice was to make retrieved memory visible to the user. AI recommendations can be difficult to trust when the application does not explain where they came from. OpsMemory AI therefore presents historical memory alongside its recommendation. A user can inspect the retrieved context and understand why a particular action is being highlighted. This also makes the prototype easier to evaluate. Rather than simply displaying a confident-sounding answer, it exposes the evidence available to the system. The goal is not to make the agent appear certain. The goal is to make its reasoning more inspectable. What I learned while building it Building a memory-driven application involves more than connecting an LLM to a database. One challenge was integrating a persistent memory service into an interactive application without making the user interface depend on fragile execution patterns. Another was handling the difference between structured application records and memories extracted by the memory system. A single operational event can be represented as several separate facts, so retrieval needs to be evaluated in context. A third challenge was avoiding exaggerated conclusions. A prototype with a small number of examples cannot establish that a recommendation will work across every business or situation. It can, however, demonstrate a useful architecture: capture experience, retain it, retrieve relevant context, and present evidence with appropriate limitations. These constraints shaped the current implementation. Current limitations and next steps OpsMemory AI is an early prototype, not a production-ready business operations platform. Its current demonstration uses a small courier-delivery dataset. The recommendation is based on limited recorded experience, and the application does not yet have enough data to measure long-term improvements in delivery performance. Future development could include: More operational categories, such as inventory, customer support, and scheduling. Larger incident and feedback datasets. Time-based analysis of recurring problems. Stronger evaluation of memory retrieval quality. User permissions and audit trails. Recommendation confidence based on the quantity and quality of supporting evidence. Integrations with the tools small businesses already use. The longer-term direction is an operational knowledge system that becomes more useful as a business accumulates experience. Final thoughts The most valuable information in a small business is not always found in a formal document. It is often hidden in the decisions people make, the problems they solve, and the outcomes they observe. OpsMemory AI is an experiment in preserving that knowledge and making it available when a similar decision appears again. The larger idea is straightforward: A business should not have to learn the same operational lesson twice simply because it forgot what happened the first time. By combining persistent memory with evidence-based recommendations, OpsMemory AI explores how AI agents can move beyond answering questions and begin helping organizations build on their own experience.
