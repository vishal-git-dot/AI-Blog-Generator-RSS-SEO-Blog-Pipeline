# The Founders Behind BAXTR and the Idea That Brought Them Together

## Overview

Great ideas often begin with an ordinary problem that people face again and again. For Gentria Lenoir, Kolice Sattiewhite, and Analise, that problem was the amount of thought, planning, and remembering that goes into meaningful relationship moments. Birthdays, gifts, dates, special plans, and everyday situations can all become stressful when someone wants to do the right thing but is not sure what would feel thoughtful or personal. That understanding became part of the idea behind BAXTR. BAXTR is not being positioned as a dating app. It is being built as an AI relationship assistant that can help users keep partner information in one place, talk through relationship situations, plan meaningful experiences, stay within a budget, and remember important moments. The goal is simple: make relationship planning more organised and personal without removing the human decision from it. A Practical Idea Built Around Real-Life Situations People often care deeply about their relationships, but that does not mean they always know what to plan. A partner may have a birthday coming up, a gift may need to be chosen, or a date may need to be arranged. Sometimes the question is even simpler: should this be a coffee, dinner, gift, or something else? BAXTR is designed to make those decisions easier by giving the assistant context. Users can create their own profile and build a profile for their partner. That partner profile can include interests, likes, preferences, and other details the user wants BAXTR to remember. When a question comes up later, the assistant can use that saved information instead of treating every situation like a new conversation. This gives each conversation a stronger sense of continuity. Why Partner Profiles Matter If BAXTR already knows that a partner enjoys certain activities, prefers particular types of places, or has specific interests, those details can help shape future suggestions. The user does not need to keep repeating the same background every time they ask for help. The same approach applies to budget and location. A user can explain how much they want to spend, and BAXTR can suggest options that fit that amount. If location information is available, it can help consider nearby places. That means a gift idea, coffee plan, dinner, or date suggestion can be shaped around the real situation instead of being generic. The Founders Bring Different Strengths to the Same Vision Gentria Lenoir brings more than 20 years of experience in youth development, community service, and program leadership. Her background reflects a people-centered approach and a history of working around practical needs, support, and challenges. As a co-founder of BAXTR, that perspective connects naturally with a product designed to make everyday relationship decisions easier to manage. Kolice Sattiewhite is a serial entrepreneur and strategist with more than a decade of experience turning human needs into practical businesses and experiences. Her work brings a strong planning and problem-solving perspective to the company. Within BAXTR, that thinking supports the idea of helping users move from wanting to do something thoughtful to having a clear plan. Analise brings a background in business, marketing, and hospitality, along with the perspective of a world traveler. Her appreciation for different cultures and memorable experiences adds to the company’s thinking. That experience fits closely with BAXTR’s focus on helping users create plans and moments that feel more considered and meaningful. Together, the three founders share one central belief: the best businesses begin by solving real problems, and BAXTR grew from that mindset. From a Reminder to a Complete Relationship Plan Another part of the BAXTR idea is remembering what matters. Users can set reminders for birthdays, dates, gifts, plans, or other relationship-related tasks. That can reduce the pressure of trying to keep every important detail in mind. A reminder can also become the starting point for a plan. If a birthday is approaching, the user can ask BAXTR for a gift idea, share a budget, and use the partner profile to make the suggestion more relevant. The same process can apply to a planned date or another important relationship moment. Making the Next Step Easier The benefit of BAXTR is not simply that it can answer questions. Its value comes from connecting partner information, chatbot support, planning, budgets, nearby options, and reminders in one place. That can mean less repeated searching, fewer random choices, and less last-minute pressure. BAXTR is launching soon, but the idea behind it is already clear. Gentria, Kolice, and Analise are building a relationship assistant around a problem: people often want to be thoughtful, but planning and remembering everything can be difficult. By helping users keep important details organised and use them when needed, BAXTR aims to make meaningful relationship planning feel simpler, more personal, and easier to act on.
