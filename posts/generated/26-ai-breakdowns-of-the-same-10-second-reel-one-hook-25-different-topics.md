# 26 AI Breakdowns of the Same 10-Second Reel: One Hook, 25 Different Topics

## Overview

I run a visa agency in Bali. The other half of my week goes into Apify Actors, and one of them takes a public Instagram Reel apart into a reusable script: the hook and why it works, the beats with what is on screen in each one, the call to action, and a fill-in template. It is not a transcript tool with a summary bolted on. The model gets frames sampled from the video — one every 2 seconds, 360 px wide, at most 12 — plus our own speech-to-text and the caption, and it answers in JSON. Which means half the output is an enum and half is prose written fresh each time. I have been quietly collecting evidence on what that costs, because like every Actor I publish, this one has a scheduled check: once a day, send one fixed Reel, make sure rows come out. Sixteen days of that check, 24 October to 8 November 2026, gave me 26 breakdowns of the same 10.4-second Reel — same model ( deepseek-flash ), same prompt, JSON mode, no temperature set, so the provider default applies. The Reel is a brow artist walking past a building: three lines of text burned into the picture, a trending song on the audio, 🔗 Link in bio to book in the caption. Each row cost $0.0506. If you are about to put LLM output into a database column, this is the field-by-field answer to "how much of it is stable". So the rule I now follow, and would hand to anyone storing model output: Diff on enums, never on prose. A scheduled watch that re-reads a creator weekly and alerts on "the breakdown changed" will fire every single week if it compares topic or scriptTemplate . Compare hook.source , hook.type , cta.type , speechIsSong , and the on-screen lines. Do not GROUP BY ai.topic . Twenty-five labels for one video is not a taxonomy. Map to your own closed list, or group by hook.type and cta.type , closed lists of 8 and 10 values respectively. Treat prose as the thing a human reads once. whyItWorks and scriptTemplate are worth the money precisely because they are written for this Reel. Store them, show them, do not join on them. If you need one canonical answer, run it once and keep it. When this is the wrong tool You need reproducible output. Everything above. Run once, store the row, treat a re-run as a second opinion. You need every burned-in line with timestamps.
