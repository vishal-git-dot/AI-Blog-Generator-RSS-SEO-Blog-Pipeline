# Your Customer Wants to Buy From You. Are You Making It Difficult?

## Overview

A customer finds your business. They like what you offer. They're ready to buy. Then they start looking for basic information. How much is it? Where are you located? How do I order? Do you deliver? How do I book? They send a WhatsApp message. No response. They check Instagram. The information isn't there. They scroll through 80 posts looking for the price. Nothing. They ask: “How can I place an order?” And you reply: “Send me a DM.” At this point, the customer is already tired. 😂 And here's the thing: They may still want your product. But wanting something and actually buying it are two different things. Every extra step you put between a customer and a purchase creates another opportunity for them to give up. Think about how you behave as a customer. If you want to order something online and the process is confusing, what do you normally do? You leave. You find another seller. Simple. Your customers are no different. That's why convenience has become such an important part of doing business online. A professional website can make the process much easier. Instead of making customers ask basic questions one by one, you can give them a clear place to find: Your products or services. Prices or pricing information. Location. Opening hours. Photos. Frequently asked questions. Contact details. Booking or ordering options. Now the customer doesn't have to go hunting for information. They can understand your business quickly and decide what to do next. And that matters because customers don't only compare prices anymore. They compare experiences. If Business A makes it easy to understand, contact and buy from them while Business B makes the customer chase information through WhatsApp messages... Guess which one feels easier? Exactly. Your business might have the better product. You might even have the better price. But if buying from you feels unnecessarily difficult, the customer may choose the easier option. So before asking: “Why aren't customers buying?” Maybe ask: “How easy have I made it for them to buy?” Because sometimes, the problem isn't that customers don't want what you're selling.
