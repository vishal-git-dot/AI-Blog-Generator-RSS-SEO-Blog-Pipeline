# Will AI Replace Entry-Level PR Jobs? What Juniors Should Do Now

## Overview

AI won't remove entry-level PR jobs, but it is removing many of the tasks those jobs were built around: building media lists, clipping coverage, first-draft releases and monthly report formatting. The junior roles that survive will be smaller in number and different in shape, with less volume work and earlier exposure to judgment, writing and reporter contact. If you're starting out, the useful move is to practise the parts of the job a tool can't do, deliberately and early. I'm writing this for juniors, and for the people who hire and train them. Neither group benefits from panic. Which junior PR tasks is AI actually taking over? Be specific, because "AI is taking jobs" is too vague to act on. Most junior PR roles were a bundle of tasks, and the bundle is coming apart. Every remaining task is a judgment call. That's the new entry-level job. What does that mean for the number of junior roles? Honestly, some agencies will hire fewer juniors, because one person with good tools can cover what used to take two or three. I won't pretend otherwise. If your plan was to spend two years doing nothing but media lists and clipping, that path is narrowing. But two things push the other way. First, every company now produces more content, and someone has to check it, which needs people who understand media. Second, the senior people everyone wants to hire had to start somewhere. An industry that stops training juniors runs out of seniors. The firms that think long term will keep hiring juniors and give them better work earlier. For more on why the senior end of comms is getting more valuable, not less, see my field note on whether AI will replace PR . How did juniors learn the job before, and what's lost? The old system had a hidden benefit. Building a media list by hand taught you who covers what. Clipping coverage every morning taught you which stories ran and why. Drafting twenty pitches that got rewritten taught you what a good one looks like. That's the real risk with AI: not that it takes the tasks, but that it takes the learning that came with them. A junior who has never read a reporter's articles, because a tool summarised them, won't develop the instinct that makes senior people valuable. The Weekly Reps Plan for junior PR people This is the practice routine I'd give anyone in their first two years. It replaces the learning the old grunt work used to provide, on purpose. Read five stories a week from one reporter on your beat, fully. Not summaries. Note what they lead with, who they quote and what they never write about. Write one pitch a week without AI, then compare it to an AI draft. Find what yours has that the AI version lacks. Usually it's a specific detail or a reason why now. Fact-check one AI draft line by line. Find the source for every claim. You'll quickly learn where models invent things, which is a skill teams now need. Answer every journalist request you're given within the hour. Speed and reliability are how reporters start to trust a name they don't know. Sit in on one interview or prep call. Ask to listen. Notice how the senior person steers the conversation. Write one short opinion piece a month under your own name. A LinkedIn post, a newsletter, a guest blog. Writing in your own voice is becoming rarer and more valuable. Ask one senior colleague "why did you do it that way" each week. The reasoning is what you can't get from a tool. What should managers and agencies do differently? If you hire juniors, the old training model doesn't work anymore. You can't rely on volume work to teach them, because the tools do it. So build the learning in deliberately: Old way juniors learned New way to teach the same thing Building lists by hand Have them audit an AI-built list and explain each cut Drafting endless pitches Have them rewrite AI drafts and defend each change Clipping coverage daily Have them write a weekly "what this coverage means" note Shadowing occasionally Bring them into prep calls and debriefs as standard Formatting reports Have them present one insight from the report to the client This also makes juniors useful faster. Someone who can check AI output, spot a weak claim and explain coverage to a client is worth more than someone who can only produce lists. If you're a founder with a junior marketer and no senior PR person to learn from, pairing them with a fractional operator is one way to get both the work and the training. It's the kind of engagement I offer through AI startup PR . Where is this advice wrong for you? A few honest exceptions: In very small teams or solo setups, there may be no junior role at all. Your path in might be freelancing, content writing or a role adjacent to PR, like community or social, and moving across. In some regions and languages, AI tools are weaker, and native-language media work still relies heavily on people. Juniors with strong regional language skills have an edge there. In highly regulated sectors, the review and compliance side grows, and juniors may spend more time on accuracy checks than on pitching. That's still valuable training. And if you love writing but hate pitching, PR may not be the only home for you. Content and editorial roles are changing too, but they reward the same original writing skill. FAQ Should juniors avoid using AI tools so they learn properly? No. Use them, because every employer will expect you to. Just don't let them replace the reps that build judgment. Draft with AI, but also write some things from scratch, and always read the source material yourself before trusting a summary. Is PR still a good career to start in 2026? For people who like writing, people and fast decisions, yes. The work is moving toward strategy and relationships earlier in a career, which is the more interesting part. It's a weaker choice if what you wanted was predictable, repetitive work. What should I put in a junior PR portfolio now? Show judgment, not volume. Include a pitch you wrote and why you targeted that reporter, an AI draft you corrected with your edits marked, and a short piece of original writing under your name. One placement you helped land, with your role explained honestly, beats ten you can't explain. Will AI replace PR interns before it replaces junior staff? Intern tasks are the most exposed, because they're often pure volume work. Good internship programmes will shift toward observation, writing practice and fact-checking.
