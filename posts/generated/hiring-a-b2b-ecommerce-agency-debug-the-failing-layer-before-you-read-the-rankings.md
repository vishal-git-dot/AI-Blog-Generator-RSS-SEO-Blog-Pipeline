# Hiring a B2B Ecommerce Agency? Debug the Failing Layer Before You Read the Rankings

## Overview

Most B2B ecommerce projects that go sideways don't fail because of bad code. They fail because the team hired to fix the system was built to fix a different layer of it. A storefront agency gets brought in to solve an ERP sync problem. A marketing shop is asked to untangle contract pricing. An enterprise transformation firm is handed a focused marketplace MVP and spends the first quarter on governance decks. If you are the engineer, tech lead or technical founder asked to "help pick an agency," this post is for you. Instead of a beauty contest, treat the decision like scoping a systems integration: figure out which layer is broken, then hire for that layer. Why B2B commerce is a different beast A consumer storefront mostly needs a catalog, a cart, a checkout and decent page speed. A B2B buyer expects much more from the system: accurate, account-specific pricing (often contract pricing) real-time inventory they can trust before committing a purchase order quote and RFQ workflows purchase approvals and account permissions inside the buyer's company fast repeat ordering integration with the systems that actually run the business: ERP, CRM, PIM, inventory, accounting Every one of those requirements is a data flow, not a page template. That is why an agency with a beautiful retail portfolio can still be the wrong pick. The useful question is not "are they good?" but "are they good at the layer where my problem lives?" Step 1: Locate the failing layer Before you open a single agency website, write down which of these layers is actually hurting. Be honest; most teams have one primary problem and two secondary ones. [ Growth layer ] demand gen, SEO, paid, CRM attribution, account revenue [ Experience layer ] UX, checkout usability, conversion, buyer portal [ Platform layer ] Adobe Commerce / Magento, Shopify Plus, BigCommerce, Shopware... [ Integration layer] ERP <-> storefront <-> CRM <-> inventory <-> EDI [ Model layer ] marketplace mechanics: multi-vendor, vendor dashboards, net terms A quick diagnostic: Orders arrive but stock, pricing or order status are wrong → integration layer. The platform is slow, unstable or due for a migration → platform layer. Traffic is fine but buyers abandon or call sales instead → experience layer. Marketing reports clicks while sales can't see account revenue → growth layer. You are launching a multi-vendor or B2B marketplace from scratch → model layer, and probably growth too. Step 3: Run a technical interview, not a sales call Once you have two or three candidates per layer, interview them like you would a senior hire. Here is a checklist you can paste into your evaluation doc. Integration questions [ ] Which ERPs have you integrated in production, and what was the sync model (event-driven, scheduled batch, middleware)? [ ] How do you handle pricing as the source of truth: ERP, PIM or the commerce platform? [ ] What happens when the ERP is down during checkout? [ ] Can you show an integration runbook or monitoring setup from a past project? Platform questions [ ] Why this platform for our catalog size and pricing complexity, and what would make you recommend a different one? [ ] How do you approach migrations without losing organic traffic (redirect maps, technical SEO checks)? [ ] Who owns the codebase and infrastructure after launch? B2B workflow questions [ ] Walk us through company accounts, roles and approval chains in a project you shipped. [ ] How do you model net terms, quotes and repeat orders? [ ] How do buyers with negotiated pricing see their catalog? Growth questions [ ] What metrics will you report: traffic, or pipeline, CAC, retention and account profitability? [ ] How does campaign data connect to CRM and order data? If an agency struggles with the questions for the layer you're hiring them for, that's your answer. If they shine on a layer you don't need, that's interesting, but it shouldn't move the decision. Step 4: Watch for mismatch anti-patterns The source calls out three combinations worth avoiding, and they're easy to fall into: A design-heavy agency when ERP data is broken. You get a gorgeous portal that shows wrong prices faster. An integration platform for a demand-generation problem. Your data flows perfectly to an audience that never arrives. An enterprise transformation program for a focused marketplace launch. Months of process before a single vendor is onboarded. A fourth from experience: hiring generalists for a marketplace. Multi-vendor B2B has its own mechanics, such as vendor onboarding, vendor dashboards, custom pricing per company account, net terms, procurement workflows and catalog management across sellers. Those belong to the model layer, and it pays to hire someone who has built them before. Where a marketplace specialist fits If your project sits in the model layer, the profile of Journeyhorizon in the source covers B2B marketplace development, Sharetribe customization and migration, multi-vendor web and mobile templates, ERP/CRM/inventory integrations, and the growth side: vendor acquisition, SEO, content, LinkedIn ads and reporting. The B2B feature set listed includes company accounts, custom pricing, net terms, advanced catalog management, procurement workflows and vendor dashboards. If that matches your scope, their B2B multi-vendor marketplace development service is the relevant starting point. Location: mostly noise The FAQ in the source tackles searches like "B2B ecommerce agency Boston" or "Austin." The short version: proximity can make collaboration easier, but B2B workflow knowledge, integration capability and platform experience matter more. A remote specialist on your exact stack usually beats a local generalist. Takeaway Treat agency selection like any other architecture decision: Identify the failing layer. Map it to the right archetype. Interview with layer-specific technical questions. Reject mismatches early, even if the portfolio is impressive. Do that, and the "top agencies" list stops being a ranking and becomes what it should be: a well-labeled parts bin. Pick the part that fits the socket you actually have.
