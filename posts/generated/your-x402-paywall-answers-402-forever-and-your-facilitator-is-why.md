# Your x402 paywall answers 402 forever, and your facilitator is why

## Overview

A paid endpoint that never sells anything usually gets blamed on traffic. Sometimes the rail is broken in a way that looks perfectly healthy from the outside: the 402 is well-formed, the crawlers index it, /supported lists your network, and every payment attempt dies inside the facilitator before it reaches the chain. I hit this on my own service last night and then tested the public facilitators from the same machine, with the same wallet and the same signed payload. The results are the point of this post. The failure mode x402 has a two-step handshake with a facilitator: GET /supported — which schemes and networks will you settle for? POST /verify — here is a signed authorization, is it payable? Most integrations only ever run step 1. That is a mistake, because step 1 is a claim and step 2 is the check, and a facilitator can pass the first and fail the second for every valid payment : GET https://facilitator.example/supported -> 200, lists eip155:8453 POST https://facilitator.example/verify -> 500 unexpected_error Nothing in your own logs looks wrong. You emit 402, the client signs, the client posts, the facilitator throws, your middleware turns that into another 402 or a 500, and the buyer leaves. The service looks up, the revenue is zero, and there is no error you can grep for because the error belongs to someone else. A 200 with isValid: true is the only answer that means anything. The other half: prove settlement on-chain, not in a header X-PAYMENT-RESPONSE: {"status":"settled"} is the facilitator's word. The balance is the chain's. Diff the payer and the payee around a paid call: before: balanceOf(payer) - balanceOf(payTo) call: GET /paid with X-PAYMENT after: balanceOf(payer) - balanceOf(payTo) # must move by exactly the price I keep a small watcher that does this for every wallet I care about and stays silent unless a balance moves. It has caught more truth in one line of output than any dashboard: when the facilitator said "settled" and nothing moved, it said nothing at all. Two traps in the 402 body itself While a reviewer was replaying my proofs (fair play, and worth doing), they found two defects in the response body that no facilitator would ever tell me about: 1. Legacy top-level fields describing a different rail. The body carried maxAmountRequired , payTo and network from the Stacks configuration while accepts[0] was Base USDC. A v1 client that reads the top level — which is exactly what a lot of clients do — would try to pay STX for a Base-priced call. Fix: make the top level mirror accepts[0] , and keep the old shape in a clearly named legacyStacks object so nothing breaks. 2. A unit lie. One rail reported amount_usdc: "0.001000" , computed by dividing micro-STX by a million and labelling the result dollars. The other rail was 0.01 USDC. Same call, a 10x price gap that existed only in the label. Fix: report the amount in the asset it is actually denominated in ( amount_in_asset: "0.001000 STX" ). Both are the kind of bug you cannot see from your own side, because your own client reads the field you wrote and agrees with you. What I would check before trusting any paid endpoint /supported is a claim. Send a real signed payload to /verify and read isValid . Send the same payload to a second facilitator. Disagreement is information. Settle once and prove it by balance delta, not by response header. Read your own 402 body as a stranger: which rail does the top level describe, and does every amount say what asset it is in. Keep the probe running on a schedule. Facilitators change under you.
