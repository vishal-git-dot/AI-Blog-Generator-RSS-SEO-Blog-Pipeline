# SMS Marketing Compliance Guide for AI-Powered Text Campaigns

## Overview

Why AI-Powered SMS Still Requires Valid Consent Artificial intelligence can personalize copy, predict engagement, and automate campaign timing, but it does not remove an organization’s obligations under the Telephone Consumer Protection Act (TCPA). Businesses remain responsible for how telephone numbers are collected, which messages are sent, and whether recipients have provided the appropriate consent. Marketing texts generally require a clear opt-in before transmission. Depending on the campaign and technology involved, prior express written consent may be necessary. The request should explain that the subscriber agrees to receive recurring automated marketing messages, identify the sender, disclose that consent is not a condition of purchase, and state that message and data rates may apply. Avoid hiding these terms inside a general privacy policy or unrelated registration agreement. The consent language should appear next to the phone-number field and use an unchecked checkbox or another affirmative action. Preselected boxes, purchased lists, and inferred permission create unnecessary compliance risk. Because federal interpretations and state requirements can change, businesses should have qualified counsel review their specific consent flows. AI platforms can enforce approved rules, but they should not be treated as substitutes for legal guidance. Build a Verifiable Opt-In and Audit Trail A compliant campaign needs more than a telephone number in a customer database. Each subscriber record should include the consent timestamp, disclosure version, source page, campaign purpose, and relevant technical evidence, such as the form URL or session record. Versioning is especially important when opt-in language changes. A double opt-in workflow can strengthen the record. After submitting a form, the user receives a confirmation text and must respond before promotional messaging begins. Although this additional step is not mandatory for every campaign, it helps verify number ownership and reduce accidental or fraudulent subscriptions. Make Opt-Out Processing Immediate and Reliable Every marketing text should provide a straightforward method to stop future messages. Common replies include STOP, END, CANCEL, UNSUBSCRIBE, and QUIT. Systems should recognize capitalization differences, surrounding punctuation, and ordinary-language requests that clearly communicate withdrawal of consent. Once a recipient opts out, the platform should suppress further marketing messages across connected workflows. A single confirmation acknowledging the request may be appropriate, but it should not contain promotional content. Suppression records should be durable so that later imports, model recommendations, or audience synchronizations do not accidentally reactivate the number. Organizations also need controls for quiet hours, frequency limits, reassigned numbers, internal do-not-contact lists, and applicable regional rules. AI optimization should never override these deterministic safeguards. Govern AI Content Before It Reaches Subscribers Generative models can produce unsupported claims, misleading urgency, or language that exceeds the scope of the original opt-in. Reduce this risk with approved prompts, prohibited-claim filters, human review for sensitive campaigns, and locked compliance disclosures. Teams should test complete journeys—not just individual messages—and retain logs showing the model version, final text, audience criteria, approval status, and send time. Regular audits can then identify consent gaps, delayed opt-outs, and unauthorized template changes before they become systemic problems. A compliance-first design makes AI-powered SMS more sustainable.
