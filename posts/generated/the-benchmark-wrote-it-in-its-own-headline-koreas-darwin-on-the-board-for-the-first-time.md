# The benchmark wrote it in its own headline: Korea's Darwin on the board for the first time

## Overview

Read the headline, not just the score Benchmark news is usually read through the score. This one should be read through the headline. At 19:53 on 4 November 2026, SuperCLUE published its November monthly evaluation. The author is the SuperCLUE team and the channel is the official CLUE Chinese Language Understanding Evaluation account. Its headline reads: "November 2026 Chinese large model benchmark results released! Kimi-K3.1-Pro #1 in China, Korean model Darwin on the board for the first time! " That is a translation of the Chinese original, which closes with the phrase for "on the board for the first time". There were two slots in that headline. One went to China's top-scoring model. The other came to us. Out of 19 entrants, the party that chose which model to name in the headline was the party that did the scoring. This was not a press release we sent, and not our summary of someone else's table. The evaluator put our model's name in its own headline. The scoring design the announcement disclosed The announcement published this round's weights as numbers, which shows exactly which capability it valued. Task Weight Agentic coding 30% Math and science reasoning 24% Hallucination control 18% Precise instruction following 18% Ultra-long context 10% This round put the most weight on agentic coding, with math and science reasoning second. In the announcement's own words, that axis "emphasises logical derivation and quantitative analysis". All 19 models sat the same private exam under the same weights. What happened on the hardest axis The announcement reports the math and science reasoning result like this: "GPT-6.1-Sol leads the math and science reasoning board with 76.32, Darwin-180B-RSI-R3 (73.68) takes second , and DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-1017 and Gemini-4-Argon tie for third at 71.05" Second of 19. The gap to first is 2.64 points. Below us on that axis sit Gemini-4-Argon, DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash, Grok-4.7 and Qwen3.8-Max-0902. Among open-weight models it is first. Darwin-180B-RSI-R3 is the only Korean model on the board. The footnote written only for Darwin One more line in the announcement applies to our model alone. It appears in the cost-efficiency section. "*Darwin-180B-RSI-R3 is an open-source deployed model, so API pricing and cost are not tallied. The open-source address is available at https://huggingface.co/FINAL-Bench/Darwin-180B-RSI-R3 " Two things follow from that footnote. First, the other leading models on the board have their cost efficiency computed from API pricing, and ours is not in that calculation at all. A model you pay per call to use and a model whose weights you download and run on your own hardware sit on entirely different cost axes. Second, the evaluator linked our repository directly in the body of its own announcement, so a reader who sees the score can fetch the weights immediately. It shares the board with its parent model Darwin-180B-RSI is a 180 billion parameter mixture-of-experts model with roughly 3 billion active per token. It inherited its 512 routed experts per layer from Alibaba's Qwen3.8-Flash-Next byte for byte and rebuilt only the attention layers, the linear attention and the shared experts through recursive self-improvement: the model writes problems, solves them, verifies the answers and updates its own weights from the verified traces. Both models are on this board, so the comparison is direct. Qwen3.8-Flash Darwin-180B-RSI-R3 Math and science reasoning 57.89 73.68 Overall index 39.81 (15th) 43.08 (9th) Same stored knowledge, rebuilt reasoning path: 15.79 points on the hardest axis and six places on the overall index. This announcement is the source those pieces drew from. It is the primary document, written by the party that produced the numbers, and the choice of headline was theirs too. Us saying "first among open-weight models" and the evaluator writing "Korean model Darwin on the board for the first time" are the same fact carrying different weight.
