# What to include in a course sales page to increase conversions

## Overview

What to Include in a Course Sales Page to Increase Conversions You've built the course. You've recorded the lessons, uploaded the modules, and set up the checkout. Now you're staring at a blank sales page wondering why the words won't come—and why, when they do, visitors still aren't buying. The gap between "good course" and "course that sells" is almost always the sales page. And the good news is that a high-converting sales page isn't about clever writing. It's about structure. When you know what to include in a course sales page to increase conversions, you stop guessing and start building a page that does the selling for you. Here's a practical, section-by-section breakdown you can apply today. 1. A Headline That Names the Transformation Your headline has one job: make the right person feel seen in under five seconds. Weak headlines describe the course ("An 8-Week Course on Freelance Writing"). Strong headlines describe the outcome ("Go From Zero Clients to a Waitlist in 8 Weeks—Without Cold Pitching"). A simple formula that works: [End result] + [timeframe or method] + [for whom] Test it against this question: Would my ideal student read this and think "that's me"? If not, rewrite it. Avoid vague words like "mastery," "journey," and "unlock your potential"—they say nothing specific. 2. A Subheadline That Kills the Main Objection The subheadline is where you address the biggest reason people hesitate. Common objections: "I don't have time." "I've tried courses before and never finished." "I'm not technical enough." Example subheadline: "Built for busy parents—short lessons, lifetime access, and a template for every step." 3. The "Who This Is For" Block Conversions rise when people can quickly self-identify. Write two short lists: This is for you if… (3–5 bullets describing your ideal student's situation) This is NOT for you if… (2–3 bullets that filter out mismatches) The second list builds trust. It signals you're not trying to sell to everyone—which makes the right buyer more confident. 4. The Problem → Agitation → Solution Sequence Before you present the course, walk the reader through their current pain. Use concrete language: Problem: "You've got a finished course but no idea how to write the sales page." Agitation: "Every day it sits unpublished is a day you're not getting paid—and the longer you wait, the harder it feels to start." Solution: "Here's a proven structure you can fill in, section by section." This is the emotional core of the page. Skip it and your course feels like a feature list instead of a fix. 5. A Clear Course Overview (What's Inside) Buyers want to know exactly what they're getting. Break it down: Module-by-module list with a one-line benefit for each Total length and format (video, audio, PDF, live calls) Bonuses and their standalone value Access details (lifetime? drip? community?) Keep each module description focused on the result , not the topic . "Module 3: Write Emails That Get Clicks" beats "Module 3: Email Marketing." 6. Instructor Credibility You don't need to be famous. You need to be relevant . Include: A short bio focused on why you're qualified to teach this specific topic 1–2 concrete results or case studies A photo (real faces convert better than logos) If you have testimonials from beta students, put them here. 7. Social Proof: Testimonials and Results Place testimonials near the claims they support , not just in one lump at the bottom. If you claim "you'll finish in 4 weeks," put a student quote about finishing right next to it. Best testimonial format: Result + specific detail + name/photo Example: "I wrote my entire sales page in one afternoon using the module 2 framework. Launched to $2,400 in the first week." — Sarah M. No testimonials yet? Use beta feedback, screenshots of student messages, or your own before/after story. 8. Pricing That Justifies Itself Don't just drop a number. Frame it: Anchor the value: What would it cost to hire someone to do this? What's the cost of staying stuck? Show the price clearly with what's included Offer a payment plan if the price is over $100 State your guarantee (refund window, conditions) A simple line like "30-day money-back guarantee—if you do the work and don't get results, email us for a full refund" removes enormous friction. 9. A Strong, Repeated CTA Your call-to-action should appear at least 3 times on the page: After the headline/hero section After the "what's inside" section After the guarantee/FAQ Use action-based button text: "Enroll Now," "Get Instant Access," "Start Today." Avoid "Learn More" or "Submit." And make sure the button stands out visually. 10. An FAQ That Handles Last-Minute Doubts The FAQ is where you close the sale for fence-sitters. Answer the real questions: How long do I have access? What if I'm a beginner? Is there a refund policy? How much time do I need per week? Do I get support? Write the answers as if you're talking to a friend—direct, honest, no fluff. 11. A Final "Two Futures" Close End the page with a short paragraph contrasting two outcomes: Future A: Nothing changes. The course stays unfinished, the sales page stays unwritten. Future B: You follow the structure, publish the page, and start enrolling students. Then place your final CTA button. This is the last emotional push—keep it warm, not pushy. A Quick Pre-Launch Checklist Before you publish, run through this: [ ] Headline names a specific outcome [ ] Subheadline addresses the top objection [ ] "Who it's for / not for" lists are clear [ ] Problem → Agitation → Solution is present [ ] Every module has a benefit, not just a topic [ ] At least 3 testimonials or proof points [ ] Pricing is framed with value and a guarantee [ ] CTA appears 3+ times with action verbs [ ] FAQ answers the 5 most common objections [ ] Page ends with a "two futures" close When the Writing Itself Is the Bottleneck Knowing what to include in a course sales page to increase conversions is one thing.
