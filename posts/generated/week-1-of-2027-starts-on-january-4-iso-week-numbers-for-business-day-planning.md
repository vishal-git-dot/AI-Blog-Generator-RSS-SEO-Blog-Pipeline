# Week 1 of 2027 Starts on January 4: ISO Week Numbers for Business-Day Planning

## Overview

I used to treat week numbers as a solved problem. Store a year and a week, render a planner, ship. Then a deadline labeled "week 1 of 2027" and a deadline labeled "the first week of January" landed on two ranges with no days in common. The gap is ISO week numbering. It's the system Python's date.isocalendar() implements, the one Excel documents as System 2 / ISOWEEKNUM , and it does not follow the calendar year the way a lot of US-style WEEKNUM defaults do. If you plan work in week buckets, that difference shows up right at the year boundary. What an ISO week actually is The current standard is ISO 8601-1:2019 (confirmed in 2024; the full text is paywalled). I am relying here on two public restatements I opened today, which agree with each other: Microsoft's WEEKNUM help calls this System 2: "The week containing the first Thursday of the year is the first week of the year, and is numbered as week 1. This system is the methodology specified in ISO 8601." return_type 21 selects it. ISOWEEKNUM is the dedicated function for the same week number. Python's date.isocalendar() docs say an ISO year "consists of 52 or 53 full weeks," a week "starts on a Monday and ends on a Sunday," and "the first week of an ISO year is the first (Gregorian) calendar week of a year containing a Thursday." Two meanings of "week 1 of 2027" Excel's default WEEKNUM is System 1: "The week containing January 1 is the first week of the year," and the default week begins on Sunday. I did not run Excel. Applying that documented rule to the calendar gives: System 1, week 1 of 2027: Sunday, December 27, 2026 through Saturday, January 2, 2027. ISO week 1 of 2027: Monday, January 4 through Sunday, January 10, 2027. Those ranges do not overlap. January 3, 2027 is in System 1 week 2, and it is still ISO week 53 of 2026. The YEAR() + ISOWEEKNUM() bug A second trap is combining a calendar year with an ISO week number. For January 1, 2027, ISOWEEKNUM should return 53 (same week number as isocalendar() ), and YEAR returns 2027. Stored together, that becomes the key (2027, 53) . That key is invalid: 2027 has only 52 ISO weeks. The ISO year for that Friday is 2026. The safer options I use: Store the ISO year and the ISO week together, not calendar_year + iso_week . Or skip week numbers as a primary key and store the Monday of the week as a date. What I now do with this If a plan is week-based, I write down the numbering system in the same place as the week number. "ISO 2027-W01" and "the calendar week that contains 2027-01-01" are different instructions. I never store year=2027, week=53 from a calendar date in January. I store the ISO year, or I store a Monday. I don't special-case week 53 away. In 2026 it is a real, seven-day week that happens to straddle New Year's Day. When I need to count business days inside that week, I start from the Monday and Sunday I already resolved, then apply the weekend and holiday rules for that workplace. The week number itself does not know those rules.
