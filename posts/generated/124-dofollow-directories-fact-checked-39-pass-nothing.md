Don't trust any list, this one included. The snippet below checks a live listing in 10 seconds. What "dofollow" actually means There is no rel="dofollow" . A link is followed when its rel attribute is empty, or holds only browser flags like noopener and noreferrer . Three values switch it off. rel value Meant for Followed? empty, noopener , noreferrer Normal links Yes nofollow Links the site doesn't vouch for No ugc Comments, forum posts, user profiles No sponsored Ads and paid placements No Google says links marked nofollow , ugc or sponsored "will generally not be followed" . Ahrefs is blunter: only followed links increase DR , and only the first one from each domain. A clean rel can still hide a dead link: A redirect. The button points to the directory's own short link, which forwards to you. If that hop is nofollow or noindex , like Dang.ai's, nothing reaches you. A plain 301 may pass value, but no page check can prove it. A JavaScript button. No <a href> , nothing for a crawler to follow. A page-level block. A noindex listing page drops out of Google, which eventually stops following its links . A nofollow robots meta tag switches off every link on the page at once. What directories won't do for you They split their value. Ahrefs passes each linking domain's DR divided by the number of unique domains it links to . Its own example: a DR 26 site linking to five sites can move your DR more than a DR 73 site linking to 5,000 . A directory with thousands of listings is that DR 73 site. Google is not Ahrefs. Google's spam policies list low-quality directory links, and paid links that pass ranking credit, as link spam. A paid dofollow listing will count in your DR. Google may simply ignore it. Directories get you off zero, and a few dozen clean links will do it. After that, what moves a site is stuff people link to because they want to, like data nobody else has published.
