# Six call-list recipes with pay-per-found phone lookups on Apify: 10 accounts, 24 decision makers, 12 mobile numbers

## Overview

Email is the default channel of outbound because it scales, not because it wins every conversation. There is a class of conversations where a call is the right tool: the decision is this week, the person is senior enough that their inbox is triaged by someone else, the topic is confidential, or three emails have already gone into silence. For those, a mobile number is worth more than another sequence step. The reason most teams do not call is not philosophy. They do not have the number, and the tools that have it sell it as a seat with a credit allowance sized for a different job. A reader of this series pointed me to a cost-per-contact comparison where one popular tool's 75 monthly credits buy about 8 contacts with phones. That is the shape of the problem: you pay for the seat, then for the reveal, then for the misses. This post is the call-list half of the series. Six situations where a number earns its keep, which lookup to run for each, what my runs on 2026-10-14 returned, and the rules before anyone dials. Disclosure: I build the b2bsearch Actors on Apify used here. They are metered per row that resolves; a person without a number on record is a free row that says so. The numbers are mobile numbers that were verified when they were collected, mostly US, read from a database of professional profile records. Nothing is scraped during a run and there is no LinkedIn login involved. What to expect before you spend Phone coverage is a function of seniority and geography, not of the lookup key. Rules before you dial Phone data carries rules that email does not, and the rules are stricter for automation than for a person dialling. These are mobile numbers. In the US the TCPA protects mobiles: no autodialers, no pre-recorded messages, no SMS without prior express consent, and several states add calling-hour and consent rules of their own. A human dialling one number at a time is the baseline; anything automated needs counsel and consent. Check the Do Not Call registry where your use is covered, honour internal do-not-call requests at once, and feed the suppression list back into the workflow. Outside the US the coverage is near zero and the rules differ; this is a US tool for a US motion. Say who you are and why you are calling in the first sentence. The signal that put the person on the list is the honest reason and a better opener than a script.
