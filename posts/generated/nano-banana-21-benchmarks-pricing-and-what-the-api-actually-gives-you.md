# Nano Banana 2.1: benchmarks, pricing, and what the API actually gives you

## Overview

Google shipped Nano Banana 2.1 on October 6, 2026. Model ID is gemini-nano-banana-2.1 , it's built on Gemini 3.6 Flash, and it slots in as the efficiency-tier sibling to Nano Banana Pro — Pro-adjacent output quality at Flash latency, at roughly half the per-image cost of Nano Banana 2. I've been running image pipelines through Gemini models long enough to be cynical about "better quality" announcements. This one has numbers attached and a deprecation date attached, which is the part that matters for anyone with production code. The numbers that decide anything Price : ~$0.0336 per 1K image. Nano Banana 2 was ~$0.067. Batch discounts apply. Output tiers : 1K (default), 2K, 4K — including fixed extreme aspect ratios. Context : developer docs list 131,072 input tokens; some model descriptions cite up to 1M. Text output up to 64K tokens in model card descriptions. Availability at launch : Gemini app, AI Mode in Google Search, Google AI Studio, Google Flow, Stitch, Google Ads, Gemini Enterprise, and the Gemini API. Deprecation : Nano Banana 2 was marked for shutdown the same day, scheduled for October 29, 2026 . If you're still pinned to it, that's your deadline. What's genuinely different Visual quality and extreme aspect ratios 2.1 renders cleaner across all three resolution tiers — lighting, composition, and texture detail all improve in side-by-side preference evals. The concrete fix I care about: the 1:4, 4:1, 1:8, and 8:1 ratios no longer tile-artifact at higher resolutions the way Nano Banana 2 did. Panoramic banners and ultra-tall social crops are usable now instead of being a post-processing problem. Text and infographic rendering This is the upgrade that changes workflows. Infographic factuality went from 0.179 on Nano Banana 2 to 0.521 in Thinking mode — a big jump, driven by Gemini's world knowledge plus optional Search grounding. Menus, charts, diagrams, and labeled mockups come out readable far more often. It also handles localization conversationally: ask for an English product diagram, then ask for the Spanish version with the same visual structure. That is not a replacement for a design system. Check spelling, legal copy, prices, and anything small at final output size. Reference consistency Up to 14 reference images in one request, with documented support for four characters and ten objects . That covers product shots from multiple angles, recurring cast in a storyboard, a fashion concept locking model + garment + accessories, or a room redesign that keeps the furniture while changing lighting. The gotcha: "combine these images" is a bad prompt. Label your references and assign roles — "Image 1 is the hero product. Images 2–4 define the model's face. Images 5–7 define packaging details." Then change one thing per turn. Thinking levels and multi-turn editing Three levels: minimal , medium (default), high . Thinking buys internal reasoning steps before the image is produced, which helps on dense instructions and hard compositions. The API continues an interaction via a previous interaction ID, so iterative editing is a real workflow, not a re-roll: generate, then move a subject, change signage language, drop an object, or swap the background while preserving the character. Use minimal for rapid variations and high only where the composition demands it — measure latency and acceptance rate on your own workload rather than maxing the setting by reflex. Prompting patterns that hold up Write a brief, not keywords. Subject, goal, audience, composition, lighting, text, output format. State what may change and what may not. Separate identity from style. "Keep the bottle label and cap exactly as reference 1; use the warm editorial lighting from reference 2" beats "combine these images." One revision per turn. "Keep subject, camera angle, and typography. Replace only the background with a pale blue studio wall." Failures stay diagnosable. Verify text and facts. Zoom in on typography, check names, dates, units, prices, and translated copy. If the asset is Search-grounded, archive the sources for editorial review. Where it still fails Small or dense text blurs at lower resolutions. Left/right spatial confusion shows up occasionally. Character consistency isn't perfect in edge cases, and advanced 3D or world-knowledge reasoning has limits. Knowledge cutoff tracks the Gemini 3.6 Flash base — around March 2026 in some domains. Search grounding improves access to current information; it doesn't make an image model a fact-checking system. Outputs carry SynthID watermarking. Migration note If Nano Banana 2 is in your pipeline, October 29, 2026 is the cutoff.
