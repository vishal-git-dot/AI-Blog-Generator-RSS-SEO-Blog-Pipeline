# Half of stack migrations are now written by AI agents. Here's where they're moving apps.

## Overview

Two years ago, half of the products I track sent their emails through SendGrid. But for the ones that launched this year, it droped to 1/8. Resend went the other way, from 41% to 82%. It doesn't surprise me, though. What surprises me the most is: when I sampled recent GitHub pull requests titled "Firebase to Supabase", "Vercel to Cloudflare" and so on, half of them were opened by a coding agent. A year and a half ago it was almost zero. Some context first. For a while now I've been cataloguing what shipped products actually run on. It's at 7,302 products, and every "uses X" links to where it was found: a script on the live site, a DNS record, a dependency in a public repo, or the maker saying it themselves. It's all on howmakersbuild.com if you want to check my work. Most of this post comes out of that data, plus a GitHub crawl. What new products pick The trap with "most popular tool" lists is that old tools win by default. They've had more years to collect users. So I split products by when they launched (on Product Hunt) and compared the share of each tool among products launched in 2026 with the share among products launched in 2024 or earlier. Everything else is within a few points. So "AI is picking your stack" is only half true. Everyone launching in 2026 picks roughly the same stack, whether or not an agent wrote the code. My guess is the same tutorials, the same templates, and the same AI answers feed both groups. Nobody gets their defaults from nowhere. Ryan Carniato wrote a good piece about this a couple of months ago, Nobody Argued For Your Stack , on how conclusions now spread without the arguments behind them. I mostly agree with him. This is the data side of it. Where AI clearly changes things: leaving Picking a stack was never the hard part. Getting off one was. That's where agents show up. In this year's sample: Express → Fastify 14 of 20, Netlify → Cloudflare 13 of 20, React → Astro 11 of 20. What I think happens next These are guesses, labelled as guesses. Defaults get even stickier for new projects. When a template or an agent reaches for Supabase, Resend and PostHog, most people won't argue. Expect the 2027 numbers for the top pick in each category to be higher, not lower. Lock-in stops being a moat. If moving costs an afternoon, "it's a pain to migrate" stops protecting anyone. Tools will have to keep you on price and reliability. The question shifts from "how fast can I set this up" to "what does this cost me at 10k users". Being readable by agents becomes distribution. 51% of the active product repos I track already have an AGENTS.md or CLAUDE.md. For repos created this year it's 72%. A tool whose docs an agent can follow without guessing will get picked, and migrated to , more often. Model APIs stay the least settled layer. Claude → Gemini: 108 PRs. Gemini → Claude: 104. People switch models like they switch tabs. Caveats Shares are of products I can see evidence for, not market share. Hosted services show up on websites; backend libraries mostly in code.
