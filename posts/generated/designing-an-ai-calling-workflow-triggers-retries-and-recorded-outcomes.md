# Designing an AI calling workflow: triggers, retries and recorded outcomes

## Overview

Originally published as a Fonix.AI practical guide by AntEngage. This republication uses the same guide content. An answered call is only the beginning AI calling software answers or places telephone calls, understands the caller's request and helps execute a defined business workflow. To be useful, it needs more than a convincing voice: it must retrieve the right information, record the outcome and hand over to a person when the request goes beyond its scope. For an Indian business, a practical starting point is inbound enquiries or follow-up with people who requested contact. The goal might be a qualified enquiry, a confirmed callback or a booked appointment. Choose one of those outcomes before choosing a script, a call volume or a software plan. Fonix.AI by AntEngage runs voice agents, chat and campaigns in one platform, supporting 14+ Indian languages and cloud or on-premise deployment. This guide explains the workflow to evaluate with the team. Specific telephony, CRM and booking integrations should be confirmed against your existing systems before rollout. Choose the job before the calling tool A dialler, a voice broadcast and a conversational agent solve different problems. Start with what the recipient needs to do, not with how many calls the system can initiate. Tool What it does A sensible use Dialler Connects a call to a person or queue Staff conduct the conversation and decide the action Voice broadcast Delivers a recorded message An announcement does not require a conversation IVR Routes the caller through configured choices A small, predictable set of destinations Conversational voice agent Handles free-form replies within a defined workflow An enquiry needs clarification, a lookup, a booking or a handoff If a contact only needs a short announcement, an interactive agent may add unnecessary cost. If the recipient needs to ask a question or change a booking, a one-way recording will not finish the job. Evaluate both the normal path and the requests you expect to escalate. Connect the enquiry to a completed next step 1. Start from a trustworthy record An inbound call starts with the caller's request. Outbound follow-up should start from an enquiry or another agreed contact reason, with the appropriate permission, number and current status. Identify the system that owns this information and keep it current. Before an outbound attempt, check whether the person has already been contacted, opted out or completed the task. A fresh campaign upload should not erase those facts. Agree who maintains contact rules and how updates reach the calling workflow. 2. Define one useful conversation Write down the agent's job in a sentence: establish the enquiry, ask the agreed qualification questions, then arrange a callback with the right team. Give it the information it can use and the decisions it is allowed to make. Separate known facts from generated language. Course fees, appointment availability, property inventory and payment status must come from an approved source. When a fact is unavailable, the next action is clarification or handoff, not a plausible guess. 3. Handle the caller's language and timing Introduce the assistant clearly and establish whether it is a suitable time to talk. Let the caller change language, ask for a callback or request a person. A Hindi opening with English dates and regional place names is a realistic evaluation case. Fonix supports Indian-language and code-switched speech. Test with your own names, vocabulary and accents, including pauses, interruptions and corrected answers. A polished sample call does not establish how every call in your campaign will behave. 4. Read, confirm, act Retrieve information from the agreed system, confirm the caller's choice and then perform the permitted action. Announce a booked meeting or appointment only after the destination system confirms the write. If the write fails, retain an explicit pending outcome and offer the agreed alternative. For a callback, establish who will receive it and what time can genuinely be offered. For lead qualification, capture the useful answers and hand them to the appropriate team. Do not turn an uncertain answer into a stronger sales signal merely to improve a dashboard metric. 5. Finish with a usable outcome Store the request, relevant answers, agreed next step, action result and handoff reason. A transcript is useful evidence, but it is not a substitute for a record that tells the team what to do next. Use explicit statuses such as callback requested, booking confirmed, handoff pending, not interested or unresolved. These are illustrative categories, not a published Fonix API contract. Agree how they map into your actual CRM and which person owns each pending task. Send only information needed for the task through the configured system. Assign responsibility so the handoff does not end in an unattended queue. Evaluate the complete system boundary: voice processing, telephony, integrations, records, staff access and ongoing maintenance. An on-premise model does not, by itself, make every connected service local. Ask the team to confirm available integrations and show the exact deployment you need. Assign owners for scripts, approved knowledge, contact policy, support and source-system changes. These responsibilities continue after the first live call. Compare cost per outcome, not just per minute A per-minute quote is an input to the budget, not the final cost of the workflow. Ask what is included: platform access, business numbers, telephony, connected or attempted minutes, setup, integration work, support and recording storage where relevant. Use a worksheet with your own expected volumes. Do not apply one estimate to both answered calls and unanswered attempts without checking how they are billed. Questions buyers ask about AI calling software Is an AI calling agent the same as a dialler? No. A dialler connects calls; an agent conducts a defined conversation and may retrieve information or trigger actions through configured integrations. Choose based on the task, not the label.
