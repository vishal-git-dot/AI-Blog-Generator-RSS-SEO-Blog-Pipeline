# Complete Guide to USPS Tracking Statuses Explained

## Overview

We have all been there: refreshing a tracking page multiple times a day, waiting for an important delivery. Whether you are eagerly anticipating a new gadget, critical business supplies, or a birthday gift for a loved one, monitoring delivery tracking updates can sometimes raise more questions than answers. The United States Postal Service processes hundreds of millions of mailpieces every single day, relying on an automated system of barcodes and facility scans to keep customers informed. However, USPS tracking terminology can occasionally feel cryptic. What does it actually mean when a package is "In Transit, Arriving Late"? Why does a scan say "Label Created" days after an order was placed? In this comprehensive guide, we will break down every common USPS status so you know exactly where your package is and what steps to take if something seems off. 1. Pre-Shipment Statuses Before a parcel enters the physical mail stream, you will likely encounter initial preparation scans. Shipping Label Created, USPS Awaiting Item This is the starting point for modern parcel tracking. It indicates that the merchant or sender has generated a shipping label and paid for postage online. At this stage, USPS has not physically received or scanned the package yet. If this status remains unchanged for several days, it usually means the seller has printed the label but has not yet dropped the item off at the post office or scheduled a carrier pickup. 2. In-Transit Statuses Once USPS takes physical custody of the item, it begins its journey through local sorting centers, regional distribution hubs, and transportation networks. Accepted at USPS Origin Facility / Departed Post Office This status confirms that USPS is now in possession of the parcel. The origin facility scan occurs when a postal worker scans the barcode at the drop-off counter or during initial intake at the local post office. Arrived at / Departed USPS Regional Facility Regional distribution centers are massive logistics hubs equipped with high-speed sorting machines. When your shipment moves from one city or state to another, it routes through these regional centers. Seeing consecutive scans across different hubs simply reflects normal route progression. In Transit to Next Facility This is an automated status indicating that your package is en route between processing locations. If no physical scan occurs within 18 to 24 hours, the USPS system automatically generates an "In Transit" update to let you know the item is still moving through the network. In Transit, Arriving Late This is perhaps the most common source of concern during shipment tracking. It means the item has encountered an unexpected bottleneck—such as severe weather, holiday volume spikes, transportation mechanical issues, or a misrouted container. While frustrating, it rarely means the package is lost; in most instances, it resumes normal movement within 24 to 48 hours. 3. Delivery Phase Statuses When the package arrives in your immediate area, it transitions to final-mile operations. Arrived at Unit The "Unit" refers to your local destination post office. This scan indicates that the parcel has arrived at the specific facility responsible for assigning it to a mail carrier's daily route. Out for Delivery This status confirms that the letter carrier has loaded the parcel onto their delivery truck. Under normal circumstances, you can expect the item to reach your mailbox, porch, or parcel locker by the end of the day (typically before 5:00 PM for residential addresses, though during peak seasons deliveries can occur later into the evening). Delivered The carrier has completed the delivery. The scan will usually specify where the package was placed—such as "In/At Mailbox," "Front Door/Porch," "Parcel Locker," or "Left with Individual." What if it says delivered, but you do not see it? First, check alternate entrance areas, porch corners, and with neighbors. Occasionally, carriers scan packages as "Delivered" while still in the vehicle a short time before actual drop-off, or place smaller items in community cluster boxes. 4. Action-Required and Exception Statuses Sometimes deliveries run into minor obstacles that require recipient intervention or explain an unusual delay. Notice Left (No Authorized Recipient Available): Required when a package demands a signature or a safe, weather-protected drop location was unavailable. The carrier will leave a physical slip (PS Form 3849) with instructions to schedule a redelivery or pick up the item at the local post office. Delivery Attempted – No Access to Delivery Location: This occurs if a gated community code was missing, a driveway was blocked, aggressive animals were loose, or severe weather prevented safe physical access to the property. USPS will typically reattempt delivery the following business day. Available for Pickup: The parcel is being held at the destination post office branch, either due to a customer request (such as Hold Mail or General Delivery) or following an unsuccessful delivery attempt. Forwarded / Processing Exception: If you have an active change-of-address order on file, the package is being rerouted to your new address. If not, it may indicate that the shipping label had an incomplete address or zip code error. Return to Sender: Triggered when an address is deemed insufficient, the recipient has moved without a forwarding address, or the package was refused. The item will be sent back along the reverse route to the original sender. Streamlining Your Package Tracking In today's e-commerce landscape, it is common to have multiple orders arriving simultaneously from different retailers. Many shippers also utilize hybrid shipping models (like UPS SurePost or FedEx Ground Economy), where private carriers handle long-distance transit before transferring the item to USPS for final delivery. When managing orders from various marketplaces and couriers, entering individual tracking numbers into separate websites can become tedious. Using a comprehensive universal tracking tool allows you to monitor all your deliveries in one clean dashboard. Services like TrackPkgs aggregate real-time carrier tracking data from USPS and over 1,500 logistics providers worldwide, making it easy to check delivery status without switching between multiple carrier apps. Conclusion Understanding what each USPS status means helps demystify the postal supply chain and eliminates unnecessary delivery anxiety. Most scan delays resolve themselves as parcels continue through automated sorting networks. However, if your tracking remains completely frozen for more than five to seven business days, opening a USPS Service Request online or contacting the sender is the best next step to get your delivery moving again.
