# How to parse Hacker News \"Who is hiring?\" threads (measured on October 2026: 244 posts, 1 request, 1.5 s)

## Overview

The full script is at the end, standard library only, so you can re-run it. Every month on the first weekday, the whoishiring account posts "Ask HN: Who is hiring?". Each top-level comment is one company's job post, and most follow a loose convention: Company | Role | Location | REMOTE/ONSITE | Salary | URL on the first line. That makes the thread one of the easiest job datasets to collect, as long as you avoid a few traps: Fetching it one comment at a time. Picking the wrong thread from the same account. Treating every top-level comment as a job. Reading funding rounds as salaries. Step 1: find the right thread The whoishiring account posts two threads on the same day . In October 2026, "Who is hiring?" (354 comments) and "Who wants to be hired?" (609 comments) both went up on 2026-10-01. Search results interleave them: 2026-10-01 354 Ask HN: Who is hiring? (October 2026) 2026-10-01 609 Ask HN: Who wants to be hired? (May 2026) November's pair went up on 2026-11-02, the first weekday of the month (the 1st was a Sunday). By 2026-11-04 they had 234 and 409 comments, and October's had grown to 386 and 689. So filter on the exact title, not just the author: GET https://hn.algolia.com/api/v1/search_by_date?tags=story,author_whoishiring&hitsPerPage=20 → hits where title == "Ask HN: Who is hiring? (October 2026)" The comment counts above also show that the thread has shrunk: 545 in May, 354 in October. The October thread is still open, so its count will grow a little. One of the top-level comments this month says exactly that: "there used to be 800+ posts here :(" . Step 2: get the whole tree in one request https://hn.algolia.com/api/v1/items/49922569 returns the story with every comment nested under children . On 2026-10-08 that was 443,292 bytes and 0.96 seconds for 244 top-level posts and 85 replies. The official Firebase API ( hacker-news.firebaseio.com/v0/item/{id}.json ) gives you the story's kids , and then you need one request per comment. Fetching the 288 top-level ids 5 at a time took 14.9 seconds. Firebase documents no rate limit, and Algolia's documented limit is 10,000 requests per hour per IP. So neither is a problem for one thread, but the request count adds up if you go back through years of threads. What Firebase gives you that Algolia doesn't is what disappeared . 26 of the 288 ids are deleted: true and 21 are dead: true (3 are both), so 44 ids, or 15%, are not posts any more. Algolia's tree simply leaves them out. If you store posts and later check whether they were removed, that's the API to use. Step 3: split the first line The convention is pipes, but not everyone follows it. This month's first lines include: Company | Role | Location | REMOTE | $X-Yk | https://... ← the common case PrairieLearn (Remote US) — Full-Stack Software Engineer — TypeScript / Postgres / React / AI Flowstate (London, UK) — Founding Engineer — TypeScript / React / Postgres / AI Senior Python Backend Engineer | REMOTE (EMEA/APAC) ← no company GROWTH HACKER - GLOBAL - $50/HR Splitting on | alone gives 3+ parts on 218 of 244 posts. Splitting on | , an em or en dash, or a spaced hyphen gives 222 (91%). Don't assume the parts come in a fixed order: some posts put location second, some put the role second, and some skip the role and list it in the body. In practice, the first part is the company, and you find the rest by matching keywords (remote, onsite, hybrid, full-time, visa, a currency symbol) in each part, not by position. How often position fails, from a second read of the same thread on 2026-10-15 (266 top-level posts by then): the second part looked like a role in 126 posts and like a location or an onsite/remote marker in 48, e.g. exe.dev | onsite | SF Bay Area | Full-time designer . "Remote" is the same kind of trap. It appears in 139 first lines, but 63 of those restrict it ( REMOTE (US/Can) , Remote (EU timezones) ), 12 also say onsite ( ONSITE NYC or REMOTE (US/Can) ), and 10 posts mention remote only in the body. A plain remote: true over-matches. Keep the text in parentheses next to the boolean. Step 4: salaries (and what is not a salary) 73 of 244 posts (30%) contain a yearly-looking salary range. All of them are in USD (62), EUR (8) or GBP (3). Three things went wrong in my first version of the regex: The K at the end covers both numbers. $130-240K , €35-45k and $140–170k mean 130,000 to 240,000. My first regex read the lower bound as $130, and its "at least 20,000" check threw it away. Fixing that took the count from 67 to 73. Funding and revenue look like money. $42M / $60M , raised roughly $70m , $5.4M and over £50 billion worth of property are about the company, not the job. Reject numbers followed by M , B or a word like "billion". Don't just take the first $ in the post. Hourly rates exist. GROWTH HACKER - GLOBAL - $50/HR , AI Engineer (£30/hr) and Senior AI Engineer (£50/hr) . My script keeps only amounts between 20,000 and 1,000,000 and treats them as yearly, which drops these posts. That's on purpose: it's the only check here I trust without reading every post. Two more things the 73 don't capture: "Up to ~$750k" becomes 750,000 to 750,000. It's really a maximum with no minimum. 100k-160k GBP , with the currency written after the numbers, has no symbol to anchor on. Step 5: drop the comments that aren't jobs At least 7 of the 244 top-level comments are not job posts: 4 are about the thread itself (its size, a request for a four-day-week tag, complaints about seniority). 1 is a job seeker who posted in this thread instead of "Who wants to be hired?" ( Location: … Remote: Yes … Willing to relocate: … ). 1 is someone applying to a role in a top-level comment. 1 is a job aggregator advertising itself. A length check catches the short ones, since the complaints are 35 to 250 characters. The seeker and the applicant are 1,000+ characters, though. What worked better than length was three checks, in order: drop a post that has both a Location: line and Willing to relocate: (the seeker template); drop a post whose first line starts with "I'm applying" or "I'm interested"; otherwise keep it if the first line splits into 3+ parts or the post gives a way to apply (a link, an e-mail address, or words like "hiring", "apply", "resume"). On 2026-10-09, with the October thread at 248 top-level comments, that dropped 6, and I read all six: 3 comments about the thread, the aggregator, the seeker and the applicant. One of the four comments about the thread still got through, so expect a few false positives. On the finished thread (2026-11-04, 257 posts) the same three checks dropped 9, and 2 of them were real job posts : whitecircle.com/careers, we're building the most advanced AI safety infra, raised roughly $70m in total, join us and a junior-developer post ending in vvd.world/careers ⬅ is our site . Both give a careers page as a bare domain, without https:// , so HN doesn't turn it into a link, and neither says "hiring" or "apply". Adding "a bare domain followed by a path" ( \b[\w-]+(?:\.[\w-]+)*\.[a-z]{2,}/\S+ ) or the word "careers" to the last check brought the drops to 7, all non-jobs: the 3 comments about the thread, the aggregator, 2 job seekers and the applicant. On November (181 posts) it dropped 6, all non-jobs too: 3 job seekers, two one-word comments ( India , EIOU ) and a product pitch. Re-check the dropped posts by hand each month; there are few enough to read. The script Standard library only.
