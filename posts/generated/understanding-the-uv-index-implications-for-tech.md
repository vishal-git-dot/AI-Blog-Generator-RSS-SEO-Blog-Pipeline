What is the UV Index and How Does it Work? The UV index is a numerical scale that quantifies the strength of ultraviolet radiation from the sun at a given location and time. It helps individuals understand their potential risk of skin damage due to sun exposure. The scale typically ranges from 0 (minimal risk) to 11+ (extreme risk). The UV index is calculated based on various factors, including the angle of the sun, cloud cover, altitude, and ozone levels. A notable point is that despite a lower UV index, individuals can still experience sunburn, particularly if they are near reflective surfaces like water or sand. Mechanisms Behind the UV Index Solar Radiation : The amount of UV radiation reaching the earth's surface varies throughout the day and year, influenced by the sun's position. Measurement : UV sensors placed on weather stations measure UV radiation and convert this data into an index. Public Awareness : Governments and organizations use the UV index to inform the public about when to take precautions against sun exposure.
