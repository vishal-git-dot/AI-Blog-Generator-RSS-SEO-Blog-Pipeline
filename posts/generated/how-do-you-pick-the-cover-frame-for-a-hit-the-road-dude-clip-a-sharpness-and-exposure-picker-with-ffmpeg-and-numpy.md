# How Do You Pick the Cover Frame for a Hit the Road Dude Clip? A Sharpness-and-Exposure Picker With ffmpeg and NumPy

## Overview

When you post a Hit the Road Dude clip, the State Property meme where a driver at a pickup window repeats "State?" and sends someone off, the cover frame decides whether anyone taps it. Most platforms pick a frame for you, and they often pick a mid-blink or a motion-blurred one. This is a small tool that picks a better one automatically. I build hittheroaddude.app , an independent studio that turns one photo of an adult or a pet into the 15-second, vertical 9:16 pickup-window clip with a newly generated voice. Which frame to use as a thumbnail is a fair question for anyone posting a remake, so here is a rule of thumb turned into a script. I tested it on a synthetic clip, not on generated customer clips, so the numbers below only show that the picker works, not how it scores any real remake. What makes a good cover frame Three things I can measure without a model: Sharpness. A blurred frame (motion, a transition) has little fine detail. The variance of a Laplacian is the classic cheap measure: the more edges, the higher the variance. Exposure. Skip frames that are mostly black or blown out. I check the mean brightness and the share of crushed or clipped pixels. Distance from the edges. The first and last half-second are often a fade, a hard cut or a frozen end frame. If you want to check those separately, see the black-and-frozen-edges check in my guides repo. What this does not measure is whether the face looks right or whether the driver's expression is the funny one. For a meme with a deadpan pause, the best frame is often the "State?" beat, and no sharpness metric knows that. So the script prints the top few timestamps and you make the final call. Nothing from the blurred or dark parts made the list. The scores are close because the test pattern is uniformly detailed; I have not measured the gap on real clips. Why the exposure term is a multiplier A plain sharpness score can reward a mostly black frame, because hard edges between black and bright areas score high on a Laplacian. Multiplying by an exposure factor (1.0 if the mean brightness is between 45 and 210, otherwise 0.3) and by the share of non-clipped pixels guards against that. It is a hack, and the thresholds are my choice, not a standard. If your clips are intentionally dark, change them. Tips for using it Raise --fps (to 8 or 12) if the clip has fast movement. The default of 4 frames per second is enough for a mostly static camera like the pickup window. Use --margin to skip more of the start. I use 0.5 s by default. Always look at the top three. The score finds sharp frames, not funny ones. If the "State?" beat is in the top three, take it. Crop for the platform. A 9:16 cover is cropped to a square on some profile grids, so keep the face in the middle third. My posting check covers the rest of what to look at before you share a remake. Label the post.
