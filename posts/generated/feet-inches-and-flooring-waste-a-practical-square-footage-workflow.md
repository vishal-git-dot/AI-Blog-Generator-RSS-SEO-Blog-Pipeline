# Feet, Inches, and Flooring Waste: A Practical Square Footage Workflow

## Overview

When a flooring estimate goes wrong, the arithmetic is rarely the only problem. The common failures are mixing feet and inches, measuring overlapping sections twice, and rounding boxes before the total area is final. Keep the measurement units honest A tape reading of 12 feet 6 inches is 12.5 decimal feet, not 12.6. Convert each complete measurement before multiplying. For metric rooms, keep meters or centimeters consistent throughout the calculation, then convert the final area only when comparing product coverage. Break irregular rooms into non-overlapping pieces For an L-shaped room, divide the floor plan into rectangles, calculate each rectangle, and add the results. Subtract a closet or cutout only once. The same rule works for several rooms: keep a room-by-room list, then total the areas after checking that no section overlaps another. Add waste after the net area Waste depends on material, pattern, room shape, and installer. Add the chosen allowance to the combined net area, then round up using the product's coverage per box. A box rounding step should not replace the waste calculation, and an online estimate should be checked against the product label and installation plan. I maintain Square Footage Calculator as a free browser tool for feet-and-inches, metric, L-shaped, and multiple-room planning. It is designed for early comparisons and shopping preparation, not as a substitute for a contractor or manufacturer quote. The most reliable workflow is simple: measure twice, label every section, calculate with one unit system, add waste once, and round boxes at the end.
