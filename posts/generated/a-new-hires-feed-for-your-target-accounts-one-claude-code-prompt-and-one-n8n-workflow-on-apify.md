# A new-hires feed for your target accounts: one Claude Code prompt and one n8n workflow on Apify

## Overview

A new manager or VP at a target account is the best moment to reach out. They have a mandate to change things and no vendor habits yet. The half of the signal that is usually missing is where that person came from. A hire who came from one of your customers already knows your product; a hire who came from a competitor's customer knows the alternative. This guide builds that signal for a list of accounts in two ways, once as a prompt an agent executes in Claude Code (or any MCP client) and once as an n8n workflow that posts to Slack every Monday. Both call the same Actor on Apify, New Hires Finder , which reads a database of professional profile records with position dates. Nothing logs in to LinkedIn and nothing scrapes a page; a company answers in seconds. Then: what an experienced GTM team actually does with the rows in the hire's first 90 days. Everything measured below was run on 2026-10-10 and the numbers are quoted as they came out. The Actor is metered per returned row; a miss comes back as a note row, not as a hire. One thing to know about any start-date window: people update their profiles weeks or months after they start, so the most recent months are always thin. A "last 6 months" window returns the hires already on record, and a weekly run picks up the late updaters as they appear. Reading "where they came from": three plays The previousCompany column is the part of the row that does the most work. It sorts every hire into one of three conversations before you write a word: The hire came from What it means The first message One of your customers They know your product and probably liked it, or they would not list the company proudly. The warmest lead a sales team gets all quarter. "You used us at [previous company]. Here is what changed since, and I can have you set up here in a week." Sent by the account executive, not a sequence. A competitor, or a company known to use the competitor They know the alternative from the inside and are about to compare it with whatever they inherited. A migration story and a switching-cost answer, not a feature list. Ask what they liked and what they would fix. Anywhere else A mandate and no habits. The classic first-90-days window. Reference the mandate, not the hire: "New leaders in [function] usually inherit X; here is how teams like yours handled it." Matching rule I give the agent and would give a junior rep: match previousCompany against the customer list by exact name or domain; when the names differ only loosely, mark "unclear" and do not guess. A false "warm" opener costs more than a missed one. Where the account list comes from companyDomains takes up to 50 domains per run, so the account list is a decision, not a technicality. The lists that have paid off: Open opportunities and target accounts from the CRM. The obvious one, and the one where a new leader changes a stalled deal's odds the most. Closed-lost from the last twelve months. A new leader at a closed-lost account is the only legitimate reason to reopen a conversation that ended with a no. Your competitors' customers. Their case-study pages name them. A new leader at one of those accounts is a switching window you can see coming. Lookalikes of your best customers. A lookalike company list built from ten reference domains gives a few hundred accounts you have never spoken to, and the hires signal tells you which of them to speak to first. Before any run the row ceiling is arithmetic: domains × maxPerCompany . What an experienced GTM team does with the rows: the 90-day playbook The rows are the easy part. This is the part that decides whether the signal pays off. Triage inside 48 hours. Warm hires (from a customer) go to the account executive who owns the account, with the previous-company context in the handoff note. Neutral manager-level hires go into a sequence. C-level hires get a two-line note from your own executive, never from a sequence. Do not write on day one. The first two weeks a new leader is listening, not deciding. Weeks two to six is when they form the plan and are still open to inputs; weeks eight to twelve is when budgets get requested. startedAt is a month, so put the follow-up on the calendar for three to five weeks after it rather than firing the moment the row arrives. Reference the mandate, not the hire. "Congratulations on the new role" is what everyone sends. "New heads of data at payment companies usually inherit three reporting stacks; here is how two of your peers consolidated" is what gets a reply. The previous company and title tell you what they are used to, so say it. Multi-thread from the start. One new VP is one seat. Run Decision Maker Finder on the same domain (ten people per company by default) for the CEO, the CFO and the peer who owns the adjacent budget. A deal that depends on one person leaving their honeymoon period is a fragile deal. Contacts last, and only for the shortlist. Pass the chosen linkedinUrl s to the email Actor. Read the emailType : work_current is the address at the new employer; work_other is an address at the previous one, which tells you the person moved and is exactly where not to write. Write it into the CRM as fields, not notes. startedAt , previousCompany and the warm/competitor/neutral bucket as contact properties; a task dated startedAt plus three weeks; a source tag so the pipeline report can show "new-hire signal" as its own row. Measure by bucket. Reply rate and meetings for warm versus competitor versus neutral. In my experience the warm bucket carries the whole program; if it does not for you, the customer list is wrong, not the signal. Re-run monthly with since . The Actor has no memory of earlier runs, so set since to the month after your last run and de-duplicate on linkedinUrl . Late profile updates arrive as new rows in later months; that is the data catching up, not noise. More ways to use the new-hires signal Beyond the account list, the same Actor and fields answer questions I would put to it next: Talent flow between competitors. Run it on your competitors' domains and count previousCompany . The map of who poaches from whom is a positioning document and a recruiting plan at once. Function-level hiring waves. jobTitles: ["sales", "account executive"] across your accounts: a company adding ten sellers in a quarter is about to need everything a sales team needs. Investor and board monitoring. Portfolio domains in companyDomains , monthly, seniority: ["cxo", "vp"] . Executive changes land in the board pack without anyone asking. Partner and channel intelligence. A partner's new head of alliances is the person who will redraw the partner tier list; be in their first ten meetings. Customer onboarding triggers. New managers at existing customers need training on your product; the row is the invitation list for onboarding sessions, and a quiet expansion motion. Regional staffing signals. countries: ["de"] on a global account shows where the company is building a team, which is where a local vendor should be. Your own competitors' customer success. New heads of support or customer success at a competitor's customer are exactly the people who will run the next vendor review. Job seekers. Point it at ten companies you want to join: the managers who started this quarter are the hiring managers of next quarter, and previousCompany tells you which backgrounds they hire from. When something is off A domain returns nothing → nobody on record started in the window at that seniority. Widen sinceMonths or drop the seniority filter. No row is returned for it. outside_window or left_already rows → the person's record has a newer job elsewhere, or their position at the company is older than the window. Shown as notes so you can see what was excluded. A hire from last month is missing → the profile has not shown the new position yet. It will be in a later run. Every hire has the same start month → the window reaches past the months the database has caught up on, so the newest month with data fills the result. Widen to twelve months to see the real distribution; narrow with since for the monthly feed.
