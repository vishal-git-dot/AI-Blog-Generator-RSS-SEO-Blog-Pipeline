# Apple Watch Series 12 Live Rewind and Siri Recap: how the new Audio Intelligence features work

## Overview

Your Apple Watch is about to get a much better memory Apple has confirmed that the Apple Watch Series 12 and Apple Watch Ultra 4 will receive two new AI-powered features later this year: Live Rewind and Siri Recap . Together they form what Apple calls the "Audio Intelligence" suite — and they could change how you use your watch in meetings, appointments, and everyday conversations. Both features are coming in beta later in 2026, and they'll be exclusive to the two newest watches. Here's how each one works, what Apple promises on privacy, and the catches to know about. Live Rewind: "what did they just say?" Live Rewind solves one of life's small universal frustrations: missing something someone just said. When activated, the watch can recall up to 15 seconds of recent conversation and display it as a text snippet on screen. To trigger it, you double-press the Digital Crown. The watch then shows a transcription of the last few seconds, which you can read at a glance — or ask Siri about for more context, or save to the Siri app for later reference. Apple has built in visible safeguards: activating Live Rewind produces an audible chime plus a microphone indicator and full-screen animation — even when the watch is in silent mode — so people nearby can tell the feature is in use. Siri Recap: an AI summary of your day Siri Recap takes a more ambient approach. It listens in the background throughout the day and uses Apple Intelligence to generate high-level summaries of your conversations — not word-for-word transcripts, but condensed notes on the key points, which you can review whenever you like. Think of it as a memory aid for days packed with meetings: instead of scribbling notes, you get a digest of what mattered. You control when the feature is active via Control Center, and summaries automatically disappear after seven days unless you choose to save them. How Apple says it protects your privacy These are, obviously, sensitive features — a watch that listens all day raises immediate questions. Apple's answers: No permanent recordings. Live Rewind doesn't store audio on the device; Siri Recap keeps summaries, not recordings. On-device processing. Audio is handled inside the Secure Enclave of the S11 chip and then wiped. Summaries are generated using Apple's Foundation Models, with Private Cloud Compute used where needed. Nothing lingers in the cloud. Apple says user data doesn't stick around on its servers, and synced summaries are protected with end-to-end encryption via iCloud. Sensitive content filtered. The system is designed to limit capture of things like financial details and government ID numbers. The catches There are several. Both features require an iPhone 16 or newer (the iPhone 16e is excluded) running Apple Intelligence. They launch in English only , with more languages promised later. They won't be available in the EU at first. And if you're buying a watch for these features, know they're still in beta with no exact release date — Apple says "later this year." Both watches also share the new S11 chip, 64GB of storage, and a Health Sensing System with high-frequency background heart-rate tracking feeding a new Readiness recovery-score app — but those are available today, unlike Audio Intelligence. Should you care? If you already own a Series 12 or Ultra 4, these are free upgrades worth trying once the beta lands — particularly Live Rewind, which has obvious accessibility benefits. If you're on an older watch, don't upgrade just for these yet: wait for the beta reviews to confirm the features work as advertised, especially the privacy promises, which will face intense scrutiny the moment they ship. The bottom line Apple is turning the watch from a notification mirror into something closer to a personal aide with perfect recall. Whether that's delightful or dystopian will depend entirely on whether the privacy architecture holds up in the real world. Disclosure: This post contains affiliate links.
