# ChatGPT Ads Now Rewrite Your Ad Copy by Default

## Overview

What changed in ChatGPT Ads on October 30? As of Friday, October 30, OpenAI's ad system can rewrite your ChatGPT ad, translate it, resize the image and send the click to a different page on your site, all without you approving each version. The setting, called creative enhancements, is now on by default for new and existing campaigns, Search Engine Journal reported . OpenAI warned advertisers by email on October 20. The email said these ads "can run automatically without requiring you to review or approve each version," according to PPC Land , which reproduced it. OpenAI's help page on creative enhancements lists the four kinds of change, and Search Engine Roundtable confirmed on October 30 that the auto-enable went out in OpenAI's weekly advertiser email. How many businesses does this affect? More than 100,000 advertisers have now run ads on ChatGPT, up from about 20,000 in August, and the ads show to free and Go users in 89 countries. OpenAI shared those numbers in late October when it added 26 new markets, Thurrott reported . So the default flipped for a pool of advertisers that grew five times in about two months. If you set up a campaign this summer, haven't opened Ads Manager since and didn't opt out before the deadline, the system is now editing your ads. What exactly can OpenAI change in your ad? ChatGPT Ads can change four parts of your ad: your headline and description, the language, the image size, and the landing page URL. The system builds versions from the conversation the ad shows up in, your original ad and the content on your landing page. Versions can be made ahead of time or on the fly. Image size Resized images. OpenAI hasn't said whether that includes cropping. Destination URL The click can land on a different page of your site than the one you picked. Search Engine Journal found gaps in OpenAI's own docs. Ads Manager doesn't show a preview of every version, and no report breaks out each enhanced version on its own. The help page also doesn't say how your tracking tags carry over when the system swaps the landing page. Why should a business owner who answers leads care? Your sales process starts with the promise in the ad. If software rewrote that promise, the person calling you or filling out your form may expect something you never offered. Your team won't know, because nobody on your side saw that version. I've been running ads since 2013, and one of the fastest ways to lose a good lead on the first call is a mismatch. The ad said one thing, the person who picks up says another, and the lead stops trusting both. Automated rewrites make that mismatch more likely, and you can't see it from Ads Manager. The landing page swap worries me more. If your form, your booking calendar or your tracking sits on one page and the system sends people to another, you can lose leads you paid for and never find out why.
