Feature 1: Loan-to-Value (LTV) Ratio—Your Borrowing Power (H2) The loan-to-value ratio is the single most important number in any crypto loan. It determines how much you can borrow against your collateral . If you deposit $10,000 worth of Bitcoin and the platform allows 50% LTV, you can borrow up to $5,000 . Different platforms and assets have different LTV limits. Stablecoins often allow higher LTV—up to 90%—because their price is stable . Bitcoin and Ethereum typically cap at 50% . Altcoins may have much lower limits. Why does LTV matter so much? A higher LTV gives you more cash upfront but increases your risk of liquidation. If the market drops 20% and you're at 70% LTV, you could face a margin call. At 30% LTV, you have breathing room. Smart borrowers choose conservative LTV ratios—50% or lower—to protect their collateral . When comparing platforms, look at both the maximum LTV and the liquidation threshold. The gap between them determines your safety buffer. Feature 2: Interest Rates and Fees—The True Cost (H2) The advertised rate isn't the whole story. You need to understand both the interest rate and all fees to calculate your true borrowing cost. Interest rates vary dramatically across platforms: Nexo: Rates start at 1.9% APR for Platinum-tier clients with 20% or lower LTV Uphold (via Exactly Protocol): Fixed rates starting at 4.28% APR Salt Lending: 9.95% to 14.45% APR depending on LTV Ledn: 9.99% to 11.49% APR based on loan size But interest isn't the only cost. Watch for: Origination fees: Ledn charges a 2% origination fee for borrowers outside the US and Canada . Gas fees: DeFi loans involve blockchain transaction fees . Early repayment penalties: Some platforms penalize early repayment. Hidden charges: Check for withdrawal or maintenance fees. Nexo and Uphold charge no origination fees . When comparing offers, calculate the total cost—not just the advertised APR. The cheapest headline rate may not be the cheapest overall. Feature 3: Repayment Flexibility—Avoiding Unnecessary Stress (H2) The repayment structure dramatically affects your borrowing experience. Some platforms are rigid; others are flexible. Open-ended credit lines like Nexo's revolve—you draw funds, repay on your own schedule, and the credit line remains open. There's no maturity date and no minimum monthly payment . Interest accrues daily only on your outstanding balance . This flexibility is valuable for borrowers with uncertain cash flow or who want to borrow opportunistically. Fixed-term loans like those from Salt Lending and Ledn have defined repayment periods (12 to 60 months). You receive a lump sum and repay on a fixed schedule . This structure works well if you want predictability—a defined repayment amount and a clear end date . Ask yourself: do you want the freedom to repay when it suits you? Or do you prefer the discipline of a fixed schedule? Both have merit, but they fit different borrowing styles. Nexo's flexibility is meaningful for ongoing cash flow needs; Ledn's fixed structure provides certainty.
