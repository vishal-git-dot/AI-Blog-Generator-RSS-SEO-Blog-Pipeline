# How to turn Google Maps Scraper output into a scored local SEO lead list

## Overview

By Alex Rowan Disclosure: Manta Media, the local SEO agency I work at, built the two Apify Actors linked near the end of this post (Google Maps Leads Scorer and Local SEO Bulk Site Auditor). Every step here also works in your own script, and the code is included. A Google Maps scrape gives you a long list of businesses: names, phones, websites, ratings. What it doesn't tell you is which of them actually need help with their website or local SEO. If you do outreach for an agency or as a freelancer, emailing all 200 is a waste of everyone's time. This walkthrough goes from a raw Maps scrape to a short, ranked list with one concrete talking point per business. You can do every step yourself in Python, or use an Actor for the scoring part. Step 1: Scrape one niche in one city Run Google Maps Scraper ( compass/crawler-google-places ) for a single category and location. Keep it to one location per run, as the Actor's own input form recommends. { "searchStringsArray" : [ "plumber" ], "locationQuery" : "Beaverton, OR, USA" , "maxCrawledPlacesPerSearch" : 50 , "language" : "en" } Each place in the output dataset includes, among many other fields, title , website , phone , totalScore (rating), reviewsCount , categoryName and city . Those seven fields are all we need. Note the run's dataset ID ; you'll pass it to the next step. Step 2: Decide what "needs help" means These are the checks we use per business. Each one is cheap: one fetch of the homepage, plus robots.txt and an http:// redirect check. No website at all. The clearest lead for a web designer. No LocalBusiness structured data. No JSON-LD telling Google the business name, address and phone. Title and meta description. A missing meta description, or a title over ~60 characters that gets cut off in results. Phone and address on the homepage. Local visitors expect to see them, and Google reads them too. HTTPS. A valid certificate, and http:// redirecting to https:// . Reviews vs the local median. 3 reviews means little on its own. Against a group median of 31 (from the sample run below), it's a concrete talking point. A mismatch is a real NAP (name, address, phone) problem worth pointing out. Step 3: Score and tier Add the signals into one 0-100 opportunity score , where higher means a better prospect. These are the weights the Google Maps Leads Scorer documents in its README: Signal Points SEO gap: 100 minus the site's local SEO score, scaled up to 35 No LocalBusiness schema +10 No SSL (or no https redirect) +8 (+4) No meta description / no title +5 / +4 No mobile viewport +5 No phone on the homepage / no street address (on the page or in schema) +4 / +3 Slow site (over 3 s to respond, or PageSpeed mobile under 50 when enabled) +5 Reviews below the batch median (scaled by how far below) up to +15 Rating under 4.0 (under 4.5) +7 (+3) Public contact email found (only when findEmails is on) +10 No website 85 base Website failed to load 45 base Review and rating points are added on top of the base for no-website and site-down leads too. Scores are capped at 100. Tiers: hot is ≥ 50 or no website, warm is ≥ 30, cold is < 30. Tune the weights to whatever you sell. A web designer might weight "no website" and "no viewport" higher; an SEO agency might weight schema and reviews. Be polite You're visiting small-business websites, so keep the load trivial: Respect robots.txt, and skip pages it disallows. Use low concurrency (we use 3) and conservative timeouts (10 s connect / 20 s read). Cap the download size per page, and use an identifiable User-Agent. Run each site with a hard time limit. One pathological page shouldn't stall the whole batch. Only collect emails a business shows publicly on its own site, and follow CAN-SPAM / GDPR / PDPA when you contact anyone. And a high review count doesn't mean a well-optimised site: in a larger run, Apollo Plumbing, Heating, AC & Electric had 2,014 reviews but a local SEO score of 20 and no LocalBusiness schema, which put it in the hot tier. That's why it's worth scoring the website rather than filtering on reviews alone. The practical outcome: instead of emailing every place on the scrape, you start with the hot and warm rows and one specific, checkable observation for each.
