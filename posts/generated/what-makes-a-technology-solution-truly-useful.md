# What makes a technology solution truly useful ?

## Overview

Lately, I’ve been diving into the world of AI and IoT, and one question keeps popping up in my mind:What really makes a technology solution useful? It’s easy to get caught up in the excitement of what new technology could do. But when it comes to real-world use, I think the key is how well it solves an actual problem.AI and IoT in the Real WorldThe Internet of Things (IoT) connects devices and sensors that gather and share data.Artificial intelligence (AI) then helps analyze that data, spotting patterns and insights.Together, these technologies can be powerful in physical spaces and industrial settings.Some examples include: Keeping track of equipment and assets Getting a clearer picture of inventory and operations Enhancing workforce safety and monitoring Controlling access to facilities Making sense of operational data But none of this really matters unless it’s tackling a real need.Start with the Problem, Not the TechA big lesson I’m learning is that technology shouldn’t be the first step.Say a company struggles to track its equipment.Instead of jumping straight to, “Which tech should we buy?” it’s way more useful to ask: Why is this a problem in the first place? How is it impacting the business? What information do we already have? What do the people involved actually need? Only after answering those can you figure out if AI, IoT, or something else makes sense.What I Learned from Venture StudiosI came across Aperture Venture Studio, which builds AI and IoT companies focused on solving real industrial problems.Their approach is all about: Finding high-value problems Designing AIoT solutions around real use cases Testing ideas with customers Growing ventures based on what really works I find this approach inspiring because it ties innovation directly to real-world challenges.It’s a reminder that innovation isn’t always about inventing something brand new—it’s often about solving existing problems better.Still Figuring It OutSince I’m still exploring AIoT, I’d love to hear from folks who’ve worked with these technologies.How do you decide if a problem is right for AI or IoT?And what’s tougher in your experience: building the tech or making sure people actually find it useful?I’m curious to know your thoughts and experiences.
