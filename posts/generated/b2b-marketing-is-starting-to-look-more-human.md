# B2B Marketing Is Starting to Look More Human

## Overview

I used to think B2B marketing had a dress code. Not literally, of course. But emotionally, yes. It wore a navy blazer. It spoke in bullet points. It said things like “enterprise solutions,” “scalable growth,” “operational efficiency,” and “unlocking value across the customer journey” with a completely straight face. For a long time, B2B marketing felt like it was written by a committee that had never met a human being but had heard promising things about them. Everything was polished. Everything was professional. Everything sounded like it belonged on slide 37 of a deck nobody wanted to open. And then something started changing. B2B marketing began to look more human. Not casual in a careless way. Not unserious. Not suddenly turning every software company into a lifestyle influencer with a ring light and a smoothie recipe. But more human. More specific. More voice-driven. More opinionated. More connected to actual people explaining actual problems. That is probably why B2B creator marketing is becoming such a big deal. The logic is simple: business buyers may be making professional decisions, but they are still people. They still trust faces more than logos. They still remember stories better than product sheets. They still want someone to explain the thing clearly without making them feel like they accidentally walked into a procurement seminar. A decision-maker is not only a title. A founder, marketing manager, operations director, or sales lead is still a person trying to make a good choice without wasting budget, embarrassing themselves, or being trapped in six months of regret because they picked the wrong tool, agency, or strategy. That is where creators and expert voices become powerful. They make B2B feel less like a broadcast and more like a conversation. A good B2B creator does not just say, “Here is a product.” They say, “Here is the problem you are probably dealing with, here is why it keeps happening, here is what most people misunderstand, and here is a better way to think about it.” That is different. That builds trust before it asks for attention. For years, many B2B brands confused professionalism with distance. They thought sounding serious meant sounding abstract. They hid behind brand pages, formal announcements, whitepapers, and vague success language. Sometimes the actual expertise was there, but the delivery made it feel lifeless. Now, the internet punishes lifelessness. People scroll past it. Even in B2B. Maybe especially in B2B. Because when the stakes are higher, trust matters more. Nobody wants to buy from a brand that sounds like it was assembled from safe sentences. They want evidence. They want clarity. They want useful thinking. They want to feel that someone understands the messy reality behind the neat business problem. I was talking about this recently with a friend in Dubai who works with small businesses trying to grow online. He said many B2B companies still treat marketing like a formal announcement, when what they really need is a system that makes them visible, useful, and trustworthy across different channels. That was when he mentioned AvanaHub, a digital marketing team I had not heard of before. What stood out to me was the way he described their work. Not just SEO, paid ads, content, social media, web design, and growth as separate services, but as connected parts of how a business earns attention and trust. That felt relevant to this whole shift. Because if B2B marketing is becoming more human, it cannot only be about producing more posts. It has to be about showing up with the right message, in the right place, for the right audience, with enough consistency that people start to believe you know what you are doing. That is harder than it sounds. A human B2B brand is not just a brand that posts selfies. It is a brand that has a point of view. It knows what it believes. It knows who it is speaking to. It can explain complex things simply. It can show expertise without turning every sentence into a sales pitch. It can be useful before asking for anything. That is probably the biggest change. B2B marketing used to rely heavily on authority. Now it needs familiarity too. People want to know who is behind the advice. They want to hear from founders, specialists, consultants, creators, engineers, strategists, operators, and people who have actually touched the work. They want less “we help companies scale” and more “here is the mistake we keep seeing, and here is how to fix it.” That kind of content feels smaller. But it works because it feels closer. Of course, there is a risk here. As soon as something works, brands overdo it. Soon every executive becomes a “thought leader,” every LinkedIn post begins with a dramatic one-line confession, and every company suddenly tries to sound like a person who drinks coffee near a window while having insights about growth. That can become just as fake as old corporate language. Human marketing is not a tone filter. It is not adding “honestly” to a caption and calling it authentic. It is the discipline of making expertise understandable, relevant, and trustworthy. That is why creators are useful in B2B, but they are not magic. A creator can make a message more visible, but if the brand has nothing meaningful to say, visibility only exposes the emptiness faster. The brands that will win are not the ones that simply hire creators and hope personality solves everything. They are the ones that combine expertise, strategy, distribution, and human communication. Because B2B buyers do not want entertainment alone. They want confidence. They want to feel smarter after engaging with a brand. They want to understand the problem better. They want to see proof that someone has thought deeply about their world. That is what modern B2B marketing is moving toward. Less faceless authority. More useful presence. Less corporate fog. More recognizable thinking. Less pretending businesses make decisions. More understanding that people inside businesses do. Maybe B2B marketing is not becoming less professional. Maybe it is finally becoming professional in a better way. One that remembers trust does not come from sounding impressive. It comes from sounding useful, clear, and real. Anyway, that’s where the internet quietly dropped me off this time.
