# Day 10: How I Hired a CMO for My Startup with a $0 Budget (The Exact Deal) 🐯☁️

## Overview

Day 9 was a massive technical win. I shipped hot reloading, enterprise spinners, and eradicated the 502 Bad Gateway error. But as I stared at my terminal on my $150 Android phone, a harsh reality hit me: A product without users is just code. I am a builder. I know how to write Deno edge functions and provision Firecracker MicroVMs. I do not know how to run marketing campaigns, optimize conversion funnels, or drive organic traffic. I needed a Chief Marketing Officer (CMO). But I am bootstrapping HYNAWEB with exactly $0. I cannot pay a salary. So, how do you hire an executive when you have no money? You offer them a piece of the future. Here is the exact blueprint of how I hired my first CMO. 🤝 1. Finding the Right Partner (Peer-to-Peer) I didn't look for a 40-year-old marketing guru. I looked for a peer. I connected with Nicky, a 13-year-old developer who had already been helping me security-test my app. He had a sharp mind, he understood my constraints, and he had already given me elite advice on how to get organic traffic. I didn't need an employee. I needed a co-founder who believed in the mission. 🧠 2. The Negotiation (He Fact-Checked Me with AI) When I first proposed a deal, I offered him 2% of Net Profit . Nicky didn't just blindly accept it. He ran my offer through an AI, fact-checked the math, and called me out. He pointed out that for a bootstrapped startup, "net profit" is often zero after server costs, making my 2% offer a literal rounding error (40 cents a month). He was 100% right. As a founder, you need people who challenge you, not just "yes-men." I respected the pushback and went back to the drawing board. 📊 3. The Final Deal: 13% Attribution-Based Revenue We scrapped the "net profit" model and built something fair, transparent, and highly motivating. The Offer: Nicky gets 13% of the Gross Revenue generated specifically from the users and traffic he brings in. What "Attribution-Based" Means: If Nicky's marketing brings in 100 INR, and the rest of the company brings in 1,000 INR, he gets 13% of the 100 INR. He is directly rewarded for his impact. It protects the company's baseline revenue while giving him a massive, top-tier incentive to grow. 📈 4. The Secret Weapon: Radical Transparency How do you prove to a remote CMO that they are getting their fair cut without a fancy SaaS dashboard? A Google Sheet. I built a "Monthly Transparency Spreadsheet" that tracks: Total clicks from his unique tracking link ( ?ref=nicky ). The exact ad revenue or subscriptions generated by that cohort. The automatic 13% calculation. At the end of every month, he gets "View Only" access to this sheet. No hidden numbers, no manual math, no guessing. Just raw, auditable data. 🧠 The Founder Takeaway You do not need a massive funding round to build a team. You do not need a fancy office or a corporate lawyer. You need fairness, transparency, and a shared vision. When you treat your partners with respect, give them real ownership (even if it's just 13%), and show them the raw data, you build trust. And trust is the currency of early-stage startups. Nicky is currently out scouting his first 5 high-value posts to drive our initial traffic. I don't know if this will make us millionaires, but I know we are building it the right way. HYNAWEB isn't just a codebase anymore. It's a company. Building in public, one line of code (and one spreadsheet) at a time.
