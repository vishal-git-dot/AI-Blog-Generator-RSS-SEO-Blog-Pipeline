# WhatsApp Integration in a CRM: What Indian Sales Teams Actually Need

## Overview

Indian sales teams live on WhatsApp. Deals are started on a forward, qualified with screenshots, and closed on a voice note. A CRM that ignores that reality doesn't integrate — it competes. Most "WhatsApp integration" in CRMs is a logging layer: forward the chat to a ticket, tag the number, call it connected. That's not what Indian sales teams actually need. They need to reply from the CRM, hit SLAs on WhatsApp's timers, surface a conversation history a second rep can actually read, and keep the number compliant. Here's the difference between a WhatsApp integration that disappears into the workflow and one that becomes a separate job. 1. Conversation history the next rep can use When a prospect moves from hunter to farm, or an inside-sale hands off to field, the new owner opens the deal and should see the conversation, not a "32 messages — open in WhatsApp" button. What good looks like: The CRM stores the message thread (text, images, documents, voice transcripts) and attaches it to the contact and the deal. The timeline is searchable. A rep can find "when did they ask about EMIs?" without re-reading 200 messages. Internal notes stay internal. Tagging a message for a manager or legal shouldn't send anything to the prospect. What most CRMs ship: a link that launches WhatsApp Web, where the new rep can only see chats the logged-in device already downloaded. Switch users, switch phones, and the history vanishes. For a team of 20–50 reps, that inconsistency destroys reporting. 2. Reply from inside the CRM A panel but sending a message requires the native WhatsApp Web tab, or replies are logged as "manual notes" instead of true sent messages. For a team of 20-50 reps, that inconsistency destroys reporting. An integration worth keeping: Lets reps reply from the CRM's own message composer. Shows WhatsApp's read/delivered ticks and the 24-hour customer-service window inside the CRM UI. Queues replies when a number is temporarily restricted, rather than dropping them silently. This is where the architecture choice matters. Webhook-based logging (inbound only) is cheap and stable. Outbound reply usually needs Meta's Cloud API or a BAAS/BSP layer sitting between the CRM and WhatsApp Business. The cost difference is real, and the compliance difference is larger — more on that below. 3. The 24-hour rule and session templates Meta's policy is the same everywhere, but Indian sales makes it sharper: the working day is long, prospects reply at 11pm, and a rep in the morning suddenly finds the thread closed. A CRM built for India handles this without training reps on Meta's documentation: It shows the session timer as a count-down in the conversation header, not as a cryptic "customer_service_notice" error at send time. It suggests re-opening messages that sound like templates — "Hi, I need the invoice for the site visit on 14th" — and lets the rep send a pre-approved template to reopen the session. It warns before sending a marketing-style follow-up on a closed thread, and surfaces the right "user-initiated" templates to choose instead. If the CRM treats WhatsApp like email — every message is a send — tickets will pile up as bounced notices and reps will learn to route important messages to personal WhatsApp, which defeats the purpose. 4. One number, many reps, no chaos Indian teams share phone numbers. A branch number, a city number, a vertical number. The CRM has to answer three questions that most generic WhatsApp plugins ignore: Assignment — when "Delhi_Sales_919XXXXXXXXX" replies to a message, does the CRM route it to the rep currently owning the deal, to a round-robin queue, or to a skills-based rule? Good integrations let you set routing inside the CRM, not only inside WhatsApp. Presence — the CRM shows which reps are actively on WhatsApp and which are on leave, so an assignment doesn't land on someone who won't reply within the session window. Audit — every outbound message is logged with sender, template ID (if any), and timestamp. For a regulated vertical — finance, insurance, healthcare — that audit is the point, not a nice-to-have. 5. Compliance that doesn't live in a PDF Compliance for WhatsApp in an Indian sales context is three things at once: Meta policy — verified business profile, approved templates, opt-in/opt-out respected, no scraping. India's DPDP Act 2023 — purpose limitation, consent, the right to erasure. A "delete contact" in the CRM should retire WhatsApp templates keyed to that number, not keep them quietly in Meta's system. Industry norms — RBI guidelines for BFSI comms, SEBI for broking, clinical-trial and patient-privacy expectations in healthcare. A generic "unsubscribe" link isn't enough when the message itself was a personal chat. A WhatsApp integration that fits Indian sales teams exposes opt-in and consent as first-class CRM fields, ties them to the contact's WhatsApp number, and lets a compliance officer run a single query: "show every WhatsApp message we sent to opted-out numbers in the last 90 days." What to ask any CRM vendor before you sign Does "WhatsApp integration" mean logging only, or can reps reply from the CRM? Who pays for the BSP/Meta Cloud API layer, and who is liable if a template gets rejected? How does the CRM show the 24-hour session window, and can it send re-opening templates without leaving the deal view? What happens to WhatsApp history when a contact is deleted or a number is reassigned to another branch? Can one WhatsApp number be shared across N reps with CRM-side routing, not only WhatsApp-side assignment rules? Are internal notes, @mentions and draft replies strictly internal — never forwarded to Meta? Is there an export that ties every outbound WhatsApp message to a CRM contact, a consent field, and a sent timestamp? If you can answer "yes" to most of these from documentation and a live demo (not a slide deck), the integration will save reps time. If the answers are vague, it will become a second tool they sync with manually — and in India, sales teams don't double-handle WhatsApp. They go around it. Bottom line WhatsApp integration in a CRM is not a connector; it's a workflow decision. For Indian sales teams, the non-negotiables are: conversation history a second rep can actually read, replying from inside the CRM, session timers and template handling that respect Meta's 24-hour rule, safe multi-rep access to shared numbers, and consent/audit that satisfies DPDP and sector norms. Build or buy against that list, and the WhatsApp piece stops being a feature and starts being the place deals actually happen.
