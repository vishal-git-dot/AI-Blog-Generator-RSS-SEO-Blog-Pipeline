# What to Check Before You Launch Your New Website

## Overview

A new website only starts working for you after it is live. Before that moment, it is worth slowing down and checking a few basics. A site can look finished and still fail if it is slow, hard to use on a phone, or unclear about what you do and how someone can reach you. This article is a rewritten take on the pre-launch checklist from sio-IT, with a focus on what to walk through before you go live, not on decoration or animation. You can read the original Dutch article on sio-it.nl . Make sure the site is fast, also on a phone Visitors leave if a page takes too long to open. That is even more noticeable on a phone. Small images, few unnecessary scripts, and reliable hosting make the difference. Test the site the way your customers will see it. Do not only open it on a fast connection in the office. If a page feels heavy, start with the photos and with parts you do not really need. After a late change, such as a new header or a new project image, check the load time again so one last addition does not make the launch feel slow. It has to work on every phone Most visitors arrive on a phone. Buttons should be large enough to tap, text should be readable without zooming, and forms should be easy to fill in. Open the site on your own phone and try the real path: open the menu, tap a phone number, and send the form. A layout that looks fine on a large screen can still break on a small one. If that happens, the site is not ready to go live. Ask your web designer to walk through this with you before the site becomes public. Be clear immediately, then offer one next step What do you do, for whom, and where? That should be at the top of the page, in plain language. A vague slogan that could belong to any company does not help. A visitor who understands you quickly is more likely to stay. A visitor who has to hunt for the point is already leaving. Then choose one clear next step: a call, a WhatsApp message, or a request for a quote. Put that button where it is easy to find, in the header, on the service pages, and at the bottom. Several calls to action at once, such as call us, download, and sign up, make the choice harder, not easier. Be easy to find locally, with consistent details Name your town and service area, keep your name, phone number, and other contact details the same everywhere, and connect your Google Business Profile. That helps nearby customers find you. Differences between your website, your profile, and your social media make it harder for people and for search engines to recognize you as the same business. Put the service area in the page text, in ordinary words, not only in the footer. If someone is looking for a business in a certain place or region, they should see that in the copy. Use photos of your own work, and agree who will maintain the site Photos of your own work and your team say more than stock images. They show how you work, in your own setting. Plan those photos before launch, so you are not left with temporary placeholders. A valid SSL certificate, updates, and backups are not extras. They are the base. Agree who will take care of that after the site is live. A polished launch without an agreement about maintenance becomes a risk later on.
