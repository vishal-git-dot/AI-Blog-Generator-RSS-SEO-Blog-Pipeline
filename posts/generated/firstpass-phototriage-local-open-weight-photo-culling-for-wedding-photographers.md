# FirstPass (phototriage): Local Open-Weight Photo Culling for Wedding Photographers

## Overview

This is a submission for the Hacktoberfest Open-Source AI Challenge Week 2: Side Hustle What I Built FirstPass (phototriage) is a privacy-first, local CLI and visual review tool built for solo wedding and event photographers. The Problem 3,000 frames come off the memory card after a 10-hour wedding. The photographer only delivers 400. Culling those photos is 4 to 8 hours of tedious, unpaid labour: eyeballing near-identical burst shots to find the single frame where nobody blinked, discarding motion blur, and grouping reception moments. It is the single most hated task in event photography, and it happens right after the adrenaline of the shoot on a tight delivery deadline. Who Would Pay For It? Solo wedding and portrait photographers currently pay $20 to $40 every month for cloud-based culling tools like Aftershoot, Narrative Select, or Imagen. FirstPass targets this exact market with a clear, honest promise: Not "AI picks your best artistic photos" (a claim photographers mistrust), but "here are the technical rejects (blurs, soft frames, redundant burst tails) isolated with mathematical precision, your keepers filed by category, and zero client photos ever leaving your drive." Demo FirstPass separates triage into two deliberate phases: Scan (which writes an audit plan and touches zero originals) and Apply (which safely isolates rejects into quarantine). How I Built It FirstPass was built with a strict hierarchy: cheap mathematical gates first, local open-weight neural networks second, and absolute reversibility at the file level. Bokeh-Preserving Focus Metric Standard blur detectors compute global image variance. For wedding photography, that fails completely: a portrait shot at $f/1.4$ has a razor-sharp eye and a creamy, defocused background. Global averaging calls it "soft" and deletes the best photo of the day. FirstPass divides the image into a $32 \times 32\text{px}$ tile grid, computes Laplacian variance per tile, and averages only the sharpest 2% of tiles . A small off-centre subject (like a bride's face covering 3% of the frame) is preserved, while true motion blur is caught instantly. 2. Dual-Signal Burst Clustering Bursts taken within a 3.0-second window are grouped, and only the sharpest frame is nominated as the keeper. To prevent fine textures (like confetti, gravel, or lace) from falsely collapsing into identical hashes, FirstPass requires both : A 64-bit difference hash ( dHash ), AND A mean-centered, scale-normalized $16 \times 16$ grayscale signature Pearson correlation ($\ge 0.92$). 3. Open-Weight CLIP ( openai/clip-vit-base-patch32 ) Powered by open weights licensed under the permissive MIT License . Downloaded once (~600 MB) to ~/.cache/huggingface/ and runs 100% offline . Evaluates photos in bounded memory batches against natural language prompts ( "a wedding ceremony" , "decorations, table settings and details" , "two people posing together" ). Critical rule: The model is used to label and organize , never to delete. Deletion decisions are strictly mathematical and explainable. Why Does Open Innovation Matter? Open-source and open-weight models aren't just an implementation choice for this project — they are the entire commercial thesis: Client Contracts Forbid Closed APIs: Wedding couples sign legally binding contracts. An increasing number of modern contracts explicitly state that raw client photos will not be fed to third-party commercial cloud AI services. Proprietary cloud tools (Aftershoot, Narrative) cannot clear these contract terms. Running an open-weight model locally is the only way a photographer can say: "I can put this in my contract: your photos never leave my machine." $0 Marginal Cost Across 90,000 Frames/Year: $3,000\text{ frames} \times 30\text{ weddings} = 90,000\text{ images/year}$. On per-image cloud APIs, token and inference costs eat directly into freelance margins. Locally on the photographer's existing MacBook or laptop, the marginal cost per image is effectively zero. Works in the Field with Zero Wi-Fi: Photographers cull on flights, in mountain cabins, or in hotel rooms with terrible Wi-Fi before traveling home. FirstPass requires zero internet connection. Swappable Architecture: Because CLIP is open, a photographer shooting motorsport or indoor receptions can swap the checkpoint ( --model <HF_ID> ) or supply custom label files without asking vendor permission.
