# How to Measure Custom Boxes Correctly for Your Products: The Structural, CAD, and Freight Blueprint

## Overview

To measure custom boxes correctly, always record internal dimensions in the strict industry order of Length × Width × Depth ($\text{L} \times \text{W} \times \text{D}$) with the box opening facing directly upward. Measuring from the inside ensures your product fits like a glove, while accounting for board flute thickness prevents costly dimensional weight freight surcharges. Core Baseline Headings The Golden Rule of Box Dimensions: Industry Sequence, Orientation, and Internal vs. External Metrics Before picking up a ruler or opening CAD software, you must lock down the orientation of the box. Misinterpreting which side represents Length versus Depth will produce a dieline that is completely flipped, resulting in a box that cannot be assembled or packed. THE MANDATORY INDUSTRY SEQUENCE TOP OPENING FACING UPWARD +-----------------------------------+ / /| / LENGTH (L) / | +-----------------------------------+ | | | | | | | WIDTH (W) DEPTH (D) | | / | |/ +-----------------------------------+ Length (L) = Longest side of the top opening. Width (W) = Shorter side of the top opening (perpendicular to Length). Depth (D) = Vertical distance from bottom inside floor to top opening score line. The Mandatory Industry Sequence ($\text{Length} \times \text{Width} \times \text{Depth}$) Packaging engineers, die-makers, and automated folding machines operate on a single, non-negotiable metric sequence: Length ($\text{L}$): The longest side of the box opening when looking directly down into the open container. Width ($\text{W}$): The shorter side of the box opening, positioned at a $90^\circ$ angle (perpendicular) to the Length. Depth / Height ($\text{D}$ or $\text{H}$): The vertical dimension measured from the inside bottom floor of the box up to the top opening score line. Orienting the Opening Correctly The opening axis dictates the entire measurement sequence. If you are designing a Roll End Tuck Top (RETT) mailer, the "top" is the large clamshell lid. If you are measuring a tall Reverse Tuck End (RTE) folding carton for a lotion bottle, the "opening" is still the top flap. Confusing Depth with Length reverses the structural blank on the printing press, ruining the layout and rendering the batch unusable. Internal Dimensions (ID) vs. External Dimensions (ED) Internal Dimensions (ID): The usable space inside the box. All custom packaging manufacturers require ID specs because structural CAD software calculates fold allowances from the inside out to ensure your product fits snugly. External Dimensions (ED): The total physical space the box occupies on the outside. Carriers, freight forwarders, and 3PL warehouses use ED specs to calculate dimensional weight billing and pallet racking density. Substrate Chemistry & Board Flute Allowances: How Material Thickness Alters Box Volume A box is not a paper-thin 2D square—it is a 3D structure made from material with real physical thickness. When corrugated board is creased and folded $90^\circ$ or $180^\circ$, the material takes up space. CAD software accounts for this through score allowances. Packaging CAD systems calculate fold allowances from the center-to-center of those score lines. If a designer ignores score allowances, the inner linerboard will compress and buckle during folding, causing corner seam tears and pushing the box's outer dimensions beyond allowable tolerances. Paperboard Caliper Measurements for Folding Cartons For primary retail boxes made from Solid Bleached Sulfate (SBS) or Coated Unbleached Kraft (CUK) paperboard, material thickness is measured in caliper points ( $1\text{ pt} = 0.001\text{ in}$ ). While an $18\text{pt}$ or $24\text{pt}$ paperboard adds less outer bulk than corrugated board, precise score allowances remain essential to ensure tuck flaps close smoothly without popping open on store shelves. 3. Comparison Section: Measuring Methods, Accuracy Levels, & Logistics Impact Matrix Logistics carriers calculate shipping costs based on Dimensional Weight (DIM Weight)—a volumetric pricing formula that charges for the physical space a package occupies inside a delivery truck: $$\text{Dimensional Weight (lbs)} = \frac{\text{Length (in)} \times \text{Width (in)} \times \text{Height (in)}}{139}$$ Failing to account for board thickness or leaving unmeasured gaps can inflate your box's outer footprint. An uncalibrated $0.5\text{-inch}$ error across all dimensions can easily push a parcel into a higher dimensional weight tier, driving up shipping costs across thousands of orders. Add appropriate clearance allowances to your product measurements to establish your target Internal Dimensions (ID) : Standard Snug Fitment: Add $+1/16\text{ in}$ to $+1/8\text{ in}$ ($1.5\text{ mm}$ to $3\text{ mm}$) to Length, Width, and Depth. Wrapped Products (Tissue Paper / Bubble Wrap): Add $+1/4\text{ in}$ ($6\text{ mm}$) across all axes. Custom Die-Cut Inserts: If using foam, molded pulp, or corrugated partitions, include a $0.5\text{-inch}$ perimeter buffer zone between the insert frame and the outer box walls. Step 4: Factor in Board Flute Profiles to Calculate External Dimensions & Freight DIM Convert your target Internal Dimensions (ID) into External Dimensions (ED) by adding board flute thickness values: $$\text{External Length} = \text{Internal Length} + \text{Flute Allowance}$$ $$\text{External Width} = \text{Internal Width} + \text{Flute Allowance}$$ $$\text{External Depth} = \text{Internal Depth} + \text{Flute Allowance}$$ For example, if your internal target for a D2C mailer is $8 \times 5 \times 3\text{ inches}$ using E-flute board ($+1/8\text{ in}$ allowance), your external dimensions will be $8.125 \times 5.125 \times 3.125\text{ inches}$ . Use these external metrics in the carrier DIM formula ($\text{ED L} \times \text{ED W} \times \text{ED D} / 139$) to confirm your billable shipping weight. Step 5: Order an Unprinted Structural CAD Sample Prototype Never approve mass production based solely on digital files. Request a single unprinted white structural prototype (unprinted sample) from your packaging manufacturer. Complete a final pre-production check: Physical Fitment Test: Place the product inside to confirm it slides in smoothly and stays secure without bulging the box walls. Closure Mechanics Check: Verify that tuck flaps and lock tabs close securely with proper friction tension. ISTA 1A Drop Test: Pack the sample and perform a 3-foot drop test onto corners, edges, and flat surfaces to confirm the structure protects the product under impact.
