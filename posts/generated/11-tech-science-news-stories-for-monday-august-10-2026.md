Space.com Could the northern lights crash next week’s total solar eclipse? A total solar eclipse sweeps across Greenland, Iceland, northern Russia, and slivers of Spain and Portugal on August 12, 2026, blocking the sun for up to two minutes and 18 seconds along the path of totality. Space.com explores whether the northern lights could join the show — it’s possible if a solar storm hits in the days beforehand, but the eclipse’s corona would likely outshine any faint aurora right around the sun, even if auroral activity showed up elsewhere in the sky. Space.com Historians uncover three ‘erased’ Assyrian kings Historians Alexander Johannes Edmonds and Eckart Frahm identified three previously unknown Neo-Assyrian kings by re-examining poorly preserved cuneiform tablets, stelae, and royal grants that had been misattributed to later rulers with the same names. The kings — including Aššur-uballiṭ, whose monuments were later destroyed by a usurper, and Shalmaneser, whose name was overwritten by his successor — appear to have ruled during periods of unrest that were later scrubbed from Assyria’s official king list, pointing to a bloodier political history than the records show. ScienceAlert A common sugar may help cancer cells spread, early study finds Researchers at the Wistar Institute found that senescent cancer cells — ones deactivated by chemotherapy — release fructose that makes nearby active cancer cells less sticky and more likely to break off and spread, the first step toward metastasis. In mice, exposure to those fructose-rich secretions, and a high-fructose diet generally, led to more metastatic tumors than glucose did; the researchers stress this is early, preclinical work on chemo-treated ovarian cancer cells, not proof that dietary sugar alone drives cancer spread in people. ScienceAlert Western Europe just had its hottest June–July on record The EU’s Copernicus Climate Change Service confirmed Western Europe just had its hottest June–July on record, with the region’s combined average temperature hitting 21.62°C (71°F), beating the previous record set in 2022. France, Spain, Austria, Hungary, the UK, Ireland, and Iceland were hit hardest, with major rivers like the Seine, Rhine, and Danube running unusually low and wildfire activity intensifying across the region.
