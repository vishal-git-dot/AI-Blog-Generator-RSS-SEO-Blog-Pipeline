# One availability read per house: how my open-source coworking template books a whole day

## Overview

A coworking member's day is rarely one booking. It's a desk from nine, a meeting room for the 11 o'clock, and somewhere quiet for a call at two. Most booking widgets sell those one at a time: three flows, three checkouts, three confirmation emails. Gable is the open-source Next.js template I built to sell the day instead. It's a coworking site for a made-up network of six buildings in Dublin, Cork and Galway, with 77 bookable spaces, and the whole design is drawn like an architect's drawing set. This post is about the parts I'd copy into any multi-resource booking site: how it reads availability, why the browser does the maths, and how it suggests what your day is missing without calling an API. Live demo · GitHub (MIT) · the longer write-up The cart is a day sheet The planner at /book is a search, not a wizard. You pick a house, a day, how long, a start time and how many people, and you get the spaces that fit, each with a day bar where booked time is hatched like a cut wall in a drawing. Whatever you add lands on the day sheet on the right, which is the cart drawn as a timeline from 08:00 to 20:00. Each booking is held for 15 minutes while you plan the rest, so the room you picked first is still yours when you go back for a desk. Checkout happens once, for everything. The planner state lives in the URL ( /book?house=dock-house&kind=room&date=…&start=10:00&length=60&people=6 ), so a link from the home page or a space page lands on the exact search. One availability read per house, not one per space per day Every space in Gable is its own resource: a room, a booth, a studio desk. The booking API answers availability per space. The naive version of this page asks for every space, for the selected day, every time you change the day, and with 77 spaces across six houses and a 21-day window, that gets expensive fast. Gable flips it. The server asks each space once for its merged free ranges across the whole booking horizon (no date parameter), caches each space for 60 seconds, and sends the browser one response per house. The reads run three at a time , with exponential backoff and jitter on 429 and 503. A space that still fails isn't cached; it goes into a failed list in the response, and the client asks again for just that space a moment later. The cache tags are what keep it honest. A booking drops the space:<id> tag for that space; a cancel, a move or a cart removal drops the house. So the 60-second cache never shows a slot as free after someone took it through this site. The browser does the maths Each free range is packed into a tuple, [start, length, taken, capacity] , in UTC epoch minutes. For a meeting room taken is 0 and capacity is 1. Changing the day, the length, the start or the number of people never calls the API. Two small things worth knowing if you do this yourself. The API can split one free range at a one-second seam, so mergePacked joins spans that touch. And because the merged range reports the peak seats taken, "seats left" is conservative: it may say 22 when 23 are free for part of the span, never the other way round. The account opens on a Week plan rather than a list, because coworking members book in patterns: Monday and Thursday at a desk, the same room every Tuesday. Desks are hatched, rooms are solid, and each upcoming booking has Change time, Stay longer, Invite and Cancel. Where it stops So you don't find out after you've forked it: No monthly memberships or credit allowances. Gable is pay-as-you-go. If your coworking space runs on "10 room hours included" plans, you need a membership system alongside it. Memberships are in beta on the Opencals side. No doors, Wi-Fi or printers. It collects a meeting name "for the door screen" at checkout, and that's as far as it goes into the building. No webhooks yet. Booking events reach other tools through Zapier. The engine isn't open source. The template, the SDK and the agent skills are MIT. Availability, holds, payments and accounts run on Opencals, which is a hosted service and the thing I build. The free development store has every feature in test mode, but it can't take real payments. Running your own copy Sign up at app.opencals.com , create a Dev Store in Ireland (the store's country sets the currency) and choose the Gable (Coworking) dataset. It seeds the six houses, 77 spaces, 16 extras, three discounts and the checkout questions. Create a private storefront key ( spk_ ) in the dashboard. Deploy with the Vercel button in the README, setting OPENCALS_API_KEY and AUTH_SECRET . To check the screens after booking without making a booking, run it locally and open /preview , which renders them from fixtures built from your catalogue.
