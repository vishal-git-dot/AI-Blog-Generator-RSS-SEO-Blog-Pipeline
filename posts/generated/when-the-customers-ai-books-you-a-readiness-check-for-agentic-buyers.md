# When the Customer's AI Books You: A Readiness Check for Agentic Buyers

## Overview

Last month an AI assistant could answer questions about your business. This month it can act for your customer. That difference is the whole story. When OpenAI shipped Sign in with ChatGPT alongside DevDay's quieter announcements — the Meeting Plugin, Spaces, Pages — the coverage was all developer plumbing: OAuth flows, scopes, integrations. That's the wrong lens. The news isn't a login button. It's that your customers' assistants are on a path to showing up as participants in your sales process : filling your forms, booking your calendar, asking your pre-sale questions, comparing you against the shop down the road — with no human eyeballs on your landing page at all. If you run a small business, you don't need to bet on a 12-month timeline. You need to know what breaks first when the person "browsing" your site is a machine. Here's that audit. First, understand the channel shift For twenty years, every site improvement assumed a human on the other end: emotional headlines, brand storytelling, "call us for a quote." Agentic buyers change the reader, not the page. A booking agent doesn't need to be persuaded; it needs to be answered . It doesn't convert — it verifies. Price, availability, scope, terms. If it can't confirm those, you don't lose the sale on features. You're simply never on its list. The uncomfortable version: the agent skips your best sales assets because it wasn't built to be moved by them. The audit: five points where an agent breaks Work through your own sales path and check each point. This takes an hour, not a quarter. 1. Can an agent reach your booking link? If your only intake is a "Request a quote" form behind a CAPTCHA, an agent is locked out at the door — it can't solve the puzzle, and it won't phone you either. Fix: Have at least one intake path with no human-verification gate: a public booking link (Calendly, Acuity, any calendar tool) or a monitored intake email address. 2. Does your booking system reject non-human inputs? Agents fill forms differently: pasted context, fast timing, odd field formats. Watch for silent failures — required phone-verification steps, dropdowns that don't match how an agent parses "home inspection, 2,000 sq ft." Fix: Submit one test entry with clipboard-style, over-formatted input. If it fails or hangs, loosen the validation before a customer's agent hits it. 3. Is your pricing an answer — or a vibe? "Affordable packages tailored to your needs" means nothing to a comparison agent. It needs extractable numbers: starting price, what's included, what costs extra. Fix: Put a starting price and inclusions on your pricing page in a consistent format. Custom work still gets "from $X." 4. Does your FAQ survive a literal reader? Your FAQ is written to nudge a skimming human. An agent reads it literally. "We usually reply fast" fails its check for a stated response time; "most projects take a few weeks" fails a request for a number. Fix: Add the numbers an agent needs to quote: response-time window, typical duration, service area, refund terms. One or two factual FAQ entries per week. 5. Can the agent ask a question when it gets stuck? A well-built agent hits ambiguity and asks — if it has a channel. A widget-only site gives it nothing. Fix: Publish a monitored intake email address and link it visibly. Email is the one channel every agent can already use. What to do — and not do Do the audit this week, ship fixes over a month. The fixes are small: a booking link, looser validation, numbers on the pricing page, factual FAQ entries, a real email address. Don't rebuild your site. None of these fixes touch your brand or design. They're additions a human never notices and an agent can't miss. Don't build an API. A scheduling link plus an email address covers nearly all of it. Don't optimize for agents at the expense of humans. Human buyers still sign the checks. Make the site legible to both. Where this goes Every channel shift rewards the businesses that moved early — being findable on search in 2005, mobile-friendly in 2012. Agentic buyers look likely to follow the same pattern. When a customer's assistant can book a service, it will book the one that confirmed price, availability, and terms fastest. The audit above is how you become that business — before your competitor does. One caveat: don't take the timeline on faith. If your customer base still emails you from a phone, this is a background task, not a fire drill. But background tasks only get done if someone puts them on the list. Put it on yours.
