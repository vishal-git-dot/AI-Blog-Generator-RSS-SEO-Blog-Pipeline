# Stop Guessing Which Ad Produced the Lead: Capture gclid in Next.js and Send the Conversion Server-Side

## Overview

A marketing team asks you a simple question: which campaign produced the twelve leads that came in last week? You open the database. Every lead has a name, an email, a message, and a timestamp. Nothing about where the person came from. The ad platform says it drove forty clicks. Analytics says eight form submissions. Nobody can connect a single row in your leads table to a single ad. This is fixable in an afternoon, and the fix lives in your codebase, not in a marketing tool. The plan: persist the click identifiers and UTM parameters the moment a visitor lands, attach them to the lead when the form posts, and send the conversion event from the server so it survives ad blockers and abandoned tabs. Why client-side tracking alone misses leads The usual setup fires a conversion from a gtag call on the thank you page. It works until it does not. Content blockers strip the tag. The visitor closes the tab before the redirect finishes. The form posts to an API route and the page never changes. Each of those cases is a real lead the ad platform never hears about, which means the bidding algorithm learns from an incomplete picture. Server-side events solve the delivery problem. The attribution problem is separate: you still need to know which click the lead belongs to. Google Ads appends a gclid parameter to the landing URL on every click, and Meta, Microsoft and others do the same with their own identifiers. Capture it on first touch, keep it for the length of the buying cycle, and write it to the lead. The same approach covers referrals from AI assistants, which matters now that GA4 separates AI traffic into its own channel : a lead that arrived from a chat answer should say so in your database, not only in a report. Last touch wins for the click identifiers, because that is how the ad platforms attribute by default and you want your numbers to match theirs. Then do the two things that make the event useful to the people buying the ads. In GA4, mark generate_lead as a key event. In Google Ads, with the GA4 property linked, import that key event as a conversion. From that point the ad platform optimizes against leads that actually reached your database, not against page loads. What this gives the business Every lead row now answers the question the marketing team asked at the start, and it answers it from your own database. You can group leads by campaign in SQL, compare cost per lead across channels with numbers you trust, and hand the sales team the context of how each person found you. When we manage paid search for a service business , this attribution plumbing is the first thing we check, because a bidding algorithm fed by half the conversions will happily spend the whole budget on the wrong half. The next step, once the data is clean, is routing: getting the lead to the right person within minutes instead of letting it sit in a table. That is a workflow problem more than a code problem, and lead routing is where most marketing stacks bottleneck . But none of it works until the lead knows where it came from. Start there.
