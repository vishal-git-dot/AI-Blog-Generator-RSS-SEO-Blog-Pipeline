# How to Trim Audio for a Cedar Shore Video — Free, in Your Browser

## Overview

A Cedar Shore video — waves on the sand, a sunset walk, kids playing at the water's edge — sounds best when the audio matches the mood. Raw recordings rarely do: they start with wind, end with chatter, and the music runs long. Here is how to trim it to fit, free, in your browser. Step 1: Open AudioCutter Open AudioCutter in your browser. It is a free online audio cutter, and it works on your computer, tablet, or phone. Step 2: Add your audio file Click to open your file — an MP3, WAV, M4A, or another common format. Your file is processed locally in your browser, so it is never uploaded to a server. Step 3: Cut the wind and the chatter Drag the handles on the waveform to select the good middle part of the recording. Trim the windy lead-in at the start and the stray chatter at the end, then listen once to check the cut points. Step 4: Add a short fade A half-second fade-in at the start and a fade-out at the end make the clip sound finished instead of chopped. On beach audio, a gentle fade also smooths the gap between wave sounds. Step 5: Export and sync with your video Export as MP3 or WAV, then drop the clip into your video editor and line it up with the footage. If the music is still longer than the video, trim a verse or the outro rather than speeding the track up — the pace stays natural that way. A few tips that help: Keep the wave sounds low under the music instead of cutting them out completely — they are part of the place. Keep the best 30 to 60 seconds; a short, clean clip beats a long, rambling one. Save the original recording — you can always re-trim it later for a shorter version. That is all there is to it.
