# How Japanese Chinchiro and Chinese Bo Bing dice games are scored (and simulating them with 3D physics in the browser)

## Overview

Two of the most fun "roll and shout" dice games in East Asia are Chinchiro (チンチロ / チンチロリン) from Japan and Bo Bing (博饼, "cake gambling") from Xiamen, China, traditionally played around the Mid-Autumn Festival. Both are simple to play, but their scoring tables hide some interesting probability. In this post I'll go through the rules, the combination tables, the exact probabilities (computed by enumerating every outcome), and finally how to make the dice actually tumble in the browser with a physics engine. Part 1: Chinchiro (3 dice) Rules Players roll three dice into a bowl (traditionally a rice bowl). Each player gets up to three tries to make a scoring combination. The first scoring roll stands. A die that jumps out of the bowl is usually an immediate loss ( shonben ). In the classic banker game, each player's hand is compared to the banker's, and some hands pay multiples of the bet. Part 2: Bo Bing (6 dice) Rules Players take turns throwing six dice into a large porcelain bowl. Each throw is checked against the prize table; if it matches a tier, the player takes a prize (traditionally mooncakes) from that tier. Fours are special: the 4 face is red , so the number of fours drives most tiers. The game ends when all prizes are claimed. The top prize, Zhuangyuan , can be "stolen" by a later, higher Zhuangyuan roll. For a "real" feel you can simulate rigid bodies and read the result from the final orientation: Physics world – use a library like cannon-es or Rapier (WASM). Create a static floor plus walls (or a bowl built from a few static shapes) and one Box body per die. Throw – give each die a random position above the bowl, a random rotation, a linear velocity toward the center, and a random angular velocity. Step the world at a fixed timestep (e.g. 1/60 s) and copy body transforms to your Three.js meshes each frame. Settle detection – consider the roll finished when every body's linear and angular speed stays below a small threshold for a few frames (or let the engine put bodies to sleep). Read the face – rotate each face's local normal (+X, −X, +Y, −Y, +Z, −Z) by the body's quaternion and pick the one whose world-space normal has the largest dot product with the up vector (0, 1, 0) . Map that face to its pip value. Edge cases – if the best dot product is below ~0.9 the die is "cocked" against a wall; nudge it or re-roll.
