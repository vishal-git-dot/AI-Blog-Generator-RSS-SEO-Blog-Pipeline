What worked Baked light looks expensive and costs nothing at runtime. The room in the browser has no real-time lights to speak of. The lamp glow, soft shadows and dust are all painted in by Cycles ahead of time. Small files. The shipped Draco GLB is 413,332 bytes. The review copy with embedded textures is 785,416 bytes. Each WebP atlas is between about 2 KB (the lamp-off radio) and 88 KB (the notes atlas). Flags that respect my time. --reuse-atlases re-exports without rebaking when only unbaked objects change. --reuse-lit skips lamp-on bakes that already finished, which the script describes as "resume after a crash." Fixes are diffs. One rebake removed a ceiling-light smudge on the left wall, unclipped the pencils and made my portrait fill its frame. Another gave the globe a clean texture, swapped in an overcast sky and added the typewriter desk and the radio. Each one is a commit I can point to. What broke Bakes are slow. One complete run baked the room in 848.7 s and the props in 720.2 s at 2048 px and 128 samples. The lamp-off versions at 1024 px and 64 samples took 71.8 s and 148.5 s. My last full run took about 44 minutes of baking across six atlases. Runs died partway. Runs 2, 3, 4 and 5 never finished. Their logs just stop mid-render, with no error. Run 4 got through a 755-second room bake and stopped while loading the next atlases, with peak memory around 1.4 GB. That's why --reuse-lit exists. Shared machine, shared memory.
