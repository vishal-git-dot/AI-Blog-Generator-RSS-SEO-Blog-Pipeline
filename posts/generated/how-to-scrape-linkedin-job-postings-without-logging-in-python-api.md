# How to Scrape LinkedIn Job Postings Without Logging In (Python + API)

## Overview

If you're building a job board, tracking who is hiring for a role, or collecting salary data, LinkedIn is the obvious source. You don't need an account for it: LinkedIn shows job postings to logged-out visitors, and that public version is what this tutorial scrapes. I build and maintain scrapers, including a LinkedIn jobs scraper on Apify (more on that near the end, with a disclosure). Everything below was tested on 21 November 2026. What LinkedIn shows without an account Two pages matter. The search results. When you scroll a job search while logged out, the page loads more results from this URL: https://www.linkedin.com/jobs-guest/jobs/api/seeMoreJobPostings/search?keywords=python%20developer&location=United%20States&start=0 It returns an HTML fragment with 10 job cards. start moves through the results 10 at a time, and the usual search filters work as query parameters: f_TPR=r86400 for the last 24 hours, f_TPR=r604800 for the last week, f_AL=true for Easy Apply only. Each card has the job ID ( data-entity-urn="urn:li:jobPosting:<id>" ), the title, the company, the location and the exact posting date. The job page. https://www.linkedin.com/jobs/view/<id>/ is the page anyone sees when they open a job link. It has the full description, the criteria list (seniority, employment type, industries), the number of applicants and, for some jobs, a salary box. What you won't get while logged out: the external "apply on company site" link, which LinkedIn only shows to signed-in users; workplace type (remote, hybrid or on-site) as a field: you have to read it from the title or the description; the job function, and who posted the job. For a few dozen jobs from your own machine, this is enough. One gotcha: there is also a guest fragment for a single job, /jobs-guest/jobs/api/jobPosting/<id> , which looks like a cleaner source for the details. When I compared it with the real job page on 20 jobs, it said "Not Applicable" for seniority on 5 of them, where the page showed "Entry level" or "Mid-Senior level". The other fields matched. Use the job page. Where the simple version breaks These are the problems I hit when running this at volume from cloud servers. The numbers come from my own runs. Rate limiting. LinkedIn answers HTTP 429 to part of the requests that come from datacenter IPs: 13 to 15 % of them in my tests, sometimes from the very first request. You need a pause between requests, retries and a fallback (another IP or a proxy). Going faster doesn't help. One request at a time with a short pause gives me about 16 jobs per minute with full details. About 1,000 results per search. LinkedIn stops paginating around there. Split a big search by city or by date posted. The search is fuzzy. Since August 2026, a search for "python developer" also returns related titles. In today's run of 100 jobs, 49 had "python" in the title. Filter the titles yourself. Salaries are often only in the text. 23 of today's 100 jobs had LinkedIn's salary box, and 39 more gave the pay only in the description ("The base pay range for this role is..."). If salary matters to you, you need a parser for free text that also gets the currency and the period. Duplicates and "new since yesterday". The same job shows up in several searches, and for a daily alert you have to remember which jobs you already have. The markup changes. Class names like base-search-card__title have been stable for a while, but when they change, a scraper returns empty fields without failing. Check the counts, not just the exit code. For bigger runs, start the run and read its dataset when it finishes, as the Python example does. Is this legal? The data here is public: job postings that anyone can see without logging in. Still: LinkedIn's User Agreement, which members accept, prohibits scraping, and its robots.txt disallows every crawler except approved search engines. In hiQ v. LinkedIn , US courts said that scraping public pages is likely not "unauthorized access" under the CFAA. hiQ, which had LinkedIn accounts, was later found to have breached the User Agreement. Job descriptions sometimes contain personal data, such as a recruiter's name or email. If you store it, data protection laws like the GDPR apply.
