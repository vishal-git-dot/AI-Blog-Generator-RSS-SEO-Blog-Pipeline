Leave your brand out of the prompt. Otherwise you're testing what an assistant says about you, not whether it thinks to bring you up. Record the answer and the citations in separate columns. Keep the exact prompt, date, language, brand position (or “not mentioned”), cited URLs, and one sentence describing the answer's rationale if it gives one. Mark your own domain separately. Don't treat a missing link as a missing recommendation. Follow up where a buyer would need better evidence. If an answer mentions your brand but sends readers elsewhere, open the cited pages. Is there a concrete question they answer that your site doesn't? For a budget prompt, that might be a clearly dated comparison of what's available on the free plan. If your site already answers the question well, note that too; a missing citation isn't proof that anything needs fixing. Re-run the same prompts before drawing a conclusion. Compare the rows, not just the headline score. Keep the language and wording fixed for a like-for-like comparison, and treat a change in one answer as something to investigate, not as evidence that an edit worked. Start with one buyer question and two columns: Was the brand recommended? and What pages did the answer cite? The answers can point you to different follow-ups.
