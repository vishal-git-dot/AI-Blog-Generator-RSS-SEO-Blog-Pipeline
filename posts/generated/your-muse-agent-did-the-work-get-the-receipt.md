# Your Muse Agent Did the Work. Get the Receipt.

## Overview

It is late, and your Muse agent has just finished a day of work you did not watch. It researched your closest competitors and wrote up what it found. It drafted the launch post twice and kept the better one. It rebuilt the pricing page, answered the inbox, and filed everything where it belongs. A full day of real work, done at machine speed, in another window, while you were living your life. Then it reports back with one word. Done. Under that word, there is nothing. No record you can open. No trail you can walk. Just a summary, written by the agent, about the agent, out of the agent's own memory of itself. If a step failed quietly in the middle of the afternoon, the summary does not know. If a tool came back empty and the agent improvised around the hole, the summary will never mention it. Done is carrying the whole day on its back, and it is carrying it alone. So here is the question everything turns on. When your Muse agent says done, what do you hold? Right now, you hold a claim. There is another option, and it is almost embarrassingly old fashioned. A receipt. The record, captured at the moment it happens A zambo receipt is a record of one execution, minted by the execution layer at the moment the call runs. Not a story the agent tells afterward. Not a log the agent keeps about itself. A record, made when the work happened, by the system that ran it. Every receipt holds the same things: What ran. The tool that executed, by name. What it was given. The recorded inputs, with a digest of those inputs carried on the receipt. What it produced. The actual observed result, saved in full, inside a canonical record whose SHA-256 digest is printed on the receipt. When. A timestamp from the moment of execution. The receipt lives at a public link. Anyone can check it, by recomputing the digest from the saved record and comparing it to the digest on the receipt. Change one character of the saved result after the fact and the check fails. The agent does not need to be online, cooperative, or even still installed for that record to be checked. And the claim stays narrow, on purpose. A zambo receipt proves the saved result was not changed since it was saved. It does not prove the answer was right, and it never pretends to. That narrowness is the reason it can be trusted: it only ever claims what it can show. A real one, minted while this was being written Enough shape.
