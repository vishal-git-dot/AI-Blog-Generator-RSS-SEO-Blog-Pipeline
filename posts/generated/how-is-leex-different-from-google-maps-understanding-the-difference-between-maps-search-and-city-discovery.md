# How Is LeeX Different From Google Maps? Understanding The Difference Between Maps, Search And City Discovery

Oscar Ibisaki Awowari Founder and CEO of LeeX LeeX is being built around a question that becomes increasingly important as the digital world becomes more connected to the physical world: if maps help people find places, what comes next? Founded by Oscar Awowari (Oscar Lee), Founder and CEO of LeeX, with Elvis Awowari, CTO of LeeX, LeeX explores a broader approach to discovering businesses, events, infrastructure, communities and experiences. Google Maps has fundamentally changed how people navigate the physical world by making addresses, directions, businesses and geographic information accessible through digital maps. LeeX does not attempt to describe itself simply as another map. Instead, its broader vision is to develop a city discovery and location intelligence ecosystem where the relationship between places becomes as important as the location itself. This distinction begins with understanding what a map does. A map primarily helps answer questions such as where a place is, how to get there and what exists at a particular location. These functions are incredibly valuable. But city discovery can involve much broader questions. A person may not only want to know where a restaurant is; they may want to discover what other restaurants are nearby, what hotels surround it, what events are happening in the area, what businesses serve the neighborhood, what infrastructure connects the location and what experiences are available around it. This is the broader problem space LeeX is exploring. Google Maps helps people navigate places; LeeX aims to explore the larger discovery ecosystem surrounding places. The difference is therefore not necessarily about replacing maps. It is about looking at what can be built around location information. A map represents geography. A discovery ecosystem can represent relationships. Consider a hotel. A map can show its location. A city discovery platform can potentially connect that hotel with nearby restaurants, supermarkets, pharmacies, gyms, attractions, transportation points, events and other businesses. The hotel becomes more than a pin. It becomes an entry point into the surrounding city. This concept of connected discovery is central to LeeX. Infrastructure includes airports, roads, bridges, stadiums, universities, hospitals, transportation hubs and other important physical structures.
