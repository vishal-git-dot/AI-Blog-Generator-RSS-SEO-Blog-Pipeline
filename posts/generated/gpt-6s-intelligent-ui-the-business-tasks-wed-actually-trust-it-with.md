# GPT-6's Intelligent UI: The Business Tasks We'd Actually Trust It With

## Overview

Originally published on DigiHustle . ChatGPT Stopped Answering Questions and Started Building Tools On October 7, OpenAI rolled out GPT-6 with something it calls Intelligent UI. The short version: ChatGPT can now generate interactive interfaces inside the chat — calculators, bill splitters, comparison charts, adjustable diagrams, checklists — instead of just text. Paid users (Plus, Pro, Business, Enterprise) got it first with a model called GPT-6 Sol. Free and Go users were scheduled to get GPT-6 Luna from October 8. The mechanism is what makes it interesting. OpenAI built a library of native, streamable components plus a compiler that assembles the interface while the model is still generating its answer. Parts of the UI appear before the full response finishes. OpenAI also claims GPT-6 Instant starts responding 44% sooner than GPT-5.6 Instant on questions that need web search. That's OpenAI's own internal testing — nobody outside the company has verified it yet, and you should read it that way. We spent the week feeding it the kind of small jobs that usually mean opening another tab, signing up for a free trial, or bothering a developer. Here's what happened. What We Actually Asked It to Build We gave it five unglamorous business tasks: Split a $340 team dinner bill four ways, with one person skipping alcohol and a 20% tip. Build a savings calculator for a freelancer setting aside 25% of irregular monthly income. Turn a messy pros-and-cons list of two email tools into a side-by-side comparison chart. Make an interactive checklist for onboarding a new client. Explain a loan amortization schedule with adjustable inputs. Tasks 1, 3, and 4 came out genuinely useful. The bill splitter let us drag the tip percentage and toggle the non-drinker, and every number updated live. That is the whole point of the feature — the interface fits the question, instead of you reading an answer and re-typing it into a separate calculator. Task 2 looked great and hid an assumption: it used a flat monthly income even though we'd described an irregular one. Task 5 was the one that made us pause. The schedule was immaculate. Clean sliders, tidy columns. And one of the interest totals was wrong. The Part Nobody's Talking About: Pretty Math Is Still Math You Didn't Check This is the mistake we see most often with new AI features, and Intelligent UI makes it worse. A generated calculator with crisp sliders and a confident layout makes an unsupported assumption look authoritative. OpenAI's own launch notes admit the model's design judgment still needs work — and that an interactive response "can also be incorrect." The bad practice to kill immediately: copying a generated number into a client invoice, a budget, or a loan document without re-running the math yourself. We now treat every Intelligent UI number the way we treat a junior analyst's spreadsheet — useful, fast, and guilty until verified. Cross-check with one independent calculation. When the number involves money, legal exposure, or health, that's non-negotiable. Three Small Subscriptions This Quietly Replaces Here is our strong opinion: if you're paying for a standalone widget tool that does one simple interactive thing — a mortgage calculator embed, a quote estimator, a quiz builder — GPT-6's Intelligent UI probably covers 80% of it for free inside a chat you already pay for. We cancelled a $19/month calculator widget subscription the same week we tested this, because the in-chat version handled every use case our team actually had. Our free AI tools roundup just got quieter too — this feature makes half of those "free tier" entries redundant. The 20% it doesn't cover: anything you need to publish, embed on your site, or hand to a customer. Intelligent UI lives inside your chat. OpenAI hasn't described any way to export one of these interfaces as a standalone app you can share. If your visitors need to use the tool, you still need real software. What It Can't Do Yet (the Honest List) No Pro reasoning mode — that still runs on GPT-6 Astra, which doesn't support Intelligent UI. Not in ChatGPT Work, Codex, or Voice. This release changes the Chat tab only. The older macOS and Windows desktop apps don't support it. OpenAI tells those users to switch to the web. You can reduce the visuals in settings ("Layout and visuals"), but you can't get a pure text-only mode — and you can't force an interactive answer for every prompt either. The model decides. The Free-tier rollout was announced for October 8, but OpenAI's own help page has shown conflicting status since. Check your model picker instead of assuming you have access. That last point deserves a second look. We're not sure yet whether the Free rollout is finished or still staggered by region and account type. If no interface appears for you, it's almost certainly the rollout, not your prompting. Getting the Good Interfaces Instead of Text Walls The model decides whether a question deserves an interface, so prompt shape matters more than usual. What worked for us: Ask for the tool, not the answer. "Build me a bill splitter for..." beat "How do I split a bill?" every time. Name the inputs explicitly — the amounts, the percentages, the variables you want to adjust. One task per message. Compound requests came back as text; focused ones came back as widgets. This is the same selection logic we lay out in our guide to picking the right AI app for your workflow : match the tool to the job instead of forcing one app to do everything. The Verdict: A Free Intern, Not a Developer Intelligent UI is genuinely useful and genuinely unfinished. For quick, disposable, one-person tools — the stuff you'd previously do in a spreadsheet or a free web widget — it's already good enough to replace paid micro-tools. For anything customer-facing, shared with a team, or where a wrong number costs real money, it's a drafting assistant, not a replacement. One honest caveat: we have not independently verified OpenAI's 44%-faster claim, and a week of testing is not a durability test. Interfaces that impress on day one can develop quirks by day thirty. We'll revisit this in a month and update the post — and when rival assistants ship their own generated interfaces, our ChatGPT vs. Gemini vs. Claude comparison is getting a full rewrite. For now: use it for the small stuff, verify every number that matters, and stop paying for widgets a chat window builds for free.
