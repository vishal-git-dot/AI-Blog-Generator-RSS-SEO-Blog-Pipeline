# Slop Coding: The Vibe Archeologist Strikes Again!

## Overview

This is a submission for the Hacktoberfest Open-Source AI Challenge Week 2: Side Hustle What I Built after-flash PWA androidfirst Apparently, we are approaching the point where it is easier to slop together an app than to find the right one in the store. As someone who makes a living writing software, I’m not entirely sure I should be celebrating this. Anyway, I made a drawing app. It is called After Flash . One drawing tool. Three brush sizes. An eraser. Two layers: drawing and background color. No fill tool, no merging vectors. You can draw frame by frame, play the animation, and export individual drawings as SVG. There is also an AI color palette generator. Give it a theme, a mood, or a few words, and it suggests 8 or 64 colors you can use directly in your drawing. That’s roughly it. I wanted to draw, so I kept the distance between opening the app and drawing something reasonably short. Another excavation Years ago, I used to draw in Flash with a Wacom tablet connected to a Windows PC. The hardware setup was considerably more cumbersome, but I was productive. I wasn’t a particularly brilliant illustrator, although I did make a decent living as a graphic designer. On my Android tablet, this little app brings that feeling back. If you have read Vibe Archeologist: From Z80 Assembly to an 1859 Mudbrick House and Beyond , this probably sounds familiar. I keep digging through old experiences and finding things I want to use again. Sometimes it is assembly. Sometimes it is a mudbrick house. This time, it is Flash. Who would pay for it? The plan is to keep the standalone app free, including an app-store version. The paid part would be embedding it into somebody else’s website. A blog platform could let authors sketch an illustration directly inside the editor and insert it into their post. A diagram, a little character, something that is easier to draw than to explain in three paragraphs. That is the integration I would sell, with the setup and support needed to fit it into an existing website. I haven’t sold an integration yet. This is a business idea to test. And yes, I just said making an app might become easier than finding one. Now I’m suggesting somebody might pay me to integrate mine. Well, they might have other things to do. Demo Try After Flash It feels great on my Android tablet, but I also drew a character in the browser on my MacBook using just the trackpad. Then I gave that drawing to an AI image generator and asked for a small-town scene with similar characters. It reproduced the style frighteningly well. Describing the same style using only words would have been considerably harder, I think. That was a separate experiment, not a built-in feature. But it revealed another use for this little app: quickly drawing your own visual reference for AI. Apparently, everything is connected. Code After Flash on GitHub How I Built It I read an article on prog.hu about Google’s AI app-building tools , opened Google AI Studio, and started describing the drawing experience I wanted. The app uses React, TypeScript, and SVG. The palette generator calls Gemini 2.5 Flash. Yes, I used Flash to help bring back Flash. I didn’t plan that joke. A surprising amount of my contribution came from remembering what I liked about drawing years ago: which tools I wanted within reach, and which features I could happily leave out. Apparently, being old enough to remember the workflow is now part of the development stack. Eventually, I hit AI Studio’s daily development limit while trying to improve the 64-color palettes. Fair enough. There was already something I could draw with. Why Does Open Innovation Matter? For an embeddable tool, being able to inspect the code and adapt it to different websites matters. I would also want the AI component to be replaceable, so the drawing tool does not depend on one provider forever. There is still a loose end for this challenge: the current palette generator uses Gemini, which is not an open-weight model. Publishing the app’s source code alone does not fulfil the open-AI requirement. That part still needs to be addressed. Meanwhile, I have a drawing tool I enjoy using again. The AI helped build the app and can suggest the colors. The questionable drawing skills are still entirely mine.
