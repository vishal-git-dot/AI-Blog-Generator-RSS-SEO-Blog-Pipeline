# Spoofing the camera in Google Meet Zoom Teams and more

## Overview

I saw a Telegram post about Snap , a Google Meet extension by @deniza-png. Before the call you photograph the empty room; mid-call you snap your fingers and disappear. I wanted that, plus everything else I was missing on calls: backgrounds, masks, stickers, translated captions, a teleprompter. So I built Telepult.Web , a browser extension that sits on top of the web call and edits the camera before anyone else sees it. It started on Meet, then grew to Zoom Web, Microsoft Teams, Discord, Slack huddles and about thirty other sites. Same idea everywhere: the call page asks for a camera, the extension hands it a painted stream. Lag is barely visible; the mask stops shaking. Vanishing Same scenario as Snap: turn the camera on, leave the frame, press “Capture room”, come back. Then “Vanish” (or a finger snap) fades you out and the empty room stays. “Return” plays the animation backwards. “Capture room” does not take one frame. It averages 14 frames 40 ms apart. Webcams are noisy in low light; a single frame leaves false spots in the mask. The plate is JPEG-compressed into chrome.storage.local , so the next call does not need a new capture if the camera has not moved. No segmenter here: with an empty-room plate, the person is whatever differs from it. Live frame and plate shrink to 480 px wide; each pixel gets the sum of absolute RGB diffs. Sit down in a dark shirt and the camera lifts the whole picture; without the offset the entire room starts looking different from the plate. The shift is estimated in two passes: mean diff over the frame, then again only on pixels already close to the plate (the background). That is what gets subtracted. The raw mask is ragged, so it is cleaned: Blur twice and threshold again: noise goes, edges settle. Keep only the largest blob; the rest is junk. Flood-fill background from the frame edges. Wherever fill did not reach is the person. That closes holes in a shirt the same color as the wall. The Thanos effect assigns each pixel a threshold from its x coordinate plus a bit of randomness. Animation progress goes 0→1; a pixel dies when progress crosses its threshold, so the person crumbles left to right with a ragged edge. Captions with translation You speak one language; captions in the frame show another. Two stages: speech recognition, then translation. The finished line is painted at the bottom of the same canvas. I used Chris Wilson’s Audio Input Effects trick: two delay lines whose delay times sweep like a saw. Shrinking delay plays slightly faster (pitch up); growing delay plays slower (pitch down). Good enough for a joke on a standup. Filler words and swearing counter Same recognition path as captions. The phrase is lowercased, punctuation stripped, then matched against two lists: swears and fillers (“like”, “basically”, “you know”). Longer phrases first so short ones are not double-counted; words of up to four letters only match whole tokens. Without headphones the mic hears other people from the speakers and the counter counted their swears. A word only counts when the mic level shows that I am the one talking. For a three-person retro it is faster than dragging everyone onto a separate board.
