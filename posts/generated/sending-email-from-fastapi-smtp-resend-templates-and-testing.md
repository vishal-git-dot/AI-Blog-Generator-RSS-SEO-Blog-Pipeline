# Sending email from FastAPI: SMTP, Resend, templates and testing

## Overview

Every SaaS sends the same handful of emails: confirm your address, reset your password, your payment failed. Here is a small module that sends them over SMTP or an HTTP API, in HTML and plain text, in the user's language, without blocking the event loop, and without an email outage taking your sign-up form down with it. Suppose your SMTP provider has a bad hour. If sending raises, every sign-up during that hour gets a 500 after the account was already created, and a Stripe webhook that tries to send "your payment failed" fails too and gets retried for days. If sending logs and carries on, the user is signed up and can press "send the link again" later. The broad except Exception is deliberate: at this boundary, any failure to deliver means the same thing. One honest limitation: this sends during the request. With a working provider that adds a fraction of a second to a sign-up, and the 15-second timeout below is the worst case. If your app sends a lot of email, or you want retries, move this behind a job queue. For a handful of transactional messages, a queue is more infrastructure than the problem needs. Templates: words in translation files, one shared layout Writing a separate HTML file for each email means ten copies of the same layout, times the number of languages. Transactional emails all have the same shape anyway: a heading, a few paragraphs, maybe a button, maybe small print. So each email is just its words, stored with the rest of the UI texts: "email.verify.subject": "Confirm your email for {app_name}", "email.verify.heading": "Confirm your email address", "email.verify.body": "First paragraph.\n\nSecond paragraph.", "email.verify.button": "Confirm email", "email.verify.note": "Small print under the button (optional)." HTML that survives email clients Email clients ignore most of what you know about modern CSS: Outlook renders with Word's engine, and several webmail clients strip <style> blocks. So the layout is old-fashioned on purpose: tables for structure, and inline style attributes on every element; one column under 600 pixels wide, which also reads well on a phone; the link printed as text under the button, for clients that block or mangle buttons; a plain-text version of every email, which helps with spam filters and with people who prefer it. The first thing that goes wrong in production is usually mail landing in spam, and no code fixes that: Send from your own domain , through a provider, not directly from your server's IP address. Add SPF and DKIM records to your DNS. Your provider gives you the exact values. Add a DMARC record too; large mailbox providers now expect it from anyone sending at volume. Keep transactional mail separate from marketing , ideally on a different subdomain, so a newsletter complaint doesn't hurt your password resets. Send yourself the real thing after you deploy: sign up with a Gmail and an Outlook address and read the results. Checklist One send_template() that logs failures and returns False instead of raising. The backend chosen by one validated setting, with memory for tests. smtplib in asyncio.to_thread , always with a timeout. raise_for_status() on API backends. Words in translation files; one HTML and one text layout; autoescape on HTML only. The user's language stored on the account; links built from BASE_URL . Mailpit in development; a test that renders every email in every language. SPF, DKIM and DMARC set up before launch.
