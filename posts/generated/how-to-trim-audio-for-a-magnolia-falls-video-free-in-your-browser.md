# How to Trim Audio for a Magnolia Falls Video — Free, in Your Browser

## Overview

Magnolia Falls is the kind of place that sounds as good as it looks — water over rock, wind in the trees, the occasional bird cutting through. The trouble is that a phone recording from a waterfall walk is full of everything else too: the hike in, the hike out, people talking, and long stretches where nothing much happens. Trimming the audio is what turns that raw recording into something worth putting under your video. You can do the whole job in your browser. AudioCutter is a free online audio cutter that works entirely in your browser — nothing to install, no account, and your audio never leaves your device. Open the site, load your recording, and drag the selection handles to keep the part you want. Export as MP3 and it drops straight into your video editor. Start with the waterfall itself. Listen through once and note where the water sounds cleanest — usually a steady stretch without voices or footsteps. Trim away the approach and the walk back first; most waterfall recordings have a minute or more of trail noise on each end. Then cut the long quiet gaps in the middle. As a rule of thumb, no silence should run longer than about two seconds unless you are holding a deliberate pause. If a gust of wind or a loud voice ruins a few seconds in the middle of a good stretch, split the clip around the bad part and delete just those seconds. A clean jump cut sounds far better than a blast of wind. The same approach works for footsteps on gravel and for the moment someone asks you a question mid-recording — isolate it, cut it, and the rest of the clip still flows. When your best moments are scattered across a long recording, trim each one into its own short clip and arrange them in your editor in story order: the approach, the falls, the pool below, the walk out. Short clips also make fading easy — a short fade in and fade out at each end keeps every moment from starting or stopping abruptly. A few practical tips: always work on a copy and keep the original recording untouched; export at the quality you recorded at so the trim does not make anything sound worse; and if you are layering music under the waterfall sound, trim the music to the same length first so both tracks end together. That is the full workflow: record, trim the noise and the dead air, keep the best moments, export, and edit. Your Magnolia Falls video will sound as peaceful as the place felt.
