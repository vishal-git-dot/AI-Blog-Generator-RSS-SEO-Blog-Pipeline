# Migrate from VAST 2 or 3 when a supported capability justifies the work.

## Overview

Originally published on VASTLint . This article is part of the Migrate video standards with evidence series. An old VAST version is a reason to review a delivery contract. It is not a business case on its own. The case becomes concrete when a campaign needs a capability that the current route cannot reliably provide, or when repeated custom handling costs more than a supported upgrade. Start by identifying the requirement that is blocked. It might concern creative identification, an interactive experience, verification or a media handoff. Then trace the whole route from the ad server through wrappers to the player or insertion system. Changing the first response cannot upgrade every downstream component. Choose the capability before choosing the version. The IAB Tech Lab VAST page links the published specifications and related addenda. Use the version supported by your destinations and the feature you actually need. VAST 4.x includes capabilities that are useful for modern delivery, but support for a version label is not evidence that every optional capability has been implemented. Write an acceptance sentence in the campaign brief. For example, the same registered creative identifier must survive the ad-server response and appear in the agreed delivery report. That sentence gives engineering a test and operations a decision. A requirement to use the latest version gives neither team a measurable outcome. Ask partners to confirm the specific structure they accept and the behavior they support. Keep sample responses and their approval. Record exceptions by destination rather than forcing all partners into a single untested response format. Check whether the 2024 addendum meets the narrower need. A full version migration is not always necessary. The VAST CTV Addendum 2024 describes extensions for bringing selected capabilities to older VAST routes. An extension is useful only where the receiving systems recognize and process it as intended. Compare two scoped proposals: a supported addendum route for the immediate requirement, and a broader VAST 4.x migration. Include development, partner review, regression testing and future maintenance in both estimates. An addendum can reduce the first change while leaving some legacy complexity in place. Do not insert a native element from a newer schema into an arbitrary older location and assume that an extension reader will find it. Follow the published structure and the receiving partner's documented contract. Save an example that the partner has actually processed successfully. Treat a version change as a content change. Changing the root version attribute is not a conversion procedure. Elements, required metadata and interpretation need to match the target specification. Review the complete document against the published VAST 4.3 specification when that is the agreed target, including the wrappers that lead to the final creative. Keep the same intended advertisement in the before and after records. A clean empty response may be a legitimate no-ad response, but it does not establish that the campaign creative migrated successfully. Compare media, duration, identifiers, click behavior, tracking and verification requirements explicitly. Run the original and candidate through VASTLint and retain both outputs. Review new findings instead of deleting fields until the report looks clean. Some fields may be required by the destination even when a more general document check permits their absence. Test the route that will receive paid traffic. Run the candidate through the actual ad-server and wrapper path, then through the approved player or insertion workflow. Check asset selection, playback, the expected events and the destination report. Record the environment and the time of the test so the result can be reproduced. Include a working legacy creative as a control. Add failure cases for an unavailable asset and an unsupported optional feature. The purpose is to verify the agreed fallback and escalation, not simply demonstrate one successful happy path. Limit the first release to a small, observable allocation. Keep the previous routing configuration and a rollback trigger that the operator can execute. A migration needs someone watching the campaign results, particularly when the new route changes reporting as well as delivery. Budget against the problem the migration can solve. Suppose, purely as a planning example, twelve campaign handoffs each require an hour of manual identifier reconciliation. A proposed change may target that twelve-hour task. It does not follow that all twelve hours disappear, because exceptions, validation and reporting work may remain. Measure a comparable group after the change. Count unresolved identifiers, manual corrections and time spent by role. If the requirement instead concerns acceptance of a new format, retain the partner approval and subsequent delivery record. Do not present planned impression allocation as measured recovered revenue. The same discipline can justify postponing a migration. If the current supported route meets the immediate requirement and the broader upgrade has no committed consumer, invest in the bounded change first. Record the limitation so it is revisited when the product requirement changes. Make the approved route reusable. Store the accepted version, optional capabilities, partner exceptions and sample test records with the integration configuration. Use that record when a second campaign arrives. Include an owner and a review trigger for SDK, ad-server or destination changes. Use VASTLint at the document handoff and retain runtime checks for the destination. The commercial benefit is a capability that the business can repeatedly deliver with known acceptance conditions. A newer version number becomes useful when it supports that result.
