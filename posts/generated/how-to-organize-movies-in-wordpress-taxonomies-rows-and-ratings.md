# How to Organize Movies in WordPress: Taxonomies, Rows, and Ratings

## Overview

A movie collection is not a folder of posters. It is a catalog a viewer can browse by mood, era, and cast, and that only works when every film sits in the right place with complete information. Set the sorting rules before the collection grows. The order that works is simple: a short list of taxonomies, curated collections and rows, linked cast and crew pages, one rating scale, complete movie pages, a clear homepage, and search that actually works. Taxonomies are the labels that sort a collection, and they only work when used the same way. With Movie Engine, the built-in taxonomies cover genre, year, country, language, network, and tags, plus separate types for movies, TV series, seasons, episodes, and people. Before importing anything, decide which genres your collection will actually use, and keep that list short. Spell a genre, country, and studio the same way on every film, so a filter for one label returns every matching film. Most viewers arrive to browse, so give them clear places to start: a featured film, new additions, top-rated films, and a few genre rows that match your niche, kept in the same order on the homepage. Rows work best when curated: new films should really be new, top-rated films really rated, and a genre row should contain only films that belong to that genre. Cast pages are a second way into the catalog: a viewer who finishes a film often wants what else its lead actor made, and a people page answers in one click. Keep cast names spelled the same on every page and link each name to its own page, so a search for one actor does not split across two profiles. Crew credits matter too, especially where a director or writer is the draw. Ratings only help viewers when the scale never changes. Choose one scale, explain what each level means, use it on every film, and show it where viewers look first: near the title, in lists, and in search results. A movie page is the unit of trust. Before a film goes live, its facts should be filled in: overview, genres, year, country, language, cast, and rating, plus a poster and backdrop in place. A film with a poster but no description, cast, or genre cannot be filtered or searched properly, and cannot convince a viewer it is worth starting. Fix wrong years, missing descriptions, and duplicates at import, not after viewers find them. The homepage is the front door: a featured film with a clear watch or details action, followed by the curated rows, shows a first-time visitor the whole collection within seconds. Test it as a new visitor would: can you pick a film and reach playback or an affiliate viewing link without guessing? Search is where a movie site is tested. A viewer who types a genre, an actor, or a year should reach a useful result in one step, so make sure search covers films, people, and genres, with filters that narrow results predictably. A collection is never finished, because titles, licenses, and viewer interests all change. Run the same checklist for every new film and every maintenance pass: taxonomies set, collections built, people linked, ratings consistent, pages complete, homepage clear, duplicates checked, and rights recorded, with an expired license removed or renewed before a viewer meets it as a dead link. Publish only titles you have rights to. Organization and monetization meet at one question: how does a viewer move from browsing to paying, or to watching elsewhere? Banner ads suit a free catalog with steady traffic, affiliate viewing buttons suit a discovery site that does not host video itself, and paid subscriptions and pay-per-view, available in Pro with Stripe and PayPal, suit a site that also offers video access. Keep the rules visible on the film page itself, not only at checkout. An organized movie catalog is a sequence, not a single feature: clear taxonomies, curated collections, linked people pages, consistent ratings, complete film pages, a clear homepage, and working search. Follow that order, keep each step small and checkable, and the collection grows without turning into a pile of posters.
