# Story Short Script Formula: Hook, Escalation, Snap, Callback

## Overview

Originally published at buyagentskills.com . Short answer: a 55-75 second story short is easier to write if you fix the beats first: a hook that states the absurd fact in one line, a setting, three or four escalating beats, a snap where it breaks, one everyday analogy, the consequence, and a callback to end on. No structure can promise reach. It can stop you wandering. Written by the Buy Agent Skills team. Updated 8 November 2026. Why structure matters in 60 seconds At narrator pace you have room for roughly 180-200 words. The Cartoon Films pack uses about 2.6 words a second as its planning rate. There is no space for a slow intro, so each line must either escalate the story, land a joke, or explain one idea. The beats Hook. The absurd fact in one line. Example: "A man just got home from a walk. He left in nineteen ninety-eight." Setting. Year, place, who. One or two sentences. Escalation. Three or four beats, each a little bigger than the last. Snap. The moment it breaks. Deadpan double line. "Luckily... / Unluckily..." A two-line pivot. Explanation. One idea through an everyday analogy. Consequence. Callback twist. Return to something from the hook or setting and end on it. Add one rule on top: never describe in words what the viewer already sees. The hook states the surprising fact; it does not set it up. Escalation that stays flat. If beat three is not bigger than beat two, cut it. Narrating the picture. If the visual shows a horse, the script need not say "horse". Facts you cannot source. Every claim needs two independent sources; leave out anything disputed. See fact-checking AI videos . Analogy that needs a second analogy. One everyday comparison, then move on.
