# I parsed 110,000 EU procurement awards. Half of them don't say when the contract was signed.

## Overview

Every two months or so, the EU publishes an open archive of everything its public authorities bought — Tenders Electronic Daily (TED) , the official journal of the day for EU procurement. In October 2026 I downloaded the complete monthly packages for July, August and September 2026 — 1.16 GB of eForms/UBL XML, 213,716 notices, of which 110,217 are award notices (the documents that say who won what). I wrote four small Python scripts, parsed everything in about ten minutes, and got numbers the official statistics don't show you. The rule: under Directive 2014/24/EU, Article 50(1) , a contracting authority has 30 days to publish the award notice after the award is made. So a simple question: how often does that actually happen? 44% of award notices state no award date at all Of the 110,217 award notices, 48,931 (44.4%) carry no usable award date. Not "late" — the field is filled with a sentinel value (the date 2000-01-01 and friends, the eForms schema's explicit "not stated" marker). What's interesting: in the September sample I checked the raw date values per notice — the sentinel appears in 73.7% of award notices as one of their dates , but only 44.1% of notices lack any usable date. The element is never absent; the authority actively says "not stated". Official TED statistics don't report this, so it's a data-quality finding in its own right: if you want to build a compliance or analytics pipeline on TED award data, nearly half of it won't have the field you want. The three-month trend is boring (which is the good part) For the notices that do state an award date, the picture is stable across all three months — this isn't a September artifact: Month Award notices No usable award date Within 30 days Later than 90 days Jul 2026 42,742 43.2% 45.9% 27.1% Aug 2026 32,317 45.0% 46.3% 22.2% Sep 2026 35,158 44.1% 47.2% 24.0% So: roughly half of dated award notices respect the 30-day window, and roughly a quarter publish later than three months. If you'd bet on "95% comply", you'd lose in every month. The honest twist: the long tail isn't what you'd think The first version of this analysis reported a blunt "24% are 90+ days late". Then I stratified by notice type (eForms notice-type-code , the official codelist) and the story changed: can-standard — standard competitive contract/concession awards, 82% of the population: 66.7% publish within 30 days, only 11.8% later than 90 days. can-modif — contract modification notices: 71.4% later than 90 days. That's expected — a modification gets published long after the original award. veat (voluntary ex-ante transparency) and can-social (light regime, below-threshold) sit in between. The blended headline hides the decomposition. The core competitive market is faster than the average suggests; framework and modification filings drive the long tail. Any honest "EU procurement compliance" number has to say which population it covers. The single-bidder map (and where I got it wrong) A second question: when lots report how many tenders they received ( ReceivedSubmissionsStatistics , code tenders ), how often is the answer exactly one? Stable across the three months: about 32% of reporting lots are single-bid . By buyer country (September, buyer = the contracting authority, cross-checked against the contract's place of performance — both agree within half a point for every country): Top single-bid share Bottom Greece 63.1% Finland 9.0% Poland 53.7% Switzerland 10.2% Slovenia 45.7% France 16.8% Cyprus 44.4% Germany 19.9% Czechia 41.5% Belgium 18.3% Central and Eastern EU buyers award single-bid 2–3× as often as Western ones. I won't pretend that's pure competition — below-threshold and voluntary routes (where one tenderer is normal) and single-supplier frameworks both inflate the number, and I haven't fully stratified that cut yet. But the gradient is robust, and nobody seems to publish it. Here's the cautionary tale for anyone working with TED: my first pass reported Luxembourg at 51.6% single-bid — the highest in the EU. It was wrong. The country field I'd built collected every NUTS code in the document, which includes the addresses of all the suppliers. Re-attributed by buyer, Luxembourg is a normal 17.1% with 82 notices. A country can hide inside a supplier's address. (The corrected country table is in the script output; the artifact is the lesson.) Cross-verification: I pulled one notice (00610783-2026, a Faro health authority) and compared the XML totals against the server-rendered TED portal page — 324,530.22 EUR, exact match. One vintage of three months; re-published/corrected notices count separately. The four scripts (headline stats, notice-type strata, buyer attribution, place-of-performance) rerun on any monthly package in a few minutes each. If you work with EU procurement data, the honest summary is: 44% won't tell you when, ~47% of the rest tell you within the window, and the long tail is mostly framework business — and the single-bid gradient across Europe is real, stable, and apparently nobody else publishes it.
