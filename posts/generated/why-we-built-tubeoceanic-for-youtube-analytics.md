# Why WE Built TubeOceanic for YouTube Analytics

## Overview

We built TubeOceanic because we wanted an easier way to look at YouTube channel numbers without jumping between five different sites or doing the math yourself. YouTube actually shares a lot of public info — subscribers, views, video counts, likes, comments, and more. But just staring at one number doesn't really tell you much on its own. A channel with ten million subscribers that's barely getting views tells a very different story than one with a million subscribers growing fast. You need to see the bigger picture, not just a single stat. That's basically the whole idea behind TubeOceanic. It's an independent tool that pulls together public channel and video data in one place, so you can actually see how a channel is doing — growth over time, video performance, and rough earnings estimates — without digging around for it. One thing we cared about a lot was tracking change over time, not just where a channel stands right now. So instead of only showing today's subscriber count, TubeOceanic keeps a history of the data it collects, so you can actually see how a channel has grown or slowed down recently. We also want to be upfront about the earnings numbers. Nobody outside of YouTube and the creator themselves actually knows a channel's real income — that's private. So any earnings shown on TubeOceanic are estimates, built from public data and reasonable assumptions. They're not a claim about what someone actually makes. Everything on the platform comes from YouTube's public Data API. That means we only have access to the same public stuff anyone can see — we don't have access to a creator's private YouTube Studio numbers, their real revenue, audience demographics, or watch time. None of that is available to us, and we think it's important people know that. At the end of the day, our goal is pretty simple: make public YouTube data easier to explore and actually understand. You can check it out at TubeOceanic , and we're planning to keep building on it — adding more ways to understand channel and video performance, while keeping how it all works as transparent as we can.
