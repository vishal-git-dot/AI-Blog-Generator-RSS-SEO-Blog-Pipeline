# Two Minds, One History: Building a 3D AI Museum for Both Humans and AI Agents

## Overview

Most conversations about artificial intelligence treat the field as if it started with ChatGPT. At the same time, much of the best historical and technical documentation is locked behind paywalls, buried in PDFs, or wrapped in heavy frontends that AI agents struggle to parse cleanly. I built New Mind Museum around a simple premise: Two Minds, One History —an interactive museum designed to be explored by humans in 3D and read cleanly by AI agents. What’s inside for humans Instead of a static timeline, the museum is structured as a free, no-signup 3D walkthrough that runs directly in desktop and mobile browsers across six curated rooms: 1837–1950s: From Charles Babbage’s Analytical Engine and Ada Lovelace to Alan Turing and the Dartmouth workshop. Symbolic AI to Deep Learning: Expert systems, AI winters, neural networks, and the 2017 Transformer breakthrough. Milestones & Live Trackers: Turing and Nobel laureates, global AI legislation (integrated with our sister project AI Gov Tracker , which tracks state and country AI laws cited to the official statutory section), and a live calendar tracking upcoming AI model retirements (such as Claude Sonnet 4.5, GPT-5, and o3 deprecations). Why it’s built for AI agents too As a journalist covering AI, power, and search visibility, I’ve watched how crawlers and LLM agents interact with the modern web: most sites either block bots entirely or bury primary facts inside opaque client-side scripts. Across both New Mind Museum and AI Gov Tracker (which exposes machine-readable endpoints like llms.txt and structured JSON scopes), every exhibit, statutory duty, and model retirement date links directly to its primary source with clean, semantic context. Whether a visitor is a student walking the 3D gallery on their phone or an autonomous agent verifying when a model deprecates or a California AI law takes effect, both get the exact same grounded record. Try it out Walk the 3D museum & check live model retirements: newmindmuseum.com Track AI laws & statutory deadlines: aigovtracker.com I’d love feedback from the #showdev community—both on the 3D web navigation and on how you structure sites ( llms.txt , JSON schemas, semantic HTML) for agent readability.
