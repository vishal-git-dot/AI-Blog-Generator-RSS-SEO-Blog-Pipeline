# Digital signage for a client's shop or office: three questions that decide which software to pick

## Overview

If you build websites, run IT for a small company or look after a family member's café, sooner or later someone asks: "Can we put the menu, the opening hours or the calendar on that TV?" The answer is yes, and the software you choose decides whether it stays a ten-minute job or turns into a weekly chore. We build one of these tools ( Screenbird ), so take our opinion as a view from inside the category. Below is the checklist we would use if we were choosing for a client, with the reasoning, so you can apply it to any product. What digital signage software is, and what it is not Digital signage software decides what plays on your screens. It has two parts: a dashboard where you upload images and videos, build playlists and set schedules, and a player on or next to each screen that shows the content. It is not a TV's built-in USB slideshow, which you cannot update remotely, and it is not casting from a phone, which stops the moment the phone leaves the room. Three questions that decide most small setups 1. Is everything in the plan you pay for? Many platforms use a plan ladder: a cheap entry plan, with the features you actually want (proof of play, business app integrations, user roles, screen sharing) in a higher plan or as a paid add-on. Read the plan table for the cheapest plan and list what you need next to it. A low entry price with the one feature you need in the third plan is not a low price. 2. Can you share a laptop screen to the TV? Meetings, quick announcements and "show this PDF now" all start here, and it is the feature that gets held back most often. As of October 14, 2026, from each vendor's own pages: in Screenbird it is in every plan; at Play Digital Signage it starts at the Pro plan, at Fugo at the Core plan, at Rise Vision at the Enterprise plan, and at OptiSigns it is a paid add-on. The other four platforms we checked do not offer it or do not publish it. 3. Does it run on the screens you already have? Check the player list against the hardware in the room: an Amazon Signage Stick or Fire TV, a Google TV or Android box, a Windows PC, a Mac, a Linux machine or Raspberry Pi, and a plain web browser as the fallback. Samsung and LG smart TVs usually run a browser, not a native player, so ask how that works. A platform that needs special hardware turns a software decision into a purchasing project. Quick answers by situation Situation What to look for One screen, you just want to try it A free plan or a trial; know what is limited (screen count, watermark, supported devices) A shop, restaurant or office with a few screens One plan with everything, and a player for the devices already there A mix of TVs, sticks and old computers A player for all of them, so you do not manage two systems Sharing a laptop in meetings Screen sharing in the entry plan Several people editing the content Unlimited or cheap extra users, and roles A note on hardware For a first screen we would use an Amazon Signage Stick or, next best, a Google TV Streamer. If another tool fits your client better, the checklist above still applies. FAQ What do I need to run digital signage? A screen, a player (a stick, a small computer or the TV's own browser) and a software account. The content is managed in a browser. Do the screens work without internet? Good players cache the content and keep playing when the connection drops. Ask the vendor how long, and how updates behave when the screen comes back online. Can I put a calendar, a menu or a dashboard on the screen? Most platforms support images, video and web content. Business integrations (calendars, spreadsheets, slides) differ a lot, and are the most common thing held back for a higher plan. AI disclosure: an AI assistant helped structure and edit this post.
