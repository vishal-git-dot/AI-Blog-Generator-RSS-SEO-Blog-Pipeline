# Metacognition Leaderboard for LLMs: JGENai VIDRAFT Launch a Benchmark That Measures Self-Error Detection, Not Just Accuracy

## Overview

Metacognition Leaderboard for LLMs: JGENai × VIDRAFT Launch a Benchmark That Measures Self-Error Detection, Not Just Accuracy TL;DR: JGENai and VIDRAFT have jointly released a metacognition benchmark, a 24-model public leaderboard, and 11 model-specific metacognition adapters designed to measure whether an AI can detect its own mistakes — not just whether it gets answers right. The adapters attach to existing models without modifying weights or architecture, making them drop-in reliability probes for high-stakes applications. Developers can explore the leaderboard, benchmark dataset, and adapters on Hugging Face. What it is JGENai (CEO Sunyoung Choi) and VIDRAFT have co-developed and publicly released three artifacts: A metacognition benchmark — 400 adversarial "trap" questions designed so the superficially plausible answer is wrong, testing whether a model can catch its own likely errors rather than just pattern-match to a confident-sounding output. A 24-model public leaderboard — ranking current Korean and multilingual LLMs on both multiple-choice accuracy and open-ended self-error-detection capability. 11 model-specific metacognition adapters — lightweight add-ons that sit beside a target model and assess the risk that any given response is incorrect, without touching the base model's weights. The core thesis: the dangerous moment for an AI is not when it's wrong, but when it doesn't know it's wrong. In domains like medicine, law, finance, security, and scientific research — where error costs are high — self-error detection is framed as a primary reliability signal, not an afterthought. How it works Benchmark design Each of the 400 benchmark problems is constructed so that one answer appears strongly plausible at first read, while the actual correct answer requires the model to pause and re-examine its own reasoning. Evaluation is split across two axes: Multiple-choice accuracy — standard answer correctness on trap questions. Open-ended self-assessment — after generating a free-form answer, can the model correctly judge whether that answer is likely wrong? This dual-axis design exposes a gap that single-accuracy leaderboards miss: a model can score well on axis 1 while being nearly blind on axis 2. Metacognition adapters The adapters are designed around a non-invasive attachment pattern: They do not modify the base model's architecture or trained weights. They analyze internal signals produced during the model's answer-generation process to compute a risk score indicating how likely the response is to be incorrect. The output is a separate risk signal — a flag or score that downstream systems, human reviewers, or orchestration layers can consume independently of the answer itself. This architecture means you can wrap an existing deployed model with a metacognition adapter without retraining, fine-tuning, or touching inference infrastructure beyond adding the adapter layer. Benchmarks & results From the publicly reported leaderboard results: JGOS-31B-Citizen ranked #1 out of 24 models evaluated. It answered 398 out of 400 trap questions correctly, achieving a 99.5% trap-avoidance rate . JGOS-31B-Citizen is also the current top-ranked model on the K-AI Leaderboard operated by Korea's Ministry of Science and ICT (MSIT) and the National Information Society Agency (NIA). Multiple models from the Darwin family (Darwin-28B, Darwin-31B, Darwin-35B) — all developed in Korea — placed in the upper tier of the leaderboard. Critically, models that performed strongly on multiple-choice accuracy still showed limited capability on open-ended self-assessment . This confirms that raw accuracy and metacognitive awareness are empirically distinct competencies — a model can be highly accurate yet unable to flag its own uncertain or incorrect outputs.
