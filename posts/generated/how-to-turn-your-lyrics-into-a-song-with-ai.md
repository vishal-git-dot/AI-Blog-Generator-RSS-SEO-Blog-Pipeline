# How to Turn Your Lyrics into a Song with AI

## Overview

By SoundLuma editorial team To turn lyrics into a song with AI, prepare clean lyric text, separate it into sections, and choose a service that accepts custom lyrics. Then describe the arrangement independently, generate a version, and check the words and melody together. A text-to-music prompt and a custom-lyrics field may behave differently, so check which input mode you are using. If you already have verses, you can organize them in the SoundLuma lyrics workspace . For enabled trial accounts, choose My lyrics → song in the generator, enter your lyrics, title, and music style, then generate. The optional brief workspace farther down the page exports text only. If you have a memory rather than lyrics, start with the story-to-song method . Key takeaways Put only the words you want sung in the lyric field. Keep section labels consistent and arrangement instructions separate. Review pronunciation, missing lines, and section order in every generated version. Prepare a readable lyric sheet Remove notes such as “rewrite this later,” duplicate drafts, and comments intended for a collaborator. Decide which chorus version is final. A model cannot reliably know that the crossed-out line in your working document is not part of the song. Use blank lines between sections. Simple labels such as [Verse 1], [Chorus], and [Bridge] can make your intention clear to a human reader, but support varies by music service. If the service documents another format, follow it. Bracketed text is not a universal command language and may sometimes be sung. Here is an original fictional example: [Verse 1] We counted windows on the train And drew our names against the rain [Chorus] Wherever all these miles may go You're still the place I know The example is a starting pattern, not a claim about how any particular model will perform it. Read each section out loud Look for a line that takes much longer to say than its neighbors. It may still work musically, but it deserves attention before you spend a generation attempt. Replace a crowded sentence with a simpler phrase while keeping its meaning. For example, “I appreciate how you consistently supported me when everything was changing” might become “You stayed when everything changed.” You lose detail, so decide whether another verse should show what that support looked like. The goal is to make space for expression rather than force every thought into one line. Do not change a meaningful word just to obtain a rhyme. If the rhyme makes your message less accurate, leave it open or change both lines. Write a separate arrangement brief Describe instrumentation, vocal character, mood, and energy. A useful brief could be: “Piano-led ballad, intimate female vocal, restrained first verse, a fuller final chorus, clear words, short ending.” This gives direction without promising a specific output. Choose the language of the actual lyrics. If you mix languages, mark the intended transitions in a way your chosen service supports. Do not assume a language dropdown will translate your text while preserving rhyme or meaning. For a birthday track, an energetic arrangement may suit the party, while a quieter arrangement may suit a personal message. Handle names and pronunciation deliberately Names, initials, and unusual place names need a dedicated listening check. A spelling that looks correct may be sung differently from what you expect. If the service allows phonetic guidance, use its documented method. Otherwise, consider a nickname the recipient actually uses. Keep a written lyric sheet with the correct spelling even if you use a pronunciation aid during generation. Listen to the whole line after changing a name; a fix can alter its rhythm. Compare versions with a short checklist Check whether every intended section appears, whether a line is repeated unexpectedly, and whether the key message remains understandable. Listen for truncated endings and words that are stretched beyond recognition. Compare versions using the same checks so you are choosing deliberately rather than following whichever chorus you heard last. If a verse is crowded, edit the verse before asking for more versions. If the performance feels wrong but the words work, change the arrangement description. Change one kind of input at a time when you want to understand what helped. Can you use any lyrics you find online? Use lyrics you wrote or have permission to use. Access to a lyric website does not establish permission to reproduce those words in a new recording.
