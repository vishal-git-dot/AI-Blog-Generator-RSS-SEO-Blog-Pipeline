# How to Get Your Business Cited in Perplexity AI Answers

## Overview

Perplexity is different from a chatbot that answers from memory. For most questions it runs a live web search, reads the pages it finds, writes an answer, and shows numbered citations next to each claim. That changes the game for businesses: getting "mentioned" in Perplexity is really about becoming one of the sources it cites when a buyer asks about what you do. That's closer to classic SEO than to ChatGPT-style training-data visibility — good news, because it means the levers are concrete and largely in your control. Here's how Perplexity selects what it cites, how to check whether you show up, and where to spend your effort first. How Perplexity decides what to cite You don't need the internal details to work with the mechanics that are visible in every answer. In practice, a citation tends to require a few things to line up: The page has to be findable. Perplexity pulls from live web results, so a page that search engines can crawl and index is a prerequisite. If it can't be retrieved, it can't be cited. The page has to directly answer the question asked. Perplexity favors pages that address the specific query in plain, quotable language — not pages where the answer is buried, vague, or wrapped in marketing copy. The claim has to be attributable. Clear, specific, factual statements are easier to cite than fluff. Corroboration helps. When several independent sources agree, the well-aligned ones are more likely to be cited. The throughline: Perplexity rewards content that is retrievable, on-topic, and quotable. That's a different target than "rank #1 on Google," though the two overlap. Step 1: Check whether you're cited today Run the buyer-intent questions a stranger would actually type, in a fresh Perplexity session with no prior context. Use category and need-based phrasing, not your brand name: Best [your service] companies in [your city]? Who should I hire for [the specific thing you sell]? Compare top [your category] providers for [your customer type]. For each answer, look at the numbered citations , not just the prose. Note whether your domain appears in the source list, whether a competitor's does, and which specific page got cited. Step 2: Make sure your pages can be retrieved Citation is impossible if the page can't be fetched. Confirm the basics: the page is indexable (not blocked by robots.txt or a noindex tag), loads without requiring a login or heavy client-side rendering that hides the text, and is reachable from your sitemap. Step 3: Publish pages that answer the exact question The single most effective move is to create or sharpen a page that answers one buyer question cleanly: Put the answer up top in a direct, self-contained sentence or two, before any preamble. Use the buyer's own phrasing in the heading and first paragraph. Make claims specific and checkable rather than generic superlatives. Keep one page per question. Step 4: Add structured data and keep your facts consistent Mark up your pages with Organization , LocalBusiness , and FAQPage schema so machines can parse who you are without guessing. Then keep the core facts identical across your own site and third-party listings. Step 5: Earn corroboration where buyers' questions get answered Perplexity frequently cites the places that already rank for a query — directories, review platforms, industry roundups. You don't control those pages, but you can make sure your listing on them is present, accurate, and complete. Step 6: Re-run the check and watch the citations move Because Perplexity reads the live web, changes can surface in days to weeks rather than the slow drip of training-data updates — but it isn't instant, and results vary between runs. Re-run your buyer-question list every couple of weeks. FAQ How is getting cited in Perplexity different from showing up in ChatGPT? Perplexity answers most questions by searching the live web and citing the pages it reads — much like SEO. ChatGPT often answers from training data. The Perplexity levers are more concrete and faster to influence. Why does Perplexity cite my competitors instead of me? Usually because their pages are easier to retrieve and more directly answer the exact question, with consistent facts and corroboration from directories or reviews. Do I need structured data to be cited? Not strictly required, but it helps machines parse who you are and reduces the chance of being skipped or described incorrectly.
